Cell Wall Inhibitors

Complete, separate classifications with exhaustive breakdown tables, high-yield mnemonics, adverse effects, and clinical nursing pearls.

💊 Penicillins 🛡️ Cephalosporins ⚡ Carbapenems & Monobactams 🧬 Glycopeptides (Vancomycin)

1. Penicillins Classification & Profile

Beta-lactam antibiotics containing a thiazolidine ring attached to a beta-lactam ring. They inhibit peptidoglycan synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs).

Subclass Representative Drugs Spectrum / Target Memory Trick / Mnemonic
Natural Penicillin G (IV/IM), Penicillin V (Oral) Gram-positive cocci, Treponema pallidum (Syphilis). "G" for Great against Gram-positives.
Aminopenicillins Ampicillin, Amoxicillin Broader spectrum; covers Enterococci and basic Gram-negatives. "A"mox covers All ear/sinus bugs.
Antistaphylococcal Nafcillin, Oxacillin, Dicloxacillin Penicillinase-producing MSSA (Methicillin-Sensitive Staph). "N.O.D.": Naf, Ox, Dicloxacillin defend against Staph.
Antipseudomonal Piperacillin (+ Tazobactam / Zosyn) Extended spectrum against Pseudomonas aeruginosa & anaerobes. "Pipe" lines clear out Pseudomonas.

💡 Mnemonic for Uses & Effects: "N.A.P.S."

Necrotizing infections • Anthrax prophylaxis • Pneumonia • Syphilis gold standard.

🚫 Contraindications & Nursing Pearls

Absolute allergy check required for IgE-mediated beta-lactam anaphylaxis. Observe IV patients for 30 minutes post-dose. Monitor renal function and electrolyte loads (Na+/K+).

2. Cephalosporins Classification & Profile

Broad-spectrum beta-lactams structured across five generations. Increasing generation number correlates with heightened Gram-negative coverage and CNS penetration.

Generation Representative Drugs Spectrum / Key Feature Memory Trick / Mnemonic
1st Generation Cefazolin, Cephalexin Strong Gram-positive skin/soft tissue; surgical prophylaxis. "1st" class Skin coverage.
2nd Generation Cefuroxime, Cefaclor, Cefoxitin Enhanced Gram-negative rods; Cefoxitin covers anaerobes. "2" lungs (Respiratory tract infections).
3rd Generation Ceftriaxone, Ceftazidime, Cefotaxime Crosses Blood-Brain Barrier; excellent for meningitis & gonorrhea. "3"rd eye sees into the Brain (CSF penetration).
4th & 5th Gen Cefepime (4th), Ceftaroline (5th) 4th: Anti-pseudomonal. 5th: Covers MRSA strains. "Ceftaroline" starts with C and covers Clastic MRSA.

💡 Mnemonic for Adverse Effects: "B.L.E.E.D."

Bleeding risks (hypoprothrombinemia) • Liver enzymes elevated • Electrolyte shifts • Enterocolitis (C. diff) • Disulfiram alcohol reaction (with cefotetan).

🚫 Precautions & Nursing Points

Check for cross-reactivity in penicillin-allergic patients (~5-10%). Ceftriaxone excretes via biliary route (no renal dosage adjustment required).

3. Carbapenems & Monobactams Classification

Ultra-broad spectrum beta-lactams resistant to most beta-lactamases, reserved for severe nosocomial superbugs.

Subclass Representative Drugs Spectrum / Target Memory Trick / Mnemonic
Carbapenems Meropenem, Imipenem/Cilastatin, Ertapenem Broadest spectrum for severe hospital/multidrug-resistant infections. "M.I.E.": Meropenem Is Extreme broad-spectrum.
Monobactams Aztreonam Narrow ring structure targeting aerobic Gram-negative rods exclusively. "A"ztreonam is Allergic-safe (no cross-reactivity).

💡 Mnemonic for Uses: "S.E.V.E.R.E."

Sepsis • Enterobacteriaceae resistant strains • Ventilator pneumonia • Ertapenem for intra-abdominal infections • Resistant outbreaks • Extreme mixed infections.

🚫 Clinical Nursing Cautions

Imipenem can lower the seizure threshold in renal impairment or CNS disorders. Aztreonam is safe to administer to patients with severe penicillin/cephalosporin anaphylaxis history.

4. Glycopeptides (Including Vancomycin) Classification

Complex tricyclic antibiotics that bind to D-alanyl-D-alanine amino acid precursors, inhibiting cell wall synthesis independently of PBPs. Vancomycin is the flagship medication of this class.

Subclass / Drug Route & Formulations Primary Indication / Target Memory Trick / Mnemonic
Vancomycin IV (Systemic) / Oral (C. diff colitis) Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), severe Gram-positives. "Van" travels deep for resistant bugs.
Teicoplanin IV / IM Longer half-life lipoglycopeptide for severe gram-positive infections. Tei-co = Tracks Extended half-life.
Dalbavancin & Oritavancin IV (Single dose) Acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI). Dal-ba = Dose once, bye-bye skin infection!

💡 Mnemonic for Adverse Effects: "R.E.D. M.A.N."

Red Man Syndrome (histamine flush from rapid infusion) • Ear toxicity / Ototoxicity • Damage to kidneys / Nephrotoxicity • Muscle pain / phlebitis • Anaphylactoid reactions • No IM administration permitted for Vancomycin.

⭐ Critical Nursing Protocol for Vancomycin

• Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM): Draw serum trough levels immediately prior to the 4th dose to ensure therapeutic range and prevent toxicity.
• Infusion Rate: Infuse slowly over at least 60 minutes (or longer for large doses) at concentrations not exceeding 5 mg/mL.
• Red Man Management: If flushing/hypotension occurs, stop infusion immediately, administer an antihistamine (diphenhydramine), and restart at a reduced rate.
