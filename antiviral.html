Antiviral Pharmacology Guide — Expanded Classifications & Detailed MOA

Comprehensive educational reference guide featuring clear, expanded pharmacological explanations of Mechanism of Action (MOA), Clinical Uses, and Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) across all antiviral drug classes.

Classification: Drugs for Respiratory Virus Infections

Detailed pharmacological breakdown of antiviral agents targeting influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and coronavirus infections.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Oseltamivir / Zanamivir / Peramivir
MOA: These drugs competitively and selectively bind to the active site of viral neuraminidase enzymes on the surface of influenza A and B viruses. By inhibiting neuraminidase, they prevent the cleavage of sialic acid residues on the host cell surface, which traps newly synthesized virions on the cell membrane, preventing viral release and subsequent spread through the respiratory tract.
Uses: Treatment and chemoprophylaxis of acute uncomplicated Influenza A and B infections.
ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, headache, psychiatric disturbances (hallucinations), and bronchospasm (specifically with inhaled zanamivir in asthma patients).
Baloxavir Marboxil
MOA: A prodrug converted to baloxavir, which selectively inhibits the cap-dependent endonuclease protein (a subunit of the viral RNA polymerase complex). This specific inhibition halts the transcription of viral messenger RNA (mRNA), effectively blocking viral gene expression and halting viral replication.
Uses: Single-dose oral treatment of acute uncomplicated influenza in patients aged 12 years and older.
ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, nasopharyngitis, and headache.
Amantadine / Rimantadine
MOA: These tricyclic amines block the M2 proton ion channel proteins embedded in the viral envelope of Influenza A. By blocking proton influx into the virion during endocytosis, they prevent the acidification required for viral uncoating, halting the release of viral ribonucleoprotein into the host cell cytoplasm.
Uses: Historical treatment and prophylaxis of Influenza A (largely abandoned due to widespread viral resistance).
ADRs: Central nervous system toxicity (nervousness, anxiety, insomnia, difficulty concentrating, dizziness) and peripheral anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, blurred vision).
Palivizumab
MOA: A humanized monoclonal antibody (IgG1) that specifically targets and binds to an epitope on the F (fusion) protein of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). This binding neutralizes the virus and blocks the conformational changes required for viral entry and fusion into the host respiratory epithelial cells.
Uses: Prevention of serious lower respiratory tract disease caused by RSV in high-risk infants and preterm neonates.
ADRs: Injection site reactions, upper respiratory tract infections, fever, and skin rashes.
Remdesivir
MOA: An adenosine nucleotide analog prodrug that undergoes intracellular metabolism to form an active triphosphate metabolite. This metabolite acts as a competitive inhibitor of viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp). When incorporated into nascent viral RNA chains, it causes premature termination of RNA transcription, inhibiting viral replication.
Uses: Treatment of COVID-19 in hospitalized patients requiring supplemental oxygen therapy.
ADRs: Transiently elevated liver transaminases (ALT and AST), nausea, and infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.B.A.P.R."

Oseltamivir • Baloxavir • Amantadine • Palivizumab • Remdesivir.

Classification: HIV Nucleoside & Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs & NtRTIs)

Backbone agents of antiretroviral therapy (ART). They act as false building blocks that incorporate into viral DNA and cause chain termination.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Zidovudine (AZT / ZDV)
MOA: A synthetic thymidine nucleoside analog. Once phosphorylated intracellularly by cellular enzymes into its active triphosphate form, it competes with natural thymidine triphosphate for incorporation into viral DNA by HIV reverse transcriptase. Because it lacks a 3'-hydroxyl group, its incorporation results in the immediate termination of the growing viral DNA chain, halting reverse transcription.
Uses: Comprehensive HIV-1 treatment regimens and the prevention of perinatal (mother-to-child) transmission.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression leading to dose-dependent anemia and neutropenia, macrocytic anemia, headache, myopathy, and gastrointestinal upset.
Lamivudine (3TC)
MOA: A cytidine analog NRTI. Intracellularly phosphorylated to its active triphosphate form, it competes with natural deoxycytidine triphosphate for incorporation by HIV reverse transcriptase (and HBV polymerase), causing premature viral DNA chain termination.
Uses: Combination antiretroviral regimens for HIV and dual-activity management of chronic Hepatitis B virus infection.
ADRs: Highly favorable toxicity profile; occasional headache, fatigue, nausea, and rare reports of pancreatitis.
Emtricitabine (FTC)
MOA: A fluorinated cytidine analog NRTI. After intracellular phosphorylation, it inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase via viral DNA chain termination, structurally similar to lamivudine with a prolonged intracellular half-life.
Uses: Frontline HIV treatment regimens and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) to prevent high-risk acquisition.
ADRs: Headache, diarrhea, nausea, and benign skin hyperpigmentation predominantly occurring on the palms and soles.
Abacavir (ABC)
MOA: A carbocyclic guanosine analog NRTI. Intracellularly converted to carbovir triphosphate, it competitively inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase and gets incorporated into viral DNA, causing chain termination.
Uses: Frontline cART backbone (mandates pre-screening for the HLA-B*5701 allele prior to initiation).
ADRs: Potentially fatal systemic hypersensitivity reaction characterized by fever, rash, gastrointestinal distress, and fatigue in patients carrying the HLA-B*5701 allele; nausea and insomnia.
Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF)
MOA: An acyclic nucleoside phosphonate (nucleotide analog / NtRti) representing adenosine monophosphate. Because it already possesses a phosphonate group, it requires only two intracellular phosphorylation steps to reach its active diphosphate form, which inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase and HBV polymerase via chain termination.
Uses: Comprehensive HIV treatment, PrEP protocols, and chronic Hepatitis B therapy.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity, proximal renal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi syndrome), and long-term reduction in bone mineral density.
Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF)
MOA: A targeted prodrug of tenofovir designed with enhanced plasma stability. It crosses cell membranes more efficiently than TDF, allowing lower systemic drug levels while achieving high intracellular concentrations in lymphoid cells and hepatocytes, where it is hydrolyzed to tenofovir and phosphorylated to its active triphosphate form to terminate viral DNA chains.
Uses: HIV treatment and PrEP with significantly reduced systemic exposure, lowering renal and bone toxicities compared to TDF.
ADRs: Mild headache, fatigue, and nausea; demonstrates significantly lower rates of renal impairment and bone density loss compared to TDF.
Stavudine (d4T)
MOA: A thymidine analog NRTI that is phosphorylated intracellularly to stavudine triphosphate, competitively inhibiting HIV reverse transcriptase and causing viral DNA chain termination.
Uses: Historical HIV treatment (largely discontinued in modern clinical practice due to severe metabolic toxicity).
ADRs: Severe peripheral neuropathy, progressive lipoatrophy (subcutaneous fat wasting), lactic acidosis, and fatal pancreatitis.
Didanosine (ddI)
MOA: A purine analog NRTI (inosine derivative) that converts intracellularly to dideoxyadenosine triphosphate, inhibiting HIV reverse transcriptase and terminating viral DNA synthesis.
Uses: Historical alternative NRTI used in older combination antiretroviral regimens.
ADRs: Dose-dependent pancreatitis, painful peripheral neuropathy, diarrhea, and lactic acidosis.

💡 Mnemonic: "Z.L.E.A.T.S.D." (HIV NRTIs & NtRTIs)

Zidovudine • Lamivudine • Emtricitabine • Abacavir • Tenofovir (TDF/TAF) • Stavudine • Didanosine.

Classification: Drugs for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Infections

Expanded pharmacological agents dedicated to chronic Hepatitis B management and viral suppression.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Entecavir
MOA: A guanosine nucleoside analog that is phosphorylated intracellularly to active entecavir triphosphate. It competes with natural deoxyguanosine triphosphate to inhibit all three functional activities of HBV polymerase: base priming, negative-strand viral DNA synthesis, and positive-strand viral DNA elongation.
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B infection characterized by active viral replication and elevated transaminases.
ADRs: Headache, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, and rare instances of lactic acidosis with severe hepatomegaly.
Telbivudine
MOA: A natural L-nucleoside analog (L-deoxythymidine) that is phosphorylated intracellularly to telbivudine 5'-triphosphate, which selectively inhibits HBV DNA polymerase by competing with the natural substrate thymidine triphosphate.
Uses: Treatment of chronic hepatitis B infection with high viral load in adult patients.
ADRs: Elevated serum creatine kinase (CK) levels, skeletal myopathy, fatigue, headache, and dizziness.
Adefovir Dipivoxil
MOA: An oral diester prodrug of adefovir, an acyclic nucleotide analog of adenosine monophosphate. Once converted intracellularly to adefovir diphosphate, it competitively inhibits HBV DNA polymerase and causes chain termination upon incorporation into viral DNA.
Uses: Management of chronic hepatitis B, including strains resistant to lamivudine.
ADRs: Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity, proximal tubular dysfunction causing hypophosphatemia, asthenia, and headache.
Peginterferon Alfa-2a
MOA: A recombinant interferon alfa-2a molecule conjugated with a polyethylene glycol (pegylated) moiety to prolong plasma half-life. It binds to specific cell-surface interferon receptors, triggering an intracellular signaling cascade (JAK-STAT pathway) that induces interferon-stimulated genes, stimulating natural killer cells and cell-mediated immunity to suppress viral transcription.
Uses: HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B virus infection.
ADRs: Flu-like symptoms (fever, chills, myalgia), severe depression, bone marrow suppression, alopecia, and autoimmune thyroid disorders.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.T.A.P." (HBV Agents)

Entecavir • Telbivudine • Adefovir • Peginterferon.

Classification: Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) for Hepatitis C Virus (HCV)

Expanded direct-acting antiviral drug classes targeting vital viral non-structural proteins to achieve sustained virologic response (SVR).

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Sofosbuvir
MOA: A nucleotide analog prodrug that undergoes intracellular metabolism to form the active uridine triphosphate analog. This active form acts as a defective substrate for the HCV non-structural protein 5B (NS5B) RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, causing chain termination during viral RNA replication.
Uses: Pan-genotypic treatment of chronic HCV genotypes 1 through 6 in combination direct-acting antiviral regimens.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, insomnia, and severe life-threatening symptomatic bradycardia if co-administered with amiodarone.
Ledipasvir / Velpatasvir / Pibrentasvir / Daclatasvir
MOA: These agents inhibit the HCV non-structural protein 5A (NS5A), a critical phosphoprotein essential for viral RNA replication complex assembly, viral particle formation, and virion secretion.
Uses: Pan-genotypic eradication of chronic hepatitis C virus infection.
ADRs: Mild headache, fatigue, diarrhea, nausea, and dizziness.
Glecaprevir / Voxilaprevir / Simeprevir / Grazoprevir
MOA: These drugs act as covalent or reversible covalent inhibitors of the HCV non-structural protein 3/4A (NS3/4A) serine protease. By blocking this enzyme, they prevent the proteolytic cleavage of the viral polyprotein precursor into functional non-structural proteins required for replication.
Uses: Treatment of chronic HCV infections, including complex cases with prior DAA failure and compensated cirrhosis.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, pruritus, transient elevations in serum bilirubin, and photosensitivity reactions (specifically with simeprevir).
Ribavirin
MOA: A synthetic guanosine nucleoside analog that multiple mechanisms: inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (depleting intracellular GTP pools), enhances viral mutation rates during replication leading to error catastrophe, and modulates host T-cell mediated immunity.
Uses: Combination therapeutic regimens for chronic HCV or severe lower respiratory syncytial virus infections.
ADRs: Dose-dependent hemolytic anemia, severe teratogenicity (Pregnancy Category X), fatigue, and rash.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.L.G.R."

Sofosbuvir • Ledipasvir • Glecaprevir • Ribavirin.

Classification: Drugs for Herpesvirus and Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Expanded nucleoside and nucleotide analogs for herpes simplex virus (HSV), varicella-zoster virus (VZV), and cytomegalovirus (CMV) infections.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir
MOA: These purine nucleoside analogs require activation by viral thymidine kinase (encoded by HSV/VZV) for initial monophosphorylation, followed by cellular kinases to form triphosphate metabolites. These triphosphates competitively inhibit viral DNA polymerase and incorporate into viral DNA, causing chain termination.
Uses: Management of genital herpes, varicella-zoster (shingles/chickenpox), and herpes simplex encephalitis.
ADRs: Nausea, headache, crystalline nephropathy (precipitation of drug crystals in renal tubules during rapid IV administration), and acute neurotoxicity.
Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir
MOA: A synthetic guanine analog that is phosphorylated initially by a viral kinase (UL97 phosphotransferase in CMV) and subsequently by cellular kinases. Its active triphosphate form competitively inhibits CMV DNA polymerase and terminates viral DNA elongation.
Uses: Treatment and secondary prevention of life-threatening CMV retinitis and systemic disease in immunocompromised patients.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), fever, and rash.
Cidofovir
MOA: An acyclic nucleotide analog of deoxycytidine monophosphate. Because it bypasses viral kinase activation (relying entirely on cellular enzymes for diphosphorylation), it maintains activity against certain acyclovir-resistant HSV and ganciclovir-resistant CMV strains by inhibiting viral DNA polymerase directly.
Uses: Treatment of resistant cytomegalovirus retinitis in patients with AIDS.
ADRs: Severe dose-dependent nephrotoxicity (mandates co-administration with probenecid and rigorous saline hydration), proteinuria, and neutropenia.
Foscarnet
MOA: An organic pyrophosphate analog that directly and non-competitively binds to the pyrophosphate binding site of viral DNA polymerases and reverse transcriptases (without requiring phosphorylation). This action blocks the cleavage of pyrophosphate from deoxynucleotide triphosphates, halting viral DNA synthesis.
Uses: Acyclovir-resistant HSV/VZV infections and ganciclovir-resistant CMV infections.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity, severe electrolyte wasting (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia), and seizures.
Letermovir
MOA: A non-nucleoside CMV inhibitor that targets the viral terminase complex (specifically the pUL56 subunit). By interfering with viral DNA maturation and packaging into capsids, it prevents the production of infectious viral particles.
Uses: Prophylaxis of cytomegalovirus infection and disease in adult allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients.
ADRs: Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, peripheral edema, headache, and fatigue.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.G.C.F.L."

Acyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet • Letermovir.

Classification: HIV Antiretrovirals (NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & Entry/Capsid Inhibitors)

Expanded classes targeting non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase, viral proteases, host entry receptors, and capsid protein assembly.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Efavirenz / Rilpivirine / Doravirine / Nevirapine / Etravirine
MOA: Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) bind directly and non-competitively to a hydrophobic allosteric pocket near the active catalytic site of the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase enzyme. This binding induces a conformational change that immobilizes the catalytic residues, halting DNA polymerization.
Uses: Combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for treatment-naive and experienced HIV-1 infection.
ADRs: Central nervous system symptoms (vivid dreams, insomnia, dizziness with efavirenz), severe skin rashes (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), and hepatotoxicity (notably with nevirapine).
Darunavir / Atazanavir / Lopinavir / Indinavir / Saquinavir / Tipranavir
MOA: Protease inhibitors (PIs) competitively bind to the active site of the HIV-1 protease enzyme. By blocking this enzyme, they prevent the cleavage of gag-pol viral polyprotein precursors into functional structural proteins and essential enzymes, resulting in the production of immature, non-infectious viral particles.
Uses: Treatment-naive and treatment-experienced HIV-1 infection management.
ADRs: Metabolic complications including dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, hyperglycemia, lipodystrophy (fat redistribution), indirect hyperbilirubinemia (atazanavir), and nephrolithiasis (indinavir).
Maraviroc
MOA: A selective, allosteric antagonist of the human CCR5 coreceptor found on the surface of CD4+ T-cells. By binding to CCR5, it induces a conformational change that prevents the interaction between the viral gp120 envelope glycoprotein and the host cell coreceptor, blocking viral entry.
Uses: Treatment-experienced adult patients infected exclusively with CCR5-tropic HIV-1 strains.
ADRs: Hepatotoxicity (frequently preceded by a systemic allergic rash), upper respiratory tract infections, and orthostatic hypotension.
Enfuvirtide
MOA: A synthetic 36-amino acid fusion inhibitor peptide that binds to the heptad repeat 1 (HR1) region of the HIV gp41 envelope glycoprotein. This competitive binding prevents the conformational zip-like folding of gp41 required to bridge viral and host cell membranes, stopping viral entry.
Uses: Salvage therapy for heavily treatment-experienced HIV patients with resistant viral strains.
ADRs: Painful subcutaneous injection site nodules, pruritus, erythema, and an increased risk of bacterial pneumonia.
Fostemsavir / Ibalizumab / Lenacapavir
MOA: Fostemsavir is a prodrug attached to the HIV gp120 envelope protein to prevent initial receptor attachment; Ibalizumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds domain 2 of CD4 receptors to block post-attachment conformational changes; Lenacapavir is a first-in-class capsid inhibitor that binds directly to the HIV-1 capsid protein (p24) subunits, disrupting capsid assembly, disassembly, and nuclear transport.
Uses: Multidrug-resistant advanced HIV-1 salvage regimens when other therapeutic options fail.
ADRs: Nausea, diarrhea, elevated liver enzymes, and injection site swelling or nodules (lenacapavir).

💡 Mnemonic: "E.D.M.E.F."

Efavirenz • Darunavir • Maraviroc • Enfuvirtide • Fostemsavir.

Classification: HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

Expanded frontline anchor class in modern antiretroviral therapy regimens.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Dolutegravir / Bictegravir
MOA: These integrase strand transfer inhibitors bind to the active metal (magnesium ions) site in the catalytic core of the HIV integrase enzyme. By doing so, they block the strand transfer biochemical step of viral DNA integration into human host cell chromosomal DNA, arresting the replication cycle.
Uses: Preferred frontline anchor agents for initial and maintenance treatment of HIV-1 infection.
ADRs: Insomnia, headache, weight gain, and nausea.
Raltegravir
MOA: Binds to the integrase active site and chelates divalent metal ions, specifically inhibiting the strand transfer activity of HIV-1 integrase to prevent covalent insertion of viral cDNA into host cellular DNA.
Uses: Treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral agents.
ADRs: Insomnia, nausea, headache, diarrhea, severe hypersensitivity reactions (DRESS syndrome), and asymptomatic creatine phosphokinase (CPK) elevations.
Elvitegravir
MOA: Chelates divalent metal ions at the active site of HIV-1 integrase, blocking strand transfer and halting proviral DNA integration (requires pharmacokinetic boosting with cobicistat or ritonavir).
Uses: Combined single-tablet regimens for HIV-1 management.
ADRs: Nausea, diarrhea, headache, and insomnia.
Cabotegravir
MOA: A long-acting integrase strand transfer inhibitor that binds the integrase active site and blocks viral DNA strand transfer into host chromatin, formulated with nanosuspension technology for extended absorption.
Uses: Long-acting intramuscular injectable regimens for HIV treatment and pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP).
ADRs: Injection site reactions (pain, induration, nodules), pyrexia, fatigue, headache, and musculoskeletal pain.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir"

Bictegravir • Cabotegravir • Dolutegravir • Elvitegravir • Raltegravir.

Classification: Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Agents used to inhibit hepatic cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) enzymes and boost co-administered antiretroviral drug plasma half-lives.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Ritonavir
MOA: A potent inhibitor of hepatic CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein efflux pumps. While originally developed as a protease inhibitor, it is utilized at low sub-therapeutic booster doses to inhibit the metabolism of co-administered protease inhibitors, significantly raising their plasma concentrations and extending dosing intervals.
Uses: Low-dose pharmacokinetic enhancer (booster) for protease inhibitors in cART regimens.
ADRs: Gastrointestinal distress (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting), dyslipidemia, and elevated transaminases.
Cobicistat
MOA: A selective mechanism-based inhibitor of CYP3A enzymes. Unlike ritonavir, cobicistat possesses zero intrinsic anti-HIV activity and does not inhibit other cytochrome pathways, serving purely as a pharmacokinetic booster.
Uses: Pharmacokinetic booster integrated into fixed-dose single-tablet regimens with protease inhibitors or elvitegravir.
ADRs: Benign increase in serum creatinine (caused by inhibition of renal tubular creatinine secretion without affecting glomerular filtration rate), nausea, and jaundice.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C. Boosters"

Ritonavir • Cobicistat.

Classification: HIV Fixed-Dose Combinations (Single-Tablet Regimens)

Expanded single-tablet regimens combining multiple antiretroviral classes into one daily pill for maximal adherence.

📂 Regimen Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Biktarvy (Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / TAF)
MOA: Multitarget dual-class synergy: bictegravir inhibits integrase strand transfer, while emtricitabine and tenofovir alafenamide act as nucleotide/nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors to terminate viral DNA chain elongation.
Uses: Frontline complete single-tablet regimen for initial HIV-1 treatment-naive adults.
ADRs: Diarrhea, nausea, headache, and mild lipid changes; exceptional renal and bone safety profile.
Triumeq (Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine)
MOA: Triple-drug blockade combining the integrase inhibitor dolutegravir with the dual NRTI backbone of abacavir and lamivudine to inhibit both reverse transcription and viral DNA integration.
Uses: Complete single-tablet antiretroviral regimen (requires negative HLA-B*5701 pre-screening).
ADRs: Insomnia, fatigue, nausea, headache, and potentially fatal hypersensitivity if genetically susceptible.
Truvada / Descovy (NRTI Backbone Combinations)
MOA: Dual NRTI combinations (emtricitabine paired with TDF or TAF) that competitively inhibit HIV reverse transcriptase and cause viral DNA chain termination.
Uses: Pharmacological foundation for cART combination regimens and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP).
ADRs: Headache, nausea, and mild gastrointestinal distress; routine renal and bone monitoring required for Truvada (TDF).
Symtuza (Darunavir / Cobicistat / Emtricitabine / TAF)
MOA: Combines a boosted protease inhibitor (darunavir enhanced by cobicistat) with a dual NRTI backbone (emtricitabine and TAF) to simultaneously inhibit protease-mediated polyprotein cleavage and reverse transcriptase.
Uses: Complete single-tablet regimen for HIV-1 treatment-naive and experienced adults.
ADRs: Diarrhea, skin rash, nausea, headache, dyslipidemia, and insulin resistance.

🚫 Clinical Warnings & Nursing Interventions

Always perform baseline hepatitis B screening and check baseline renal function (eGFR and urinalysis) before administering tenofovir-containing regimens. Screen patients genetically for the HLA-B*5701 allele before prescribing abacavir-containing regimens to prevent severe hypersensitivity reactions.
