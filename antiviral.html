Antiviral Pharmacology Guide

Complete classification breakdown, exact mechanisms of action, clinical uses, and high-yield exam mnemonics for viral infections.

Respiratory Virus Infections

Antiviral agents used to prevent and treat acute respiratory infections, primarily Influenza and Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV).

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Oseltamivir / Zanamivir MOA: Neuraminidase Inhibitors Inhibits viral neuraminidase enzyme, preventing newly replicated influenza virions from budding off and spreading to other host cells.
Baloxavir Marboxil MOA: Cap-Dependent Endonuclease Inhibitor Blocks the polymerase acidic protein of influenza viruses, halting viral transcription and mRNA synthesis.
Palivizumab MOA: Monoclonal Antibody Fusion Blocker Binds to the F protein of RSV, preventing viral entry and infection in high-risk pediatric infants.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Blocking Respiratory Spread

🦠
Step 1: Virus Entry Respiratory virus enters airway epithelial cells and replicates rapidly.
🛑
Step 2: Enzyme Blockade Drugs inhibit neuraminidase or endonuclease enzymes to halt viral progeny maturation.
🛡️
Step 3: Release Prevention Infected cells cannot release new virions, trapping the infection locally.
✨
Step 4: Symptom Reduction Shortens illness duration and prevents severe lower respiratory complications.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.Z.B." for Influenza

Oseltamivir (oral pill) • Zanamivir (inhaled powder) • Baloxavir (single-dose endonuclease inhibitor).

Hepatic Virus Infections (Hepatitis B & C)

Targeted antiviral therapies and direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) to suppress chronic Hepatitis B and cure Hepatitis C infections.

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Entecavir / Tenofovir (HBV) MOA: Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors Competes with natural nucleotides to inhibit HBV polymerase and viral DNA chain elongation.
Sofosbuvir (HCV) MOA: NS5B Polymerase Inhibitor Acts as a nucleotide prodrug that terminates HCV RNA chain elongation during replication.
Ledipasvir / Glecaprevir (HCV) MOA: NS5A / Protease Inhibitors Disrupts HCV viral assembly proteins and blocks core protease enzymes required for viral maturation.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Hepatic Viral Suppression & Cure

🧬
Step 1: Nucleotide Mimicry Drugs mimic building blocks and target viral polymerases inside hepatocytes.
⛓️
Step 2: Chain Termination Abruptly stops viral DNA or RNA strand growth.
📉
Step 3: Viral Load Drop Significantly lowers circulating viral counts, reducing liver inflammation and cirrhosis risk.
✨
Step 4: Sustained Virologic Response Achieves permanent cure for HCV (SVR12) and long-term remission for HBV.

💡 Mnemonic: "H.E.P. S.O.F."

Hepatitis B treated with Entecavir & Peginterferon • Sofosbuvir • Other Fixed DAAs curing Hepatitis C.

Herpesvirus and Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Nucleoside analogs designed to manage Herpes Simplex (HSV), Varicella-Zoster (VZV), and Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infections.

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Acyclovir / Valacyclovir MOA: Viral DNA Polymerase Inhibitor Requires viral thymidine kinase for initial activation; incorporates into viral DNA to cause chain termination.
Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir MOA: CMV Polymerase Inhibitor Specifically targeted for cytomegalovirus; inhibits viral DNA polymerase and halts replication.
Foscarnet MOA: Pyrophosphate Analog Directly inhibits viral DNA polymerase without requiring prior viral enzyme activation.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Herpes DNA Termination

🔑
Step 1: Viral Activation Drug is selectively phosphorylated by viral enzymes inside infected cells.
🧱
Step 2: False Building Block Inserts into growing viral DNA strands, mimicking normal nucleotides.
🛑
Step 3: Synthesis Halt Stops further DNA elongation, halting blister formation and nerve replication.
✨
Step 4: Latent Suppression Controls recurrent outbreaks and systemic CMV complications.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.V.G.F."

Acyclovir • Valacyclovir • Ganciclovir (CMV specialist) • Foscarnet (resistant strains rescue).

HIV: NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & Entry Inhibitors

Antiretroviral classes targeting distinct phases of the HIV replication cycle: reverse transcription, polyprotein cleavage, and cellular entry.

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Efavirenz / Rilpivirine (NNRTIs) MOA: Non-Nucleoside RT Inhibitors Binds directly and non-competitively to HIV reverse transcriptase enzyme, changing its shape and disabling function.
Atazanavir / Darunavir (Protease Inhibitors) MOA: HIV Protease Blockers Inhibits HIV protease enzyme, preventing cleavage of viral polyproteins into mature, infectious viral particles.
Maraviroc / Enfuvirtide (Entry Inhibitors) MOA: CCR5 Antagonist / Fusion Inhibitor Blocks host cell CCR5 coreceptors or prevents viral membrane fusion to stop HIV from entering CD4 cells.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Multi-Target Antiretroviral Blockade

🚪
Step 1: Entry Block Entry inhibitors stop HIV from binding or fusing to CD4 cell membranes.
⚙️
Step 2: Enzyme Shutdown NNRTIs disable reverse transcriptase so viral RNA is never converted to DNA.
✂️
Step 3: Cleavage Halt Protease inhibitors prevent structural proteins from assembling into functional viruses.
✨
Step 4: Viral Suppression Maintains undetectable viral loads and preserves immune function.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.P.E."

Efavirenz (NNRTI) • Protease inhibitors ending in -navir • Entry & fusion blockers (Maraviroc/Enfuvirtide).

HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

The preferred frontline anchor class for modern antiretroviral therapy due to high potency and rapid viral load reduction.

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Dolutegravir / Bictegravir MOA: Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibition Binds to the HIV integrase active site, blocking strand transfer of viral DNA into the host cell chromosomal DNA.
Raltegravir MOA: First-Generation INSTI Inhibits insertion of viral genetic material into human DNA, preventing permanent provirus formation.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Stopping Genomic Integration

🧬
Step 1: DNA Migration Viral DNA enters the host cell nucleus looking for human chromosomes.
🔗
Step 2: Enzyme Blockade INSTIs bind to integrase and block strand transfer activity.
❌
Step 3: Insertion Failure Viral DNA cannot integrate into host DNA and is degraded.
✨
Step 4: Replication Arrest Stops permanent viral establishment in immune cells.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir"

Bictegravir • Dolutegravir • Raltegravir • Elvitegravir.

Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Pharmacological agents used solely to inhibit liver enzymes and boost blood concentrations of co-administered antiretroviral drugs.

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Ritonavir MOA: CYP3A4 Enzyme Inhibitor Potent cytochrome P450 3A4 inhibitor; boosts half-life and plasma levels of protease inhibitors.
Cobicistat MOA: Pharmacokinetic Enhancer Selective CYP3A inhibitor with no antiretroviral activity of its own; used to boost INSTIs and PIs.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Boosting Drug Levels

💊
Step 1: Co-Administration Booster is paired with a primary protease inhibitor or integrase inhibitor.
🛡️
Step 2: Liver Enzyme Block Inhibits CYP3A metabolism in the liver and gut wall.
📈
Step 3: Extended Half-Life Maintains higher, therapeutic drug levels in the bloodstream for longer periods.
✨
Step 4: Reduced Dosing Allows for lower pill burdens and once-daily dosing schedules.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C." Boosters

Ritonavir • Cobicistat (The two pharmacokinetic boosters used in HIV therapy).

HIV Fixed-Dose Combinations (Single-Tablet Regimens)

Combinations of multiple antiretroviral medications formulated into a single daily tablet to maximize patient adherence and treatment success.

📂 Important FDC Regimens & Uses

Biktarvy Components: Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / Tenofovir alafenamide First-line single-tablet regimen for treatment-naive adults.
Triumeq Components: Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine Once-daily potent INSTI-based backbone regimen (requires HLA-B*5701 testing for abacavir).
Symtuza Components: Darunavir / Cobicistat / Emtricitabine / Tenofovir alafenamide Complete protease inhibitor-based single-tablet regimen.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Simplified Adherence Strategy

📋
Step 1: Multi-Class Synergy Combines 2 or 3 active drugs from different classes in one pill.
🎯
Step 2: Dual Attack Blocks multiple viral replication steps simultaneously (e.g., integrase + reverse transcriptase).
🤝
Step 3: High Adherence Simplifies dosing schedule, preventing missed doses and drug resistance.
✨
Step 4: Long-term Success Sustains viral suppression and preserves immune health.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.T.R."

Single Tablet Regimens improve patient compliance and reduce viral resistance.

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Always screen patients for baseline hepatitis B and kidney function before starting tenofovir-containing regimens. Screen for HLA-B*5701 before prescribing abacavir-containing regimens to prevent severe hypersensitivity reactions.
