Antiviral Pharmacology Guide — Classifications, MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Complete reference guide featuring separated Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C classifications, Nucleoside/Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors, precise clinical uses, and specific drug-level Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs).

Classification: Drugs for Respiratory Virus Infections

Detailed pharmacological breakdown of antiviral agents targeting influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and severe acute respiratory infections, complete with indications and specific adverse drug reactions.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Oseltamivir / Zanamivir / Peramivir MOA: Neuraminidase Inhibitors
Clinical Uses: Treatment and prophylaxis of acute uncomplicated Influenza A and B infections. Specific ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain; neuropsychiatric events (confusion, abnormal behavior, self-injury, mostly in pediatric patients taking Oseltamivir); bronchospasm and cough (primarily with inhaled Zanamivir).
Baloxavir Marboxil MOA: Cap-Dependent Endonuclease Inhibitor
Clinical Uses: Acute uncomplicated influenza in patients aged 12 years and older. Specific ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, nasopharyngitis, headache.
Palivizumab MOA: Monoclonal Antibody Fusion Blocker
Clinical Uses: Prevention of serious lower respiratory tract disease caused by RSV in high-risk infants. Specific ADRs: Injection site reactions, upper respiratory tract infections, fever, rash, rare anaphylaxis.
Remdesivir MOA: RNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Clinical Uses: Treatment of COVID-19 requiring hospital admission and supplemental oxygen. Specific ADRs: Elevated liver transaminases (ALT/AST), nausea, headache, infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions (hypotension, sweating, shivering).
Nirmatrelvir / Ritonavir (Paxlovid) MOA: Viral Protease Inhibitor + Booster
Clinical Uses: Mild-to-moderate COVID-19 in adults at high risk for progression to severe disease. Specific ADRs: Dysgeusia (altered taste/metallic taste), diarrhea, hypertension, myalgia, severe drug-drug interactions due to CYP3A4 inhibition.

⚡ Action Flow: Stopping Respiratory Pathogen Proliferation

🦠
Step 1: Inhalation & Entry Viruses invade upper and lower respiratory tract mucosal tissues.
⚙️
Step 2: Targeted Inhibition Drugs block viral uncoating, polymerases, endonucleases, or neuraminidase.
🛡️
Step 3: Replication Arrest Intracellular transcription and viral budding are completely blocked.
✨
Step 4: Clinical Recovery Reduces symptom severity, duration, and hospitalization risks.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.Z.P.B.R." (Respiratory Antivirals)

Oseltamivir • Zanamivir • Peramivir • Baloxavir • Remdesivir.

Classification: Nucleoside & Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs) for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV)

Targeted nucleoside and nucleotide analogues engineered to incorporate into viral DNA and inhibit HBV polymerase, suppressing chronic viral replication and preventing cirrhosis.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Molecular MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Entecavir MOA: Guanosine Nucleoside Analog NRTI
Clinical Uses: Chronic Hepatitis B virus infection with active viral replication and evidence of persistent hepatocellular inflammation. Specific ADRs: Headache, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, elevated blood glucose, rare lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis.
Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) / Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF) MOA: Nucleotide Analog RTIs (NtRTI)
Clinical Uses: Chronic Hepatitis B infection and HIV-1 treatment. Specific ADRs: TDF: Nephrotoxicity, Fanconi syndrome, hypophosphatemia, decreased bone mineral density (osteomalacia). TAF: Significantly reduced renal and bone toxicity, headache, nausea, fatigue.
Lamivudine MOA: Cytidine Analog NRTI
Clinical Uses: Chronic Hepatitis B (and HIV coinfection regimens). Specific ADRs: Headache, fatigue, nausea, abdominal discomfort, pancreatitis (rare), lactic acidosis upon sudden cessation (viral flare).
Adefovir Dipivoxil MOA: Acyclic Nucleotide Analog NRTI
Clinical Uses: Chronic Hepatitis B, especially lamivudine-resistant strains. Specific ADRs: Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity (elevated serum creatinine), hypophosphatemia, asthenia, generalized weakness.
Peginterferon Alfa-2a MOA: Immune Modulator / Cytokine
Clinical Uses: HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B. Specific ADRs: Influenza-like symptoms (fever, chills, myalgia), depression, suicidal ideation, alopecia, bone marrow suppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia).

⚡ Action Flow: Chronic Hepatitis B Viral Suppression

🧬
Step 1: Hepatocyte Entry Drugs are absorbed into liver cells and phosphorylated into active triphosphate forms.
⛓️
Step 2: Polymerase Inhibition Competes with natural nucleotides to block HBV reverse transcriptase.
📉
Step 3: Viral Load Drop Significantly reduces circulating serum HBV DNA and halts progressive liver inflammation.
✨
Step 4: Long-Term Remission Prevents hepatic fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.T. L.A.P." (Hepatitis B Medications)

Entecavir • Tenofovir (TDF/TAF) • Lamivudine • Adefovir • Peginterferon.

Classification: Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) for Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) Infections

Advanced direct-acting antiviral (DAA) drug classes engineered to completely cure chronic Hepatitis C by targeting specific viral non-structural proteins.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Molecular MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Sofosbuvir MOA: NS5B RNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Clinical Uses: Chronic Hepatitis C virus infection across genotypes 1 through 6 in combination therapy. Specific ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, insomnia; severe symptomatic bradycardia if co-administered with amiodarone.
Ledipasvir / Velpatasvir / Pibrentasvir MOA: NS5A Replication Complex Inhibitors
Clinical Uses: Chronic HCV eradication regimens combined with polymerase or protease inhibitors. Specific ADRs: Fatigue, headache, diarrhea, nausea, asthenia.
Glecaprevir / Voxilaprevir MOA: NS3/4A Protease Inhibitors
Clinical Uses: Pangenotypic chronic Hepatitis C treatment, including direct-acting antiviral failures. Specific ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, transient elevations in serum bilirubin.
Ribavirin MOA: Guanosine Analog Co-Therapy
Clinical Uses: Combination therapy for difficult-to-treat chronic HCV or viral hemorrhagic fevers. Specific ADRs: Severe hemolytic anemia, fatigue, headache, rash, cough, teratogenicity (absolute contraindication in pregnancy for both men and women).

⚡ Action Flow: Achieving Permanent Hepatitis C Cure

🎯
Step 1: Multi-Target Attack Combines NS5B, NS5A, and Protease inhibitors to block viral replication pathways.
🛑
Step 2: Chain Termination Stops HCV RNA synthesis and disrupts core structural assembly inside liver cells.
📉
Step 3: Rapid Clearance Clears HCV RNA from the bloodstream within weeks of starting therapy.
✨
Step 4: Sustained Virologic Response (SVR12) Achieves a permanent, lifelong virologic cure for Hepatitis C.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.O.F. + A.S.V.I.R. / P.R.E.V.I.R." (HCV DAAs)

Sofosbuvir paired with DAAs ending in -asvir (NS5A inhibitors) and -previr (Protease inhibitors).

Classification: Drugs for Herpesvirus and Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Nucleoside and nucleotide analogs engineered to treat Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV 1 & 2), Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV), and Cytomegalovirus (CMV).

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Molecular MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir MOA: Viral DNA Polymerase Inhibitors
Clinical Uses: Herpes simplex encephalitis, genital herpes, varicella-zoster (shingles), chickenpox. Specific ADRs: Phlebitis and local irritation (IV injection), headache, nausea, acute kidney injury from crystalline nephropathy (prevented with adequate IV hydration).
Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir MOA: CMV DNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Clinical Uses: Treatment and prevention of Cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis and systemic disease in immunocompromised patients. Specific ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), fever, rash, diarrhea, confusion.
Cidofovir MOA: Nucleotide Analog (CMV/HSV)
Clinical Uses: CMV retinitis in patients with AIDS (especially ganciclovir-resistant strains). Specific ADRs: Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity (requires concurrent probenecid and saline hydration), proteinuria, neutropenia, metabolic acidosis.
Foscarnet MOA: Pyrophosphate Analog
Clinical Uses: CMV retinitis and acyclovir-resistant HSV or VZV infections. Specific ADRs: Severe nephrotoxicity, severe electrolyte abnormalities (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia), seizures, painful penile ulcerations.

⚡ Action Flow: Herpes & CMV Chain Termination

🔑
Step 1: Selective Activation Viral or cellular enzymes phosphorylate the drug inside infected cells.
🧱
Step 2: DNA Incorporation Inserts fraudulent nucleosides into the viral DNA replication fork.
🛑
Step 3: Synthesis Halt Halts viral replication, reducing lesion duration and nerve pain.
✨
Step 4: Latent Outbreak Control Suppresses recurrent flare-ups and severe systemic CMV complications.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.V.G.C.F."

Acyclovir • Valacyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet.

Classification: HIV Antiretrovirals (NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & Entry Inhibitors)

Classes targeting early and late stages of the HIV life cycle, including reverse transcriptase non-nucleoside binding, polyprotein cleavage, and viral-cell entry blocking.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Molecular MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Efavirenz / Rilpivirine / Doravirine MOA: Non-Nucleoside RT Inhibitors (NNRTIs)
Clinical Uses: Combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for HIV-1 infection. Specific ADRs: Efavirenz: Central nervous system toxicity (vivid dreams, dizziness, depression, insomnia, rash). Rilpivirine: Depression, insomnia, prolonged QTc interval at high doses.
Darunavir / Atazanavir / Lopinavir MOA: Protease Inhibitors (PIs)
Clinical Uses: Treatment-naive and treatment-experienced HIV-1 infection regimens. Specific ADRs: Metabolic syndrome (hyperlipidemia, hyperglycemia, insulin resistance), lipodystrophy (fat redistribution), GI intolerance (nausea, diarrhea), hyperbilirubinemia (Atazanavir).
Maraviroc MOA: CCR5 Antagonist (Entry Inhibitor)
Clinical Uses: Treatment-experienced adults with R5-tropic HIV-1 infections. Specific ADRs: Hepatotoxicity (preceded by systemic allergic reactions like pruritic rash), cough, musculoskeletal symptoms, orthostatic hypotension.
Enfuvirtide MOA: Fusion Inhibitor
Clinical Uses: Salvage therapy for treatment-experienced HIV patients with resistant strains. Specific ADRs: Painful subcutaneous injection site reactions (nodules, erythema, cysts), increased risk of bacterial pneumonia, hypersensitivity reactions.

⚡ Action Flow: Multi-Target HIV Blockade

🚪
Step 1: Entry & Coreceptor Block Maraviroc and Enfuvirtide stop HIV from attaching or fusing to CD4 cells.
⚙️
Step 2: Reverse Transcription Halt NNRTIs disable reverse transcriptase enzyme from building viral DNA.
✂️
Step 3: Cleavage Prevention Protease inhibitors block structural protein maturation.
✨
Step 4: Viral Suppression Preserves immune function and maintains undetectable viral loads.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.D.A.M." (Entry & NNRTI Classes)

Efavirenz • Darunavir • Atazanavir • Maraviroc.

Classification: HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

The preferred frontline anchor class in modern antiretroviral therapy regimens due to rapid viral load suppression and high genetic barrier to resistance.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Molecular MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Dolutegravir / Bictegravir MOA: Second-Generation INSTIs
Clinical Uses: Preferred frontline anchor drugs for initial HIV-1 infection treatment regimens. Specific ADRs: Insomnia, headache, weight gain, nausea, rare hypersensitivity reactions and mild benign increases in serum creatinine (due to tubular secretion blocking, not kidney injury).
Raltegravir MOA: First-Generation INSTI
Clinical Uses: HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral agents. Specific ADRs: Diarrhea, nausea, headache, fatigue, rare severe skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), creatine kinase elevations and myopathy.
Cabotegravir MOA: Long-Acting INSTI
Clinical Uses: Long-acting maintenance HIV treatment (monthly/bimonthly IM injection) and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP). Specific ADRs: Injection site pain/nodules, pyrexia, fatigue, headache, musculoskeletal pain, hepatotoxicity.

⚡ Action Flow: Blocking Provirus Integration

🧬
Step 1: Viral DNA Entry Transcribed viral DNA migrates into the host cell nucleus.
🔗
Step 2: INSTI Binding Inhibitor binds to integrase enzyme and chelates active metal ions.
❌
Step 3: Strand Transfer Block Stops covalent insertion of viral DNA into human host DNA.
✨
Step 4: Permanent Halt Degrades unintegrated viral DNA and halts permanent infection.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir"

Bictegravir • Cabotegravir • Dolutegravir • Raltegravir.

Classification: Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Pharmacological boosting agents utilized to inhibit hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes and amplify co-administered antiretroviral drug exposures.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Molecular MOA, Uses & Specific ADRs

Ritonavir MOA: Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitor
Clinical Uses: Pharmacokinetic booster for protease inhibitors in HIV therapy. Specific ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, hyperlipidemia, paresthesias, elevated transaminases, numerous severe drug-drug interactions.
Cobicistat MOA: Selective CYP3A Enhancer
Clinical Uses: Pharmacokinetic booster for elvitegravir, darunavir, and atazanavir in fixed-dose combinations. Specific ADRs: Nausea, jaundice, mild serum creatinine increases due to blocked tubular secretion without lowering glomerular filtration rate.

⚡ Action Flow: Enhancing Therapeutic Half-Life

💊
Step 1: Combined Dosing Booster is co-formulated with protease or integrase inhibitors.
🛡️
Step 2: Hepatic Enzyme Blockade Inhibits CYP3A metabolism in liver and intestinal walls.
📈
Step 3: Elevated Plasma Levels Maintains peak therapeutic drug concentrations for longer intervals.
✨
Step 4: Once-Daily Schedule Reduces pill burden and supports high patient adherence.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C. Boosters"

Ritonavir • Cobicistat (The two pharmacokinetic enhancers used in clinical practice).

Classification: HIV Fixed-Dose Combinations (Single-Tablet Regimens)

Advanced single-tablet regimens (STRs) combining multiple classes of antiretrovirals into one daily pill to optimize patient compliance and viral suppression.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory, Component Breakdown, Uses & Specific ADRs

Biktarvy Components: Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / TAF
Clinical Uses: Premier frontline single-tablet regimen for treatment-naive and virologically suppressed adults with HIV-1. Specific ADRs: Diarrhea, nausea, headache, fatigue; minimal renal and bone toxicity profile compared to older TDF regimens.
Triumeq Components: Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine
Clinical Uses: Complete single-tablet regimen for HIV-1 infection (requires HLA-B*5701 negative test). Specific ADRs: Insomnia, headache, nausea, hypersensitivity reaction related to abacavir (fever, rash, GI distress, respiratory symptoms which can be fatal if rechallenged).
Symtuza Components: Darunavir / Cobicistat / Emtricitabine / TAF
Clinical Uses: Protease inhibitor-based complete single-tablet regimen for robust viral control. Specific ADRs: Diarrhea, rash, nausea, headache, hyperlipidemia, mild insulin resistance.
Dovato Components: Dolutegravir / Lamivudine
Clinical Uses: Two-drug single-tablet regimen for treatment-naive HIV-1 infected adults without baseline resistance. Specific ADRs: Headache, diarrhea, nausea, insomnia, fatigue.

⚡ Action Flow: Single-Tablet Regimen Synergy

📋
Step 1: Multi-Drug Synergy Combines 2 or 3 distinct antiviral classes into one convenient pill.
🎯
Step 2: Dual & Triple Attacks Blocks multiple viral replication steps simultaneously (integrase + reverse transcriptase).
🤝
Step 3: Maximum Adherence Simplifies daily schedules, preventing missed doses and drug resistance mutations.
✨
Step 4: Long-Term Suppression Sustains undetectable viral loads and normalizes patient life expectancy.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.T.R."

Single Tablet Regimens maximize adherence and prevent therapeutic failure.

🚫 Clinical Warnings & Nursing Interventions

Always screen patients for baseline hepatitis B coinfection, renal function (eGFR), and bone mineral density before starting tenofovir-containing regimens. Perform HLA-B*5701 genetic testing prior to prescribing abacavir to prevent fatal hypersensitivity reactions.
