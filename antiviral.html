Classification: Drugs for Respiratory Virus Infections

Detailed pharmacological breakdown of antiviral agents targeting influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and coronavirus infections.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: Neuraminidase Inhibitor Action (Oseltamivir)
1
Viral Budding
New influenza virions assemble on host cell surface.
2
Neuraminidase Blocked IMP FOR EXAM
Drug blocks cleavage of sialic acid residues.
3
Virions Trapped
Virions cannot release; spread across lungs is halted.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Neuraminidase Inhibitors
Oseltamivir Zanamivir Peramivir
MOA: Competitively and selectively bind to the active site of viral neuraminidase enzymes on Influenza A and B viruses. By inhibiting neuraminidase, they prevent the cleavage of sialic acid residues on host cells, trapping newly synthesized virions on the cell membrane and preventing viral spread. IMP FOR EXAM
Uses: Treatment and chemoprophylaxis of acute uncomplicated Influenza A and B (must start within 48 hours of symptom onset).
ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, headache, psychiatric disturbances (delirium/hallucinations), and bronchospasm (specifically with inhaled zanamivir in asthma patients).
Cap-Dependent Endonuclease Inhibitor
Baloxavir Marboxil
MOA: A prodrug converted to baloxavir, which selectively inhibits cap-dependent endonuclease (a key subunit of viral RNA polymerase). This halts viral messenger RNA (mRNA) transcription, blocking viral replication.
Uses: Single-dose oral treatment of acute uncomplicated influenza in patients aged 12 years and older.
ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, nasopharyngitis, and headache.
Viral Uncoating Blockers (M2 Channel Blockers)
Amantadine Rimantadine
MOA: Block the viral M2 proton ion channel proteins in Influenza A. By blocking proton influx into the virion during endocytosis, they prevent acidification needed for viral uncoating, halting the release of viral RNA into the host cytoplasm.
Uses: Historical treatment of Influenza A (largely abandoned today due to widespread viral resistance). IMP FOR EXAM
ADRs: Central nervous system toxicity (nervousness, anxiety, insomnia, dizziness) and peripheral anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, blurred vision).
RSV Fusion Inhibitor (Monoclonal Antibody)
Palivizumab
MOA: A humanized monoclonal antibody (IgG1) that specifically targets the F (fusion) protein of Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV). This neutralizes the virus and blocks entry/fusion into host respiratory epithelial cells.
Uses: Prevention of serious lower respiratory tract RSV disease in high-risk premature infants and neonates.
ADRs: Injection site reactions, upper respiratory tract infections, fever, and skin rashes.
RNA-Dependent RNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Remdesivir
MOA: An adenosine nucleotide analog prodrug metabolized intracellularly into an active triphosphate metabolite. It competitively inhibits viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp), causing premature RNA chain termination and halting viral replication.
Uses: Treatment of COVID-19 in hospitalized patients requiring supplemental oxygen therapy.
ADRs: Elevated liver transaminases (ALT/AST), nausea, and infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.B.A.P.R." (Respiratory Antivirals)

Oseltamivir • Baloxavir • Amantadine • Palivizumab • Remdesivir

Classification: HIV Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs & NtRTIs)

Nucleoside and Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs/NtRTIs) serve as the backbone of antiretroviral therapy (ART). They act as false building blocks that cause premature chain termination.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: NRTI Chain Termination Mechanism
1
Triple Phosphorylation
Drug is activated by host cellular enzymes into triphosphate form.
2
False Nucleotide Added IMP FOR EXAM
Reverse transcriptase incorporates drug into growing viral DNA strand.
3
Chain Termination
Lacks 3'-OH group; no more nucleotides can attach. DNA stops growing.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Thymidine Nucleoside Analog (NRTI)
Zidovudine (AZT / ZDV)
MOA: Phosphorylated intracellularly into active triphosphate form. Competes with natural thymidine triphosphate for incorporation into viral DNA by HIV reverse transcriptase, causing immediate chain termination due to lack of a 3'-OH group.
Uses: HIV-1 treatment regimens and prevention of perinatal (mother-to-child) HIV transmission during labor and delivery.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (macrocytic anemia, neutropenia), headache, myopathy, and lactic acidosis.
Cytidine Nucleoside Analog (NRTI)
Lamivudine (3TC)
MOA: Intracellularly phosphorylated to active triphosphate form, competing with natural deoxycytidine triphosphate to cause viral DNA chain termination by HIV reverse transcriptase and HBV polymerase.
Uses: Combination HIV regimens and dual-activity management of chronic Hepatitis B virus (HBV).
ADRs: Favorable safety profile; occasional headache, fatigue, nausea, and rare pancreatitis.
Fluorinated Cytidine Analog (NRTI)
Emtricitabine (FTC)
MOA: Fluorinated cytidine analog that terminates viral DNA synthesis. Structurally similar to lamivudine but possesses a longer intracellular half-life.
Uses: Frontline HIV combination treatment regimens and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP).
ADRs: Headache, diarrhea, nausea, and benign hyperpigmentation on palms and soles.
Guanosine Nucleoside Analog (NRTI)
Abacavir (ABC)
MOA: Carbocyclic guanosine analog converted to carbovir triphosphate, which competitively inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase and terminates viral DNA elongation.
Uses: Frontline ART backbone (mandates pre-screening for HLA-B*5701 allele). IMP FOR EXAM
ADRs: Potentially fatal systemic hypersensitivity reaction (fever, rash, GI distress) in patients carrying the HLA-B*5701 allele.
Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitor (NtRTI)
Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF)
MOA: Acyclic nucleoside phosphonate (adenosine monophosphate analog). Requires only two intracellular phosphorylation steps to reach active diphosphate form to terminate HIV and HBV DNA chains.
Uses: HIV treatment, PrEP protocols, and chronic Hepatitis B therapy.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity, proximal renal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi syndrome), and reduction in bone mineral density. IMP FOR EXAM
Targeted Nucleotide Prodrug (NtRTI)
Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF)
MOA: Targeted prodrug of tenofovir with high plasma stability. Delivers active drug directly into lymphoid cells and hepatocytes at lower doses, minimizing kidney and bone exposure.
Uses: HIV treatment and PrEP with significantly reduced renal and bone toxicities compared to TDF.
ADRs: Mild headache, fatigue, nausea; significantly lower rates of renal impairment and bone loss than TDF.
Older Thymidine Analog (NRTI)
Stavudine (d4T)
MOA: Phosphorylated intracellularly to stavudine triphosphate, inhibiting reverse transcriptase and terminating DNA chains.
Uses: Historical HIV treatment (discontinued in modern practice due to severe metabolic toxicity).
ADRs: Severe peripheral neuropathy, progressive lipoatrophy (facial/limb fat loss), lactic acidosis, and pancreatitis.
Older Purine Analog (NRTI)
Didanosine (ddI)
MOA: Inosine derivative converted to dideoxyadenosine triphosphate, causing viral DNA chain termination.
Uses: Historical alternative NRTI used in older combination antiretroviral regimens.
ADRs: Dose-dependent pancreatitis, painful peripheral neuropathy, diarrhea, and lactic acidosis.

💡 Mnemonic: "Z.L.E.A.T.S.D." (HIV NRTIs & NtRTIs)

Zidovudine • Lamivudine • Emtricitabine • Abacavir • Tenofovir (TDF/TAF) • Stavudine • Didanosine

Classification: Drugs for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Infections

Potent nucleoside/nucleotide analogs and immunomodulators dedicated to suppressing viral replication in chronic Hepatitis B.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Guanosine Nucleoside Analog
Entecavir
MOA: Phosphorylated to active entecavir triphosphate. Inhibits all 3 functions of HBV polymerase: base priming, negative-strand DNA synthesis, and positive-strand DNA elongation.
Uses: First-line therapy for chronic Hepatitis B with active viral replication and elevated liver enzymes.
ADRs: Headache, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, and rare lactic acidosis with severe hepatomegaly.
Thymidine L-Nucleoside Analog
Telbivudine
MOA: Phosphorylated to active 5'-triphosphate, selectively inhibiting HBV DNA polymerase by competing with natural thymidine triphosphate.
Uses: Treatment of chronic Hepatitis B in adult patients with high viral loads.
ADRs: Elevated serum creatine kinase (CK) levels, skeletal myopathy, fatigue, headache, and dizziness.
Adenosine Nucleotide Diester Prodrug
Adefovir Dipivoxil
MOA: Diester prodrug of adefovir converted to adefovir diphosphate. Competitively inhibits HBV DNA polymerase and causes chain termination.
Uses: Chronic Hepatitis B treatment, including lamivudine-resistant strains.
ADRs: Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity, proximal tubular dysfunction with hypophosphatemia, asthenia, and headache.
Pegylated Immunomodulator Cytokine
Peginterferon Alfa-2a
MOA: Recombinant interferon conjugated with polyethylene glycol to prolong half-life. Activates JAK-STAT intracellular signaling to induce antiviral genes and enhance immune response.
Uses: Finite-duration therapy for HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative chronic Hepatitis B.
ADRs: Flu-like symptoms (fever, chills), severe depression/suicidal ideation, bone marrow suppression, and thyroid dysfunction.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.T.A.P." (HBV Agents)

Entecavir • Telbivudine • Adefovir • Peginterferon

Classification: Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) for Hepatitis C (HCV)

Target non-structural (NS) viral proteins to achieve complete cure (SVR) without interferon.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

NS5B Polymerase Inhibitor (Nucleotide)
Sofosbuvir
MOA: Uridine nucleotide analog prodrug that incorporates into HCV NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, causing RNA chain termination.
Uses: Pan-genotypic curative regimens for chronic HCV genotypes 1 through 6.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache; severe fatal symptomatic bradycardia if co-administered with amiodarone!
NS5A Protein Replication Complex Inhibitors
Ledipasvir Velpatasvir Pibrentasvir Daclatasvir
MOA: Inhibit HCV NS5A phosphoprotein, which is essential for viral RNA replication complex assembly and virion secretion.
Uses: Pan-genotypic direct-acting combination therapy for chronic HCV eradication.
ADRs: Mild headache, fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, and dizziness.
NS3/4A Protease Inhibitors
Glecaprevir Voxilaprevir Simeprevir Grazoprevir
MOA: Inhibit HCV NS3/4A serine protease enzyme, preventing proteolytic cleavage of viral polyprotein precursor into functional proteins.
Uses: Combination treatment for chronic HCV, including cases with prior DAA failure.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, photosensitivity (simeprevir), and transient bilirubin elevation.
Guanosine Nucleoside Analog
Ribavirin
MOA: Inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase, depleting GTP pools and inducing lethal mutations in viral RNA synthesis.
Uses: Combination therapy for complex chronic HCV or severe pediatric RSV bronchiolitis.
ADRs: Dose-dependent hemolytic anemia and severe teratogenicity (Pregnancy Category X!).

💡 Mnemonic: "S.L.G.R." (HCV DAAs)

Sofosbuvir • Ledipasvir • Glecaprevir • Ribavirin

Classification: Drugs for Herpesvirus & Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Nucleoside and nucleotide analogs targeting Herpes Simplex (HSV), Varicella-Zoster (VZV), and Cytomegalovirus (CMV).

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

HSV/VZV Guanosine Nucleoside Analogs
Acyclovir Valacyclovir Famciclovir
MOA: Require viral thymidine kinase for initial monophosphorylation, followed by cellular kinases. Active triphosphate form competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates viral DNA. IMP FOR EXAM
Uses: Genital herpes, shingles (VZV), chickenpox, and HSV encephalitis.
ADRs: Nausea, crystalline nephropathy (drug precipitation in renal tubules during rapid IV infusion; prevent with IV hydration!), and neurotoxicity.
CMV Acyclic Guanosine Analog
Ganciclovir Valganciclovir
MOA: Phosphorylated initially by viral UL97 kinase in CMV, then cellular kinases. Active triphosphate inhibits CMV DNA polymerase and halts elongation.
Uses: Treatment and secondary prevention of CMV retinitis and invasive systemic disease in immunocompromised patients.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia) and fever.
CMV Nucleotide Analog (Bypasses Viral Kinase)
Cidofovir
MOA: Acyclic nucleotide monophosphate that bypasses viral kinase activation. Active against acyclovir-resistant HSV and ganciclovir-resistant CMV by directly inhibiting viral DNA polymerase.
Uses: Treatment of resistant CMV retinitis in patients with AIDS.
ADRs: Severe dose-dependent nephrotoxicity (requires co-administration with oral probenecid and IV normal saline hydration!).
Non-Inorganic Pyrophosphate Analog
Foscarnet
MOA: Organic pyrophosphate analog that directly binds the pyrophosphate site of viral DNA polymerase without requiring phosphorylation activation.
Uses: Acyclovir-resistant HSV/VZV and ganciclovir-resistant CMV infections.
ADRs: Severe nephrotoxicity, electrolyte wasting (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia), and seizures.
CMV Terminase Complex Inhibitor
Letermovir
MOA: Inhibits the viral UL56 subunit of the CMV terminase complex, blocking viral DNA maturation and packaging into capsids.
Uses: Prophylaxis of CMV infection in adult allogeneic stem cell transplant recipients.
ADRs: Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, peripheral edema, headache, and fatigue.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.G.C.F.L." (Herpes & CMV)

Acyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet • Letermovir

Classification: HIV Antiretrovirals (NNRTIs, PIs & Entry/Capsid Inhibitors)

Expanded non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase, viral protease, entry receptor, and capsid assembly blockers.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NNRTIs)
Efavirenz Rilpivirine Doravirine Nevirapine Etravirine
MOA: Bind directly and non-competitively to an allosteric pocket near the active catalytic site of HIV-1 reverse transcriptase, inducing a conformational change that stops polymerization.
Uses: Combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for HIV-1 infection.
ADRs: CNS disturbances (vivid dreams, insomnia with efavirenz), severe skin rash (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), and hepatotoxicity (nevirapine).
HIV Protease Inhibitors (PIs)
Darunavir Atazanavir Lopinavir Indinavir Saquinavir Tipranavir
MOA: Competitively inhibit HIV-1 protease, preventing cleavage of Gag-Pol polyprotein precursors into functional structural proteins, producing immature, non-infectious virions.
Uses: Treatment-naive and experienced HIV-1 management.
ADRs: Metabolic complications (dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, lipodystrophy), hyperbilirubinemia (atazanavir), and kidney stones (indinavir).
CCR5 Coreceptor Antagonist
Maraviroc
MOA: Binds human CCR5 coreceptor on CD4 T-cells, preventing binding interaction with viral gp120 envelope protein to block viral entry.
Uses: Treatment-experienced patients infected exclusively with CCR5-tropic HIV-1 strains.
ADRs: Hepatotoxicity (preceded by severe allergic rash), upper respiratory infections, and postural hypotension.
gp41 Fusion Inhibitor
Enfuvirtide
MOA: Synthetic 36-amino acid peptide that binds the HR1 region of viral gp41 protein, blocking the membrane fusion process.
Uses: Salvage therapy for heavily treatment-experienced patients with multidrug-resistant HIV.
ADRs: Painful subcutaneous injection site nodules, erythema, and increased risk of bacterial pneumonia.
Novel Attachment, Post-Attachment & Capsid Inhibitors
Fostemsavir Ibalizumab Lenacapavir
MOA: Fostemsavir binds gp120 to block attachment; Ibalizumab is a monoclonal antibody blocking CD4 domain 2; Lenacapavir inhibits viral p24 capsid assembly, disassembly, and nuclear transport.
Uses: Multidrug-resistant advanced HIV-1 salvage regimens when other choices fail.
ADRs: Nausea, diarrhea, elevated liver enzymes, and injection site nodules (lenacapavir).

💡 Mnemonic: "E.D.M.E.F."

Efavirenz • Darunavir • Maraviroc • Enfuvirtide • Fostemsavir

Classification: HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

Preferred frontline anchor class in modern antiretroviral therapy (ART) regimens.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: Integrase Inhibitor Action (INSTIs)
1
Viral cDNA Created
Reverse transcriptase synthesizes double-stranded viral DNA.
2
Integrase Chelated IMP FOR EXAM
INSTI binds magnesium ions at the active catalytic core.
3
Integration Blocked
Viral DNA cannot insert into human chromosomes.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Second-Generation INSTIs
Dolutegravir Bictegravir
MOA: Bind magnesium ions in the catalytic core of HIV integrase, blocking strand transfer of viral cDNA into host chromosomal DNA. High barrier to resistance.
Uses: Preferred frontline anchor agents for initial and maintenance treatment of HIV-1.
ADRs: Insomnia, headache, weight gain, and mild nausea.
First-Generation INSTI
Raltegravir
MOA: Chelates divalent metal ions in the integrase active site, preventing covalent insertion of viral cDNA into host DNA.
Uses: Treatment of HIV-1 in combination with other antiretroviral agents.
ADRs: Insomnia, nausea, headache, CPK enzyme elevation, and rare severe hypersensitivity (DRESS syndrome).
Pharmacokinetically Boosted INSTI
Elvitegravir
MOA: Chelates metal ions at the integrase active site to block strand transfer (requires pharmacokinetic boosting with cobicistat or ritonavir).
Uses: Combined single-tablet regimens for HIV-1 management.
ADRs: Nausea, diarrhea, headache, and insomnia.
Long-Acting Injectable INSTI
Cabotegravir
MOA: Long-acting integrase inhibitor formulated as a nanosuspension that blocks viral cDNA strand transfer into host chromatin.
Uses: Monthly or bi-monthly IM injectable regimens for HIV treatment and PrEP.
ADRs: Injection site reactions (pain, swelling), fever, fatigue, and headache.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir" (INSTIs)

Bictegravir • Cabotegravir • Dolutegravir • Elvitegravir • Raltegravir

Classification: Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Agents used to inhibit hepatic CYP3A4 enzymes and boost plasma levels of co-administered antiretroviral drugs.

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

CYP3A4 / P-gp Inhibitor Booster
Ritonavir
MOA: Potent inhibitor of hepatic CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein. Used at low sub-therapeutic doses strictly to inhibit protease inhibitor metabolism and raise drug plasma levels.
Uses: Low-dose pharmacokinetic booster for HIV protease inhibitors.
ADRs: GI distress (nausea, diarrhea), dyslipidemia, and elevated liver transaminases.
Selective CYP3A Inhibitor Booster (No Antiviral Activity)
Cobicistat
MOA: Selective mechanism-based CYP3A inhibitor. Possesses zero intrinsic antiviral activity and functions purely as a PK booster.
Uses: Integrated booster in fixed-dose single-tablet regimens with protease inhibitors or elvitegravir.
ADRs: Benign increase in serum creatinine (blocks tubular secretion without decreasing GFR), nausea, and jaundice.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C. Boosters"

Ritonavir • Cobicistat

Classification: HIV Fixed-Dose Single-Tablet Regimens

Single-tablet complete regimens combining multiple drug classes into one daily pill for maximum adherence.

📂 Regimen Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

INSTI + Dual NRTI Single-Tablet Regimen
Biktarvy (Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / TAF)
MOA: Combines bictegravir (INSTI) with emtricitabine and TAF (NRTIs) to simultaneously block integrase strand transfer and terminate viral DNA synthesis.
Uses: Frontline single-tablet regimen for initial HIV-1 treatment in treatment-naive adults.
ADRs: Diarrhea, nausea, headache; excellent renal and bone safety profile.
INSTI + Dual NRTI Single-Tablet Regimen
Triumeq (Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine)
MOA: Triple-drug blockade combining dolutegravir (INSTI) with abacavir and lamivudine (NRTIs) to block integration and reverse transcription.
Uses: Complete single-tablet ART regimen (requires negative HLA-B*5701 test!). IMP FOR EXAM
ADRs: Insomnia, fatigue, headache, and risk of hypersensitivity in HLA-B*5701 positive patients.
Dual NRTI Backbone Regimens
Truvada (Emtricitabine / TDF) Descovy (Emtricitabine / TAF)
MOA: Dual NRTI backbones that competitively inhibit reverse transcriptase and cause viral DNA chain termination.
Uses: Backbone foundation for ART combination therapy and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP).
ADRs: Nausea, headache; Truvada requires kidney/bone density monitoring due to TDF.
Boosted Protease Inhibitor + Dual NRTI Regimen
Symtuza (Darunavir / Cobicistat / Emtricitabine / TAF)
MOA: Combines boosted darunavir (PI + booster) with emtricitabine and TAF (NRTIs) to block protease cleavage and reverse transcription.
Uses: Complete single-tablet regimen for HIV treatment-naive and experienced patients.
ADRs: Diarrhea, rash, nausea, headache, dyslipidemia, and insulin resistance.

🚫 Clinical Warnings & Nursing Interventions IMP FOR EXAM

• Hepatitis B Screening: Always screen for HBV before starting tenofovir-containing regimens, as stopping tenofovir suddenly can cause severe Hepatitis B flare-ups!
• Genetic Screening: Perform HLA-B*5701 testing before giving Abacavir to prevent fatal hypersensitivity reactions.
• Renal Health: Monitor eGFR and urinalysis closely for patients on TDF (Truvada).
