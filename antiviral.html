Classification: Drugs for Respiratory Virus Infections

Detailed pharmacological breakdown of antiviral agents targeting influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and severe acute respiratory infections.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Molecular MOA

Oseltamivir / Zanamivir / Peramivir MOA: Neuraminidase Inhibitors Inhibits viral neuraminidase enzyme on influenza A and B surfaces, preventing newly formed viral progeny from releasing and spreading across respiratory tracts.
Baloxavir Marboxil MOA: Cap-Dependent Endonuclease Inhibitor Selectively blocks the endonuclease activity of influenza polymerase acidic protein, shutting down viral mRNA transcription right at initiation.
Palivizumab MOA: Monoclonal Antibody Fusion Blocker Binds to the F protein epitope on RSV, neutralizing viral entry into respiratory epithelial cells of high-risk infants.
Remdesivir MOA: RNA Polymerase Inhibitor Nucleotide prodrug analog that prematurely terminates viral RNA replication chains in coronavirus and severe respiratory viral infections.
Nirmatrelvir / Ritonavir (Paxlovid) MOA: Viral Protease Inhibitor + Booster Blocks coronavirus main protease (Mpro) to prevent polyprotein cleavage, boosted by ritonavir to sustain therapeutic blood concentrations.
Amantadine / Rimantadine MOA: M2 Ion Channel Blockers Blocks the viral M2 proton ion channel of Influenza A, preventing uncoating inside host cells (largely historical due to high viral resistance).

⚡ Action Flow: Stopping Respiratory Pathogen Proliferation

🦠
Step 1: Inhalation & Entry Viruses invade upper and lower respiratory tract mucosal tissues.
⚙️
Step 2: Targeted Inhibition Drugs block viral uncoating, polymerases, endonucleases, or neuraminidase.
🛡️
Step 3: Replication Arrest Intracellular transcription and viral budding are completely blocked.
✨
Step 4: Clinical Recovery Reduces symptom severity, duration, and hospitalization risks.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.Z.P.B.R.N." (Respiratory Antivirals)

Oseltamivir • Zanamivir • Peramivir • Baloxavir • Remdesivir • Nirmatrelvir.

Classification: Drugs for Hepatic Virus Infections (Hepatitis B & C)

Targeted nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors and immune modulators for chronic Hepatitis B, alongside direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) for curing Hepatitis C.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Molecular MOA

Entecavir / Tenofovir (DF & AF) MOA: HBV Polymerase Inhibitors Competes with natural purine/pyrimidine substrates to inhibit HBV reverse transcriptase and block viral DNA chain elongation.
Lamivudine / Adefovir / Telbivudine MOA: Nucleoside Analogs (HBV) Older oral nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors used to suppress active viral replication in chronic Hepatitis B.
Peginterferon Alfa-2a MOA: Immune Modulator / Cytokine Enhances host cellular immune response and suppresses viral replication in both Hepatitis B and C.
Sofosbuvir MOA: HCV NS5B Polymerase Inhibitor Nucleotide analog that acts as a chain terminator for Hepatitis C RNA polymerase.
Ledipasvir / Velpatasvir / Glecaprevir / Pibrentasvir MOA: HCV NS5A / Protease Inhibitors Inhibits viral phosphoprotein assembly (NS5A) and core serine proteases to halt HCV maturation and replication.
Ribavirin MOA: Broad-Spectrum Guanosine Analog Enhances mutation rates in viral RNA and inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase in combination HCV regimens.

⚡ Action Flow: Hepatic Suppression & HCV Cure

🧬
Step 1: Hepatocyte Delivery Drugs concentrate within liver tissue and infected hepatocytes.
⛓️
Step 2: Enzyme Blockade Stops viral DNA polymerase or RNA polymerase chain elongation.
📉
Step 3: Viral Eradication Dramatically drops viral load, preventing cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.
✨
Step 4: Sustained Virologic Response (SVR) Achieves permanent cure for Hepatitis C and long-term remission for Hepatitis B.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.O.F. + D.A.A." for Hepatitis C Cure

Sofosbuvir combined with Direct-acting Antiviral agents ending in -asvir and -previr.

Classification: Drugs for Herpesvirus and Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Nucleoside and nucleotide analogs engineered to treat Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV 1 & 2), Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV), and Cytomegalovirus (CMV).

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Molecular MOA

Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir MOA: Viral DNA Polymerase Inhibitors Requires viral thymidine kinase for monophosphorylation inside infected cells; incorporates into viral DNA to cause chain termination.
Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir MOA: CMV DNA Polymerase Inhibitor Specialized nucleoside analog effective against cytomegalovirus infections, particularly in immunocompromised patients.
Cidofovir MOA: Nucleotide Analog (CMV/HSV) Does not require viral kinase activation; inhibits viral DNA polymerase directly for resistant viral strains.
Foscarnet MOA: Pyrophosphate Analog Directly inhibits pyrophosphate binding site on viral DNA polymerase without requiring phosphorylation.
Docosanol / Penciclovir MOA: Topical Fusion / Replication Blockers Topical agents that inhibit host cell plasma membrane fusion, preventing viral entry in herpes labialis (cold sores).

⚡ Action Flow: Herpes & CMV Chain Termination

🔑
Step 1: Selective Activation Viral or cellular enzymes phosphorylate the drug inside infected cells.
🧱
Step 2: DNA Incorporation Inserts fraudulent nucleosides into the viral DNA replication fork.
🛑
Step 3: Synthesis Halt Halts viral replication, reducing lesion duration and nerve pain.
✨
Step 4: Latent Outbreak Control Suppresses recurrent flare-ups and severe systemic CMV complications.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.V.G.C.F."

Acyclovir • Valacyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet.

Classification: HIV Antiretrovirals (NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & Entry Inhibitors)

Classes targeting early and late stages of the HIV life cycle, including reverse transcriptase non-nucleoside binding, polyprotein cleavage, and viral-cell entry blocking.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Molecular MOA

Efavirenz / Rilpivirine / Doravirine MOA: Non-Nucleoside RT Inhibitors (NNRTIs) Binds directly to a hydrophobic pocket on HIV reverse transcriptase, inducing conformational change and halting enzyme function.
Etravirine / Nevirapine MOA: Second-Generation NNRTIs Overcomes common mutations to inhibit reverse transcription in treatment-experienced HIV patients.
Darunavir / Atazanavir / Lopinavir MOA: Protease Inhibitors (PIs) Binds to the active site of HIV protease, preventing cleavage of viral gag-pol polyproteins into mature infectious virions.
Tipranavir / Saquinavir / Fosamprenavir MOA: Alternative Protease Inhibitors Potent PIs reserved for multi-drug resistant HIV strains when boosted with ritonavir.
Maraviroc MOA: CCR5 Antagonist (Entry Inhibitor) Blocks human host cell CCR5 coreceptors, preventing R5-tropic HIV from attaching and entering CD4 T-cells.
Enfuvirtide / Ibalizumab / Fostemsavir MOA: Fusion & Attachment Inhibitors Prevents gp41 conformational changes (Enfuvirtide) or blocks CD4 receptors (Ibalizumab/Fostemsavir) to stop viral entry.

⚡ Action Flow: Multi-Target HIV Blockade

🚪
Step 1: Entry & Coreceptor Block Maraviroc and Enfuvirtide stop HIV from attaching or fusing to CD4 cells.
⚙️
Step 2: Reverse Transcription Halt NNRTIs disable reverse transcriptase enzyme from building viral DNA.
✂️
Step 3: Cleavage Prevention Protease inhibitors block structural protein maturation.
✨
Step 4: Viral Suppression Preserves immune function and maintains undetectable viral loads.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.D.A.M." (Entry & NNRTI Classes)

Efavirenz • Darunavir • Atazanavir • Maraviroc.

Classification: HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

The preferred frontline anchor class in modern antiretroviral therapy regimens due to rapid viral load suppression and high genetic barrier to resistance.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Molecular MOA

Dolutegravir MOA: Second-Generation INSTI Binds tightly to HIV integrase active site metals, blocking strand transfer of viral DNA into host chromosomes with a very high resistance barrier.
Bictegravir MOA: Potent Frontline INSTI Advanced integrase inhibitor featured in leading once-daily single-tablet regimens.
Raltegravir MOA: First-Generation INSTI Inhibits insertion of viral genetic material into human DNA; twice-daily dosing profile.
Elvitegravir MOA: Pharmacokinetically Boosted INSTI Requires boosting with cobicistat to maintain therapeutic blood levels.
Cabotegravir MOA: Long-Acting INSTI Available in oral and injectable nanosuspension formulations for long-acting HIV treatment and pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP).

⚡ Action Flow: Blocking Provirus Integration

🧬
Step 1: Viral DNA Entry Transcribed viral DNA migrates into the host cell nucleus.
🔗
Step 2: INSTI Binding Inhibitor binds to integrase enzyme and chelates active metal ions.
❌
Step 3: Strand Transfer Block Stops covalent insertion of viral DNA into human host DNA.
✨
Step 4: Permanent Halt Degrades unintegrated viral DNA and halts permanent infection.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir"

Bictegravir • Cabotegravir • Dolutegravir • Elvitegravir • Raltegravir.

Classification: Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Pharmacological boosting agents utilized to inhibit hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes and amplify co-administered antiretroviral drug exposures.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Molecular MOA

Ritonavir MOA: Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitor Although an older protease inhibitor, used at low doses exclusively to inhibit CYP3A4 metabolism and boost half-lives of primary PIs.
Cobicistat MOA: Selective CYP3A Enhancer Mechanistic pharmacokinetic booster with zero intrinsic anti-HIV activity; inhibits CYP3A enzymes to raise levels of elvitegravir and darunavir.

⚡ Action Flow: Enhancing Therapeutic Half-Life

💊
Step 1: Combined Dosing Booster is co-formulated with protease or integrase inhibitors.
🛡️
Step 2: Hepatic Enzyme Blockade Inhibits CYP3A metabolism in liver and intestinal walls.
📈
Step 3: Elevated Plasma Levels Maintains peak therapeutic drug concentrations for longer intervals.
✨
Step 4: Once-Daily Schedule Reduces pill burden and supports high patient adherence.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C. Boosters"

Ritonavir • Cobicistat (The two pharmacokinetic enhancers used in clinical practice).

Classification: HIV Fixed-Dose Combinations (Single-Tablet Regimens)

Advanced single-tablet regimens (STRs) combining multiple classes of antiretrovirals into one daily pill to optimize patient compliance and viral suppression.

📂 Expanded Drug Inventory & Regimen Breakdown

Biktarvy Components: Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / Tenofovir alafenamide Premier frontline single-tablet regimen for treatment-naive adults with high efficacy and minimal toxicity.
Triumeq Components: Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine Potent INSTI-based backbone regimen (requires HLA-B*5701 genetic screening prior to use).
Symtuza Components: Darunavir / Cobicistat / Emtricitabine / Tenofovir alafenamide Protease inhibitor-based complete single-tablet regimen for robust viral control.
Dovato Components: Dolutegravir / Lamivudine Innovative two-drug single-tablet regimen minimizing long-term renal and bone toxicities.
Delstrigo Components: Doravirine / Lamivudine / Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate NNRTI-based modern single-tablet regimen offering favorable lipid profiles.
Atripla Components: Efavirenz / Emtricitabine / Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate Historical classic single-tablet regimen that revolutionized modern HIV therapy.

⚡ Action Flow: Single-Tablet Regimen Synergy

📋
Step 1: Multi-Drug Synergy Combines 2 or 3 distinct antiviral classes into one convenient pill.
🎯
Step 2: Dual & Triple Attacks Blocks multiple viral replication steps simultaneously (integrase + reverse transcriptase).
🤝
Step 3: Maximum Adherence Simplifies daily schedules, preventing missed doses and drug resistance mutations.
✨
Step 4: Long-Term Suppression Sustains undetectable viral loads and normalizes patient life expectancy.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.T.R."

Single Tablet Regimens maximize adherence and prevent therapeutic failure.

🚫 Clinical Warnings & Nursing Interventions

Always screen patients for baseline hepatitis B coinfection, renal function (eGFR), and bone mineral density before starting tenofovir-containing regimens. Perform HLA-B*5701 genetic testing prior to prescribing abacavir to prevent fatal hypersensitivity reactions.
