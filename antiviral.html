1 Respiratory Virus Antivirals (Influenza, RSV, COVID-19)

Respiratory

Target viral entry, uncoating, replication, or release in upper and lower respiratory infections.

Step-by-Step MOA Flowchart: Neuraminidase Inhibitors (Oseltamivir)
1 Viral Replication Influenza viruses multiply inside respiratory epithelial cells.
2 Enzyme Blockade (Target) Drug inhibits viral neuraminidase, preventing the snip of attachment anchors.
3 Virions Trapped New viruses stay glued to host cell walls and cannot infect new tissue.

Prototype Drugs: Oseltamivir (Tamiflu), Zanamivir, Peramivir.

MOA: Competitively inhibits viral neuraminidase enzyme, trapping replicated influenza virions at the host cell surface.

Clinical Uses: Treatment & prophylaxis of Influenza A & B (must be started within 48 hours of symptom onset).

ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, psychiatric neuropsychiatric events (delirium), and bronchospasm (avoid inhaled zanamivir in asthma/COPD).

Prototype Drug: Baloxavir Marboxil.

MOA: Inhibits cap-dependent endonuclease within the viral polymerase complex, halting transcription.

Clinical Uses: Single-dose oral treatment for acute uncomplicated influenza.

ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, and headache.

Prototype Drugs: Amantadine, Rimantadine (M2 blockers); Palivizumab (RSV monoclonal antibody); Remdesivir (COVID-19 RdRp inhibitor).

MOA & Uses: Amantadine blocks viral uncoating (rarely used due to resistance). Palivizumab blocks RSV F-protein in preemies. Remdesivir stops viral RNA replication.

Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) Profile

Indication: Acute Flu A/B within 48h.

Key ADR: GI upset & neuropsychiatric changes.

Nursing Pearl: Take with food to reduce nausea.

Remdesivir Profile

Indication: Hospitalized COVID-19 patients.

Key ADR: Elevated ALT/AST liver enzymes.

Nursing Pearl: Monitor renal and hepatic function.

Mnemonics for Respiratory Antivirals

• Uses Mnemonic ("FLU-DEFEND"): Flue symptoms treated early | Lung protection | Uncoating blocked | Days shortened (within 48h) | Endonuclease inhibited

• ADR Mnemonic ("NAUSEA"): Neuropsychiatric risk | Abdominal upset | Upper airway irritation (Zanamivir) | Seizures (rare) | Emesis | Anxiety

2 HIV Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs vs. NtRTIs)

HIV Core

Fake nucleoside/nucleotide building blocks that incorporate into viral DNA and immediately cause chain termination.

Step-by-Step MOA Flowchart: NRTI & NtRTI Chain Termination
1 Intracellular Activation NRTIs require 3 phosphorylation steps; NtRTIs (Tenofovir) already have 1 phosphate.
2 Fake Incorporation (Target) Reverse transcriptase inserts the drug into the growing viral DNA strand.
3 DNA Termination Lacking the 3'-OH group, no further nucleosides can attach; elongation stops.

Prototype Drugs: Zidovudine (AZT), Lamivudine (3TC), Emtricitabine (FTC), Abacavir (ABC), Tenofovir (TDF / TAF).

MOA: Competitive inhibition of HIV reverse transcriptase and viral DNA chain termination.

Clinical Uses: Cornerstone backbone of combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for HIV-1 and chronic Hepatitis B.

ADRs & Safety: Lactic acidosis with hepatic steatosis, bone marrow suppression (Zidovudine), and Fanconi syndrome / bone loss (Tenofovir TDF).

Abacavir (ABC) Profile

Indication: HIV cART backbone.

Key ADR: Severe hypersensitivity reaction.

Nursing Pearl: Mandatory screening for HLA-B*5701 gene before prescribing!

Tenofovir Disoproxil (TDF) Profile

Indication: HIV & Hepatitis B suppression.

Key ADR: Nephrotoxicity & osteoporosis.

Nursing Pearl: Monitor serum creatinine and BUN regularly.

Mnemonics for NRTIs / NtRTIs

• Uses Mnemonic ("BACKBONE"): Builds cART foundation | Aids viral suppression | Combination therapy | Kills replication | Blocks transcriptase | Overcomes resistance | Nucleoside analogues | Effective immunity

• ADR Mnemonic ("LACTIC"): Lactic acidosis | Anemia (Zidovudine) | Creatinine rise (Tenofovir) | Toxicity to mitochondria | Insomnia | Cardiac/GI upset

3 Hepatitis B & C Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs)

Hepatitis

Target HBV polymerase and HCV non-structural (NS) proteins to achieve permanent virologic cure (SVR).

Step-by-Step MOA Flowchart: HCV Direct-Acting Antiviral Targets
1 NS3/4A Protease (-previr) Blocks viral polyprotein cleavage.
2 NS5A Complex (-asvir) Blocks viral RNA replication complex assembly.
3 NS5B Polymerase (-buvir) Blocks RNA-dependent RNA polymerase chain.

Prototype Drugs: Entecavir, Tenofovir (HBV); Sofosbuvir, Ledipasvir, Glecaprevir (HCV DAAs); Ribavirin.

MOA: Multi-target interruption of viral enzyme synthesis and replication.

Clinical Uses: Chronic Hepatitis B suppression and curative HCV combination therapy.

ADRs: Fatigue, headache, bilirubin spikes, hemolytic anemia (Ribavirin), and severe bradycardia if Sofosbuvir is combined with Amiodarone.

Sofosbuvir Profile

Indication: Pan-genotypic Hepatitis C cure.

Key ADR: Fatigue & headache.

Nursing Pearl: NEVER combine with Amiodarone (fatal bradycardia risk).

Ribavirin Profile

Indication: HCV adjunct & viral infections.

Key ADR: Severe hemolytic anemia & teratogenicity.

Nursing Pearl: Pregnancy Category X (requires 2 forms of birth control).

Mnemonics for Hepatitis DAAs

• Suffix Mnemonic ("P-A-B"): Previr = Protease | Asvir = Assembly (NS5A) | Buvir = Building block (NS5B Polymerase)

• ADR Mnemonic ("HEPA"): Hemolytic anemia (Ribavirin) | Enzymes elevated | Pain/fatigue | Amiodarone cardiac hazard

4 Herpesvirus (HSV/VZV) & Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Drugs

Herpes/CMV

Nucleoside analogs requiring viral or cellular kinase activation to inhibit viral DNA polymerase in HSV, VZV, and CMV.

Step-by-Step MOA Flowchart: Acyclovir Kinase Activation
1 Viral Kinase Entry Acyclovir enters infected cells and is phosphorylated by viral Thymidine Kinase.
2 Triphosphate Conversion (Target) Host cellular enzymes convert it into active acyclovir triphosphate.
3 DNA Polymerase Inhibition Competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase, halting viral replication.

Prototype Drugs: Acyclovir, Valacyclovir (HSV/VZV); Ganciclovir, Valganciclovir (CMV); Foscarnet, Cidofovir (Kinase-independent).

MOA: Selective viral DNA chain termination.

Clinical Uses: Genital herpes, shingles, chickenpox, HSV encephalitis, and CMV retinitis in immunocompromised patients.

ADRs & Safety: Crystalline nephropathy with IV Acyclovir (requires aggressive IV hydration) and severe bone marrow suppression with Ganciclovir.

Acyclovir Profile

Indication: HSV & VZV infections.

Key ADR: Crystalline nephropathy (IV).

Nursing Pearl: Always administer IV infusion slowly with concurrent IV saline hydration.

Ganciclovir Profile

Indication: CMV retinitis & prophylaxis.

Key ADR: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia/thrombocytopenia).

Nursing Pearl: Monitor CBC with differential weekly.

Mnemonics for Herpes/CMV Agents

• Uses Mnemonic ("HERPES"): Herpes labialis/genitalis | Encephalitis | Retinitis (CMV) | Pox (chickenpox/shingles) | Epstein-barr adjunct | Suppression of outbreaks

• ADR Mnemonic ("CRYSTAL"): Crystals in renal tubules (Acyclovir) | Reduced blood counts (Ganciclovir) | Yields electrolyte waste (Foscarnet) | Seizures | Toxicity to kidneys | Anuria risk | Lethargy

5 Advanced HIV Classes (NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & INSTIs)

Advanced HIV

Inhibit viral integration, protein cleavage, or allosteric reverse transcriptase binding.

Step-by-Step MOA Flowchart: Integrase Inhibitors (INSTIs)
1 Viral cDNA Formation Reverse transcriptase creates double-stranded viral DNA.
2 Magnesium Chelation (Target) INSTIs bind Mg²⁺ ions at the active site of HIV integrase enzyme.
3 Strand Transfer Blocked Viral DNA cannot integrate into human host chromosomes.

Prototype Drugs: Dolutegravir, Bictegravir (INSTIs); Darunavir, Atazanavir (PIs); Efavirenz, Rilpivirine (NNRTIs); Ritonavir / Cobicistat (Boosters).

MOA: INSTIs stop strand transfer; PIs stop polyprotein cleavage yielding non-infectious virions; NNRTIs bind allosterically to RT.

Clinical Uses: Frontline and salvage combination antiretroviral therapy (cART).

ADRs: Vivid dreams/insomnia (Efavirenz), hyperglycemia, lipodystrophy ("buffalo hump"), and dyslipidemia (PIs).

Dolutegravir (INSTI) Profile

Indication: Preferred frontline HIV anchor.

Key ADR: Insomnia & weight gain.

Nursing Pearl: High genetic barrier to resistance.

Darunavir (PI) Profile

Indication: Treatment-experienced HIV.

Key ADR: Hyperglycemia & fat redistribution.

Nursing Pearl: Co-administered with Ritonavir or Cobicistat booster.

Mnemonics for Advanced HIV Classes

• Suffix Mnemonic ("T-N-P"): Tegravir = Integrase (-tegravir) | Navir = Protease (-navir) | Virenz = NNRTI (-virenz)

• ADR Mnemonic ("SUGAR"): Sugar elevated (hyperglycemia from PIs) | Usual lipid increase | Gain of fat (buffalo hump) | Anomaly in dreams (Efavirenz) | Rash (SJS)

6 Critical Nursing Exam Warnings

Exam Safety
1. Hepatitis B Reactivation Alert

Always test patients for Hepatitis B before starting or stopping HIV regimens containing Tenofovir, Lamivudine, or Emtricitabine to prevent lethal hepatic flares.

2. Abacavir HLA-B*5701 Screening

Mandatory genetic screening for HLA-B*5701 before dispensing Abacavir to prevent fatal systemic hypersensitivity reactions.

3. Acyclovir Hydration Requirement

Run IV normal saline alongside IV Acyclovir infusions to prevent drug crystallization in renal tubules and acute kidney injury.

4. Amiodarone & Sofosbuvir Interaction

Never co-administer Sofosbuvir with Amiodarone due to severe, potentially fatal symptomatic bradycardia and heart block.
