Antiviral Pharmacology Master Study Guide

A clear breakdown of antiviral classifications, simplified Mechanisms of Action (MOA), flowchart visuals, nursing alerts, and high-yield exam points.

1. Respiratory Virus Antivirals (Influenza, RSV, COVID-19)

Medications used to treat or prevent respiratory viral infections by stopping uncoating, gene copying, or viral release.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: Neuraminidase Inhibitor Action (Oseltamivir)
1
Viral Replication
New flu viruses copy themselves inside respiratory cells.
2
Neuraminidase Blocked IMP FOR EXAM
Drug blocks the enzyme needed to snip viral attachment anchors.
3
Virions Trapped
Viruses remain glued to host cell wall and cannot spread.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. Neuraminidase Inhibitors
Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) Zanamivir Peramivir
MOA: Prevents viral detachment from host cell membrane by inhibiting viral neuraminidase. Keeps new flu viruses trapped inside the infected cell.
Uses: Treatment and prevention of acute Influenza A and B (must give within 48 hours of symptoms!). IMP FOR EXAM
ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, delirium/hallucinations, and bronchospasm (avoid inhaled zanamivir in asthma/COPD!).
2. Cap-Dependent Endonuclease Inhibitor
Baloxavir Marboxil
MOA: Blocks viral gene transcription early by inhibiting cap-dependent endonuclease inside the viral polymerase complex.
Uses: Single-dose oral treatment for acute uncomplicated flu (ages 12+).
ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, headache, and nausea.
3. M2 Ion Channel Blockers
Amantadine Rimantadine
MOA: Blocks M2 proton channels on Influenza A to prevent viral uncoating in host cells.
Uses: Historical treatment of Influenza A (rarely used now due to widespread resistance). IMP FOR EXAM
ADRs: CNS dizziness, insomnia, confusion, and peripheral anticholinergic symptoms (dry mouth).
4. Monoclonal Antibody for RSV
Palivizumab
MOA: Binds directly to the RSV Fusion (F) protein to block viral entry into child airway cells.
Uses: Prophylaxis against severe RSV lower respiratory disease in high-risk premature infants.
ADRs: Injection site pain, fever, rash, and upper respiratory tract symptoms.
5. RNA Polymerase (RdRp) Inhibitor
Remdesivir
MOA: Adenosine analog that incorporates into viral RNA chain and stops SARS-CoV-2 RNA polymerase.
Uses: Treatment of COVID-19 in hospitalized patients requiring oxygen therapy.
ADRs: Raised ALT/AST liver enzymes, nausea, and IV infusion hypersensitivity.

💡 Mnemonic for Respiratory Antivirals: "O.B.A.P.R."

Oseltamivir • Baloxavir • Amantadine • Palivizumab • Remdesivir

2. HIV Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs vs. NtRTIs)

These drugs act as fake DNA building blocks that incorporate into viral DNA and immediately break off DNA chain elongation.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: NRTI & NtRTI Chain Termination
1
Phosphorylation
NRTIs need 3 host enzyme steps; NtRTIs (Tenofovir) already have 1 phosphate!
2
Fake Block Added IMP FOR EXAM
Reverse transcriptase inserts drug into growing viral DNA strand.
3
Chain Termination
Lacks 3'-OH chemical tail; no more building blocks can link.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. Nucleoside RT Inhibitors (NRTIs)
Zidovudine (AZT) Lamivudine (3TC) Emtricitabine (FTC) Abacavir (ABC)
MOA: Synthetic nucleosides requiring 3 intracellular phosphorylation steps to stop reverse transcriptase.
Key Exam Points:
• Abacavir: Mandatory genetic screening for HLA-B*5701 before starting (risk of fatal hypersensitivity!). IMP FOR EXAM
• Zidovudine: Causes severe macrocytic anemia and bone marrow suppression.
• Emtricitabine: Can cause hyperpigmentation on palms and soles.
ADRs: Lactic acidosis, hepatic steatosis, headache, GI upset.
2. Nucleotide RT Inhibitor (NtRTI)
Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF)
MOA: Possesses a pre-attached phosphonate group (nucleotide), so it needs only 2 phosphorylation steps to activate!
Key Exam Points:
• TDF: Higher risk of renal proximal tubule failure (Fanconi syndrome) and bone mineral density loss. IMP FOR EXAM
• TAF: Newer formulation with lower kidney/bone toxicity.
Uses: Preferred backbone for HIV cART, PrEP, and chronic Hepatitis B.
3. Discontinued / Legacy NRTIs
Stavudine (d4T) Didanosine (ddI)
MOA: Older thymidine/purine analogs that cause viral DNA chain termination.
ADRs: Severe peripheral neuropathy, acute pancreatitis, and severe lipoatrophy (fat wasting).

💡 Mnemonic for NRTIs & NtRTIs: "Z.L.E.A.T.S.D."

Zidovudine • Lamivudine • Emtricitabine • Abacavir • Tenofovir • Stavudine • Didanosine

3. Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Antivirals

Agents that suppress chronic HBV by inhibiting its reverse transcriptase/polymerase enzyme or activating immune destruction.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. HBV Polymerase Inhibitors (Nucleoside/Nucleotide)
Entecavir Tenofovir (TDF / TAF) Telbivudine Adefovir Dipivoxil
MOA: Inhibits HBV DNA polymerase base priming, negative-strand DNA synthesis, and positive-strand elongation.
Uses: First-line chronic Hepatitis B oral maintenance therapy.
ADRs: Elevated serum creatine kinase (telbivudine), nephrotoxicity (adefovir/TDF), and lactic acidosis.
2. Immunomodulating Cytokines
Peginterferon Alfa-2a
MOA: Activates host JAK-STAT pathway to boost T-cell immunity and degrade viral RNA.
Uses: Finite-duration treatment for HBeAg-positive or negative chronic Hepatitis B.
ADRs: Flu-like symptoms (fever/chills), severe depression/suicidal ideation, and neutropenia.

💡 Mnemonic for Hepatitis B Agents: "E.T.A.P."

Entecavir • Tenofovir • Adefovir • Peginterferon

4. Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs)

Target non-structural (NS) proteins of Hepatitis C to achieve over 95% complete cure rates (SVR) without interferon.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: HCV Direct-Acting Antiviral Targets
1
NS3/4A Protease (-previr)
Blocks polyprotein cleavage.
2
NS5A Complex (-asvir)
Blocks RNA replication complex assembly.
3
NS5B Polymerase (-buvir)
Blocks viral RNA polymerase chain.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. NS5B Polymerase Inhibitors (Ending in "-buvir")
Sofosbuvir
MOA: Uridine nucleotide analog that blocks HCV NS5B RNA polymerase.
Uses: Pan-genotypic oral curative regimen backbone for HCV.
Nursing Warning: Do NOT combine with Amiodarone! Causes fatal severe bradycardia! IMP FOR EXAM
2. NS5A Replication Complex Inhibitors (Ending in "-asvir")
Ledipasvir Velpatasvir Pibrentasvir
MOA: Inhibits NS5A phosphoprotein required for viral assembly and secretion.
Uses: Combined with Sofosbuvir for all Hepatitis C genotypes.
ADRs: Mild headache, fatigue, nausea.
3. NS3/4A Protease Inhibitors (Ending in "-previr")
Glecaprevir Voxilaprevir Grazoprevir
MOA: Cleavage block of viral precursor proteins.
Uses: Combination treatment for chronic HCV (especially prior DAA treatment failure).
ADRs: Bilirubin spikes, fatigue, nausea.
4. Guanosine Analog Adjunct
Ribavirin
MOA: Depletes intracellular GTP pools to induce lethal mutations in viral RNA.
ADRs: Dose-dependent hemolytic anemia and severe teratogenicity (Pregnancy Category X!). IMP FOR EXAM

💡 Mnemonic for HCV Name Endings: "P.A.B."

• Protease Inhibitors end in "-PREVIR" (NS3/4A)
• Assembly Inhibitors end in "-ASVIR" (NS5A)
• Building/Polymerase Inhibitors end in "-BUVIR" (NS5B)

5. Herpesvirus (HSV/VZV) & Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Drugs

Agents that block viral DNA polymerase in Herpes Simplex, Shingles, and Cytomegalovirus infections.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. HSV/VZV Thymidine Kinase-Dependent Drugs
Acyclovir Valacyclovir Famciclovir
MOA: Requires viral Thymidine Kinase to activate initial monophosphate form, then cellular enzymes form triphosphate to stop viral DNA polymerase.
Uses: Genital herpes, shingles (VZV), chickenpox, and HSV encephalitis.
Nursing Warning: IV Acyclovir causes Crystalline Nephropathy! Always infuse with aggressive IV saline hydration! IMP FOR EXAM
2. CMV UL97 Kinase-Dependent Drugs
Ganciclovir Valganciclovir
MOA: Activated by viral UL97 kinase in CMV to inhibit viral DNA polymerase.
Uses: Treatment and secondary prevention of severe CMV retinitis in AIDS/transplant patients.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia). IMP FOR EXAM
3. Non-Kinase Dependent DNA Polymerase Blockers
Cidofovir Foscarnet
MOA: Bypass viral kinase activation entirely to directly block resistant HSV/CMV DNA polymerase.
Uses: Resistant HSV and Ganciclovir-resistant CMV retinitis.
ADRs: Severe nephrotoxicity and profound electrolyte wasting (hypocalcemia, hypokalemia). Give Cidofovir with Probenecid!
4. CMV Terminase Complex Inhibitor
Letermovir
MOA: Targets viral UL56 subunit of CMV terminase to block viral DNA packaging.
Uses: CMV prophylaxis in stem cell transplant recipients.

💡 Mnemonic for Herpes/CMV: "A.G.C.F.L."

Acyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet • Letermovir

6. HIV NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & INSTIs

Target non-nucleoside binding sites, viral protein cleavage, or integration into human chromosomes.

⚡ Flowchart MOA: Integrase Inhibitor Action (INSTIs)
1
Double-Stranded cDNA
Reverse transcriptase creates viral cDNA.
2
Integrase Chelated IMP FOR EXAM
INSTI binds magnesium ions in integrase active core.
3
Integration Prevented
Viral DNA cannot splice into human chromosome.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. Non-Nucleoside RT Inhibitors (NNRTIs)
Efavirenz Rilpivirine Nevirapine
MOA: Bind directly to an allosteric pocket on reverse transcriptase (no phosphorylation required!).
ADRs: Vivid dreams/nightmares & insomnia (efavirenz), Stevens-Johnson syndrome rash, hepatotoxicity.
2. Protease Inhibitors (PIs - Ending in "-navir")
Darunavir Atazanavir Lopinavir
MOA: Inhibit HIV protease from chopping Gag-Pol polyproteins into functional units. Causes immature non-infectious virions.
ADRs: Hyperglycemia/diabetes, lipodystrophy (buffalo hump), dyslipidemia, hyperbilirubinemia (atazanavir).
3. Integrase Inhibitors (INSTIs - Ending in "-tegravir")
Dolutegravir Bictegravir Raltegravir
MOA: Chelate magnesium metal ions at the active site of HIV integrase, stopping proviral DNA strand transfer into human host chromosomes.
Uses: Preferred frontline anchor class for HIV treatment.
4. Pharmacokinetic Boosters
Ritonavir Cobicistat
MOA: Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors used at sub-therapeutic doses to boost serum levels of co-administered protease inhibitors or integrase inhibitors.

🚫 Critical Nursing Exam Warnings IMP FOR EXAM

• Hepatitis B Reactivation: Always test patients for Hepatitis B before starting or stopping HIV regimens containing Tenofovir, Lamivudine, or Emtricitabine!
• HLA-B*5701 Screening: Mandatory before giving Abacavir.
• Acyclovir Hydration: Run IV saline to prevent renal crystal precipitation.
• Amiodarone + Sofosbuvir: Never co-administer (fatal bradycardia risk).
