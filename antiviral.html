🫁 1. Respiratory Virus Antivirals (Influenza, RSV, COVID-19)

Respiratory System

Target viral entry, uncoating, replication, or cellular release in upper and lower respiratory tract infections.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: Neuraminidase Inhibitors
1
Viral Replication
Influenza multiplies inside respiratory cells.
2
Enzyme Blockade (Target)
Drug blocks neuraminidase enzyme from snipping anchors.
3
Virions Trapped
New viruses stay glued to cells and cannot spread.
Neuraminidase Inhibitor
Oseltamivir High Yield
Mechanism of Action: Competitively inhibits viral neuraminidase, preventing viral release.
Primary Uses: Influenza A & B treatment (must start within 48h).
Side Effects (ADRs): Nausea, vomiting, delirium.
Nursing Pearl: Administer with food to minimize GI upset.
Endonuclease Inhibitor
Baloxavir Marboxil
Mechanism of Action: Inhibits cap-dependent endonuclease in the viral polymerase complex.
Primary Uses: Single-dose oral treatment for acute uncomplicated influenza.
Side Effects (ADRs): Diarrhea, bronchitis, headache.
Nursing Pearl: It is a prodrug converted in vivo to active baloxavir.

📖 Clear Drug Abbreviations

• RSV: Respiratory Syncytial Virus | RdRp: RNA-Dependent RNA Polymerase | COVID-19: Coronavirus Disease 2019

💡 Easy Mnemonic for Respiratory Class: "Flu Gets Out Fast"

Flu (Influenza) | Gets (Baloxavir) | Out (Oseltamivir) | Fast (Remdesivir for COVID)

🎗️ 2. Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) Antivirals

HIV Therapeutics

Comprehensive combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) targeting reverse transcription, viral integration, and protein cleavage.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: NRTI & INSTI Blockade
1
Intracellular Activation
NRTIs/NtRTIs are phosphorylated inside host cells.
2
Fake Block / Chelation (Target)
Inserts fake bases into DNA or chelates Mg²⁺ at integrase core.
3
Replication Halted
Stops viral DNA chain extension and nuclear integration.
Nucleoside RTI (NRTI)
Abacavir (ABC) Critical Test Alert
Mechanism of Action: Tri-phosphorylated fake nucleoside causing DNA chain termination.
Primary Uses: Backbone agent in combination antiretroviral therapy (cART).
Side Effects (ADRs): Severe hypersensitivity reaction, lactic acidosis.
Nursing Pearl: Mandatory HLA-B*5701 genetic testing prior to prescribing!
Nucleotide RTI (NtRTI)
Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF) / TDF
Mechanism of Action: Pre-phosphorylated nucleotide analog causing viral DNA chain termination.
Primary Uses: Frontline HIV cART, PrEP, and chronic Hepatitis B.
Side Effects (ADRs): Fanconi syndrome and bone mineral loss (higher with TDF than TAF).
Nursing Pearl: TAF is a targeted prodrug achieving higher intracellular levels with less systemic kidney load.
Integrase Inhibitor (INSTI)
Dolutegravir (DTG)
Mechanism of Action: Chelates magnesium ions at the catalytic core of HIV integrase.
Primary Uses: Preferred frontline anchor agent in modern cART regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Insomnia, headache, mild weight gain.
Nursing Pearl: High genetic barrier to viral drug resistance.
Protease Inhibitor (PI)
Darunavir (DRV)
Mechanism of Action: Inhibits viral protease from cleaving Gag-Pol polyproteins into mature units.
Primary Uses: Treatment-experienced HIV infection and salvage regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Hyperglycemia, lipodystrophy ("buffalo hump"), dyslipidemia.
Nursing Pearl: Always co-administered with a pharmacokinetic booster (Ritonavir or Cobicistat).

📖 Clear Drug Abbreviations

• HIV: Human Immunodeficiency Virus | cART: Combination Antiretroviral Therapy | PrEP: Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis | NRTI: Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitor | NtRTI: Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitor | INSTI: Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitor | PI: Protease Inhibitor | AZT: Zidovudine | FTC: Emtricitabine | 3TC: Lamivudine

💡 Easy Mnemonic for HIV Class: "Always Try Doing Daily Care"

Abacavir | Tenofovir | Dolutegravir | Darunavir (Core active drugs in HIV treatment schemas)

🩺 3. Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Antivirals

Hepatitis B

Target chronic HBV polymerase replication and modulate host immune response to suppress viral load and prevent cirrhosis.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: HBV Polymerase Inhibition
1
Viral Entry & CccDNA
HBV infects hepatocytes and forms stable cccDNA templates.
2
Polymerase Blockade (Target)
Oral nucleosides/nucleotides inhibit HBV reverse transcriptase.
3
Suppression Achieved
Halts DNA synthesis and reduces hepatic inflammation.
Polymerase Inhibitor
Entecavir (ETV)
Mechanism of Action: Selectively inhibits HBV polymerase base priming and DNA elongation.
Primary Uses: First-line oral maintenance therapy for chronic Hepatitis B.
Side Effects (ADRs): Headache, fatigue, elevated liver transaminases.
Nursing Pearl: Take on an empty stomach for maximum drug absorption.
Immunomodulator
Peginterferon Alfa-2a (PEG-IFN)
Mechanism of Action: Activates host JAK-STAT pathway to boost T-cell immunity against infected hepatocytes.
Primary Uses: Finite-duration treatment course for HBeAg-positive/negative chronic HBV.
Side Effects (ADRs): Flu-like symptoms, severe depression/suicidal ideation, neutropenia.
Nursing Pearl: Screen patients for psychiatric history before initiating therapy.

📖 Clear Drug Abbreviations

• HBV: Hepatitis B Virus | cccDNA: Covalently Closed Circular DNA | ETV: Entecavir | PEG-IFN: Peginterferon Alfa-2a | HBeAg: Hepatitis B e-Antigen

💡 Easy Mnemonic for HBV Class: "Every Patient Heals"

Entecavir | Peginterferon | Hep B controlled!

🧬 4. Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs)

Hepatitis C

Target non-structural (NS) viral proteins to achieve permanent virologic cure (SVR > 95%) without interferon.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: HCV DAA Targets
1
NS3/4A Protease (-previr)
Blocks viral polyprotein cleavage.
2
NS5A Complex (-asvir)
Stops RNA replication assembly.
3
NS5B Polymerase (-buvir)
Stops RNA chain extension.
NS5B Polymerase Inhibitor
Sofosbuvir (SOF) Black Box Warning
Mechanism of Action: Nucleotide analog inhibiting HCV NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
Primary Uses: Pan-genotypic backbone for curative Hepatitis C regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Fatigue, headache, severe symptomatic bradycardia.
Nursing Pearl: NEVER combine with Amiodarone (causes fatal heart block!).
Guanosine Analog Adjunct
Ribavirin (RBV)
Mechanism of Action: Depletes GTP pools, inducing lethal mutations in viral RNA.
Primary Uses: Combination therapy for refractory HCV cases.
Side Effects (ADRs): Dose-dependent hemolytic anemia, severe teratogenicity.
Nursing Pearl: Pregnancy Category X; requires strict double contraception.

📖 Clear Drug Abbreviations

• HCV: Hepatitis C Virus | DAA: Direct-Acting Antiviral | SVR: Sustained Virologic Response | SOF: Sofosbuvir | RBV: Ribavirin | NS: Non-Structural Protein

💡 Easy Mnemonic for HCV Class: "Super Cure for Hep C"

Sofosbuvir | Curing | Hep C effectively!

🔬 5. Herpesvirus (HSV/VZV) & Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Agents

Herpes / CMV

Nucleoside analogues requiring viral or cellular kinase activation to inhibit viral DNA polymerase in HSV, VZV, and CMV.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: Acyclovir Kinase Activation
1
Viral Kinase Entry
Activated by viral Thymidine Kinase inside infected cells.
2
Triphosphate Conversion (Target)
Host enzymes convert drug into active triphosphate form.
3
DNA Polymerase Blocked
Stops viral DNA chain extension and replication.
Thymidine Kinase-Dependent
Valacyclovir / Acyclovir Renal Hazard
Mechanism of Action: Viral thymidine kinase activation followed by selective viral DNA polymerase inhibition.
Primary Uses: Genital herpes outbreaks, chickenpox, shingles (VZV), HSV encephalitis.
Side Effects (ADRs): Crystalline nephropathy (IV administration), neurotoxicity.
Nursing Pearl: Run IV normal saline hydration alongside infusion to prevent kidney crystal damage!
UL97 Kinase-Dependent
Valganciclovir / Ganciclovir
Mechanism of Action: Activated by viral UL97 kinase in CMV to inhibit viral DNA polymerase.
Primary Uses: Treatment and prophylaxis of severe CMV retinitis in immunocompromised hosts.
Side Effects (ADRs): Severe bone marrow suppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia).
Nursing Pearl: Valganciclovir is the oral prodrug of ganciclovir with 10x greater bioavailability.

📖 Clear Drug Abbreviations

• HSV: Herpes Simplex Virus | VZV: Varicella-Zoster Virus | CMV: Cytomegalovirus | TK: Thymidine Kinase

💡 Easy Mnemonic for Herpes Class: "Always Guard Cold Sores"

Acyclovir | Ganciclovir | Combat cold sores, shingles, and cytomegalovirus!

🚫 Critical Nursing Exam Safety Warnings

• Hepatitis B Reactivation: Test for HBV before starting or stopping Tenofovir/Lamivudine regimens.
• Acyclovir Hydration: Prevent acute tubular kidney injury with high-volume IV normal saline.
• Sofosbuvir + Amiodarone: Absolute contraindication due to lethal cardiac arrest/bradycardia risk.
