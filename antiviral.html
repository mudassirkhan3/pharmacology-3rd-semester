Antiviral Pharmacology Guide — Complete Classifications, Uses & ADRs

Complete reference guide featuring separated Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C classifications, detailed Nucleoside and Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors, Clinical Uses, and Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) for every agent.

Classification: Drugs for Respiratory Virus Infections

Detailed pharmacological breakdown of antiviral agents targeting influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and coronavirus infections with mechanisms, uses, and adverse drug reactions.

📂 Drug Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Oseltamivir / Zanamivir / Peramivir MOA: Neuraminidase Inhibitors
Uses: Treatment and chemoprophylaxis of acute uncomplicated Influenza A and B.
ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, headache, psychiatric events (hallucinations, delirium mostly in pediatric patients), bronchospasm (in inhaled zanamivir).
Baloxavir Marboxil MOA: Cap-Dependent Endonuclease Inhibitor
Uses: Single-dose treatment of acute uncomplicated influenza in patients aged 12 years and older.
ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, nasopharyngitis, headache.
Palivizumab MOA: Monoclonal Antibody Fusion Blocker
Uses: Prevention of serious lower respiratory tract disease caused by RSV in high-risk infants.
ADRs: Injection site reactions, upper respiratory infection, fever, rash, rare anaphylaxis.
Remdesivir MOA: RNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Uses: Treatment of COVID-19 requiring supplemental oxygen and severe viral pneumonias.
ADRs: Elevated liver transaminases (ALT/AST), nausea, increased risk of infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.Z.P.B.R." (Respiratory Antivirals)

Oseltamivir • Zanamivir • Peramivir • Baloxavir • Remdesivir.

Classification: Drugs for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Infections

Strictly separated into Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs), Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NtRTIs), and Immune Modulators for chronic hepatitis B management.

1. Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs) for HBV

These agents lack a 3'-hydroxyl group, causing immediate DNA chain termination upon incorporation by viral reverse transcriptase.

Entecavir MOA: Guanosine Nucleoside Analog
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B infection with active viral replication and evidence of hepatocellular inflammation.
ADRs: Headache, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, rare lactic acidosis with severe hepatomegaly and steatosis.
Lamivudine MOA: Cytidine Analog NRTI
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B and HIV coinfection (though high resistance rates limit HBV monotherapy).
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, pancreatitis, lactic acidosis, severe acute exacerbation of hepatitis upon discontinuation.

2. Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NtRTIs) for HBV

These analogs already possess a preformed phosphonate group mimicking nucleotides, bypassing initial phosphorylation steps to inhibit viral polymerase.

Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) MOA: Nucleotide RT Inhibitor
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B and HIV-1 infection.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity, proximal renal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi syndrome), decreased bone mineral density, nausea, flatulence.
Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF) MOA: Targeted Nucleotide RT Inhibitor
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B with safer renal and bone profile compared to TDF.
ADRs: Headache, abdominal pain, fatigue, nausea; significantly lower rates of renal impairment and bone mineral density loss.
Adefovir Dipivoxil MOA: Acyclic Nucleotide Analog
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B, including lamivudine-resistant strains.
ADRs: Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity (elevated serum creatinine, hypophosphatemia), asthenia, headache, abdominal pain.

3. Immune Modulators for HBV

Peginterferon Alfa-2a MOA: Cytokine / Immune Stimulator
Uses: HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B.
ADRs: Flu-like symptoms (fever, chills, myalgia), severe depression, suicidal ideation, bone marrow suppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), alopecia, thyroid disorders.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.T. L.A.P." (Hepatitis B Agents)

Entecavir • Tenofovir (TDF/TAF) • Lamivudine • Adefovir • Peginterferon.

Classification: Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) for Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) Infections

Advanced direct-acting antiviral drug classes classified by target viral non-structural proteins to achieve a permanent virologic cure for Hepatitis C.

📂 Drug Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Sofosbuvir MOA: NS5B RNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Uses: Chronic hepatitis C genotypes 1 through 6 in combination regimens.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, insomnia; severe symptomatic bradycardia if co-administered with amiodarone.
Ledipasvir / Velpatasvir / Pibrentasvir MOA: NS5A Replication Complex Inhibitors
Uses: Pan-genotypic chronic hepatitis C virus eradication.
ADRs: Headache, fatigue, diarrhea, nausea, dizziness.
Glecaprevir / Voxilaprevir MOA: NS3/4A Protease Inhibitors
Uses: Treatment of chronic HCV including direct-acting antiviral failure cases and compensated cirrhosis.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, pruritus, transient elevations in serum bilirubin.
Ribavirin MOA: Guanosine Analog Co-Therapy
Uses: Combination regimens for difficult-to-treat chronic HCV or severe RSV infections.
ADRs: Hemolytic anemia (can worsen cardiac disease), severe teratogenicity (Category X - requires strict contraception), fatigue, rash.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.O.F. + Asvir / Previr" (HCV DAAs)

Sofosbuvir paired with DAAs ending in -asvir (NS5A) and -previr (Protease inhibitors).

Classification: Drugs for Herpesvirus and Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Nucleoside and nucleotide analogs engineered to treat HSV-1, HSV-2, Varicella-Zoster, and Cytomegalovirus.

📂 Drug Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir MOA: Viral DNA Polymerase Inhibitors
Uses: Genital herpes, herpes labialis, varicella-zoster (shingles), and herpes encephalitis.
ADRs: Malaise, headache, nausea, diarrhea; crystalline nephropathy and neurotoxicity (tremors, confusion) with rapid IV bolus administration.
Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir MOA: CMV DNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Uses: Treatment and prevention of Cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis and systemic disease in immunocompromised patients.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), fever, rash, nausea, central nervous system toxicity.
Cidofovir MOA: Nucleotide Analog (CMV/HSV)
Uses: CMV retinitis in patients with AIDS (especially resistant strains).
ADRs: Severe nephrotoxicity (requires probenecid co-administration and saline hydration), proteinuria, neutropenia, metabolic acidosis.
Foscarnet MOA: Pyrophosphate Analog
Uses: Acyclovir-resistant HSV/VZV and ganciclovir-resistant CMV infections.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity, severe electrolyte disturbances (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia), genital ulcerations, seizures.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.V.G.C.F."

Acyclovir • Valacyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet.

Classification: HIV Antiretrovirals (NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & Entry Inhibitors)

Classes targeting early and middle stages of the HIV life cycle.

📂 Drug Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Efavirenz / Rilpivirine / Doravirine MOA: Non-Nucleoside RT Inhibitors (NNRTIs)
Uses: Combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for HIV-1 infection.
ADRs: Central nervous system symptoms (vivid dreams, dizziness, depression with efavirenz), rash, elevated liver enzymes, QT prolongation (rilpivirine).
Darunavir / Atazanavir / Lopinavir MOA: Protease Inhibitors (PIs)
Uses: Treatment-naive and treatment-experienced HIV-1 infection management.
ADRs: Dyslipidemia, insulin resistance/hyperglycemia, lipodystrophy (fat redistribution), GI intolerance, indirect hyperbilirubinemia (atazanavir).
Maraviroc MOA: CCR5 Antagonist (Entry Inhibitor)
Uses: Treatment-experienced adults infected with CCR5-tropic HIV-1 strains.
ADRs: Hepatotoxicity (preceded by systemic allergic rash), upper respiratory infections, cough, musculoskeletal symptoms, orthostatic hypotension.
Enfuvirtide MOA: Fusion Inhibitor
Uses: Salvage therapy for treatment-experienced patients with ongoing HIV replication despite optimized regimens.
ADRs: Painful subcutaneous injection site reactions (nodules, erythema), increased risk of bacterial pneumonia, hypersensitivity reactions.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.D.A.M."

Efavirenz • Darunavir • Atazanavir • Maraviroc.

Classification: HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

Frontline anchor class in modern antiretroviral therapy regimens.

📂 Drug Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Dolutegravir / Bictegravir MOA: Second-Generation INSTIs
Uses: Preferred frontline anchor agents for HIV-1 initial and maintenance treatment.
ADRs: Insomnia, headache, weight gain, nausea, rare neural tube defect risk when used at time of conception.
Raltegravir MOA: First-Generation INSTI
Uses: Treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretrovirals.
ADRs: Insomnia, nausea, headache, diarrhea, rare severe hypersensitivity reactions (DRESS syndrome), CPK elevations/myopathy.
Cabotegravir MOA: Long-Acting INSTI
Uses: HIV treatment (monthly/bimonthly injectables) and pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP).
ADRs: Injection site reactions (pain, swelling, nodules), pyrexia, fatigue, headache, musculoskeletal pain.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir"

Bictegravir • Cabotegravir • Dolutegravir • Raltegravir.

Classification: Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Agents used to inhibit CYP3A4 enzymes and boost co-administered antiretroviral drug half-lives.

📂 Drug Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Ritonavir MOA: Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitor
Uses: Low-dose pharmacokinetic enhancer (booster) for protease inhibitors.
ADRs: Gastrointestinal distress (nausea, diarrhea), dyslipidemia, elevated transaminases, peripheral paresthesias.
Cobicistat MOA: Selective CYP3A Enhancer
Uses: Pharmacokinetic booster for elvitegravir, darunavir, and atazanavir in fixed-dose combinations.
ADRs: Benign increase in serum creatinine (due to tubular secretion inhibition without affecting true GFR), nausea, jaundice.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C. Boosters"

Ritonavir • Cobicistat.

Classification: HIV Fixed-Dose Combinations (Single-Tablet Regimens)

Advanced single-tablet regimens combining multiple antiretroviral classes into one daily pill.

📂 Regimen Inventory: Mechanism, Uses & ADRs

Biktarvy (Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / TAF) STR Regimen (INSTI + 2 NRTIs)
Uses: Complete frontline regimen for HIV-1 treatment-naive adults.
ADRs: Diarrhea, nausea, headache, mild lipid elevations; minimal renal/bone risk due to TAF backbone.
Triumeq (Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine) STR Regimen (INSTI + 2 NRTIs)
Uses: Complete antiretroviral regimen (requires HLA-B*5701 negative screening).
ADRs: Insomnia, fatigue, nausea, headache, severe hypersensitivity reactions (if HLA-B*5701 positive).
Dovato (Dolutegravir / Lamivudine) 2-Drug STR Regimen (INSTI + NRTI)
Uses: Initial and switch maintenance therapy for HIV-1 suppression.
ADRs: Headache, diarrhea, nausea, insomnia, fatigue; reduces long-term renal and bone toxicity risks.

🚫 Clinical Warnings & Nursing Interventions

Always perform baseline hepatitis B screening and check renal function (eGFR) before administering tenofovir-containing regimens. Screen patients genetically for HLA-B*5701 before prescribing abacavir to prevent fatal hypersensitivity reactions.
