Antiviral Pharmacology Guide — Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Complete reference guide featuring separated Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C classifications, individual short Mechanisms of Action (MOA), Clinical Uses, and Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs).

Classification: Drugs for Respiratory Virus Infections

Detailed pharmacological breakdown of antiviral agents targeting influenza, RSV, and coronavirus infections.

📂 Drug Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Oseltamivir / Zanamivir / Peramivir
Short MOA: Inhibits viral neuraminidase enzyme, preventing viral release.
Uses: Treatment and chemoprophylaxis of Influenza A and B.
ADRs: Nausea, vomiting, headache, psychiatric events (hallucinations), bronchospasm (inhaled zanamivir).
Baloxavir Marboxil
Short MOA: Blocks cap-dependent endonuclease activity, stopping viral transcription.
Uses: Single-dose treatment of acute uncomplicated influenza.
ADRs: Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, nasopharyngitis, headache.
Palivizumab
Short MOA: Monoclonal antibody binding to RSV F protein to block cellular entry.
Uses: Prevention of severe lower respiratory RSV disease in high-risk infants.
ADRs: Injection site reactions, upper respiratory infection, fever, rash.
Remdesivir
Short MOA: Nucleotide analog inhibiting viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
Uses: Treatment of COVID-19 requiring supplemental oxygen.
ADRs: Elevated liver transaminases (ALT/AST), nausea, infusion hypersensitivity.

💡 Mnemonic: "O.Z.P.B.R."

Oseltamivir • Zanamivir • Peramivir • Baloxavir • Remdesivir.

Classification: Drugs for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Infections

Strictly separated into Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs), Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NtRTIs), and Immune Modulators.

1. Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs) for HBV

Entecavir
Short MOA: Competes with guanosine to inhibit HBV polymerase and reverse transcription.
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B with active viral replication.
ADRs: Headache, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, rare lactic acidosis.
Lamivudine
Short MOA: Cytidine analog inhibiting HBV reverse transcriptase and chain extension.
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B and HIV coinfection.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, pancreatitis, lactic acidosis, hepatitis flare on withdrawal.

2. Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NtRTIs) for HBV

Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF)
Short MOA: Nucleotide analog terminating viral DNA chain elongation.
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B and HIV-1 infection.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity (Fanconi syndrome), decreased bone mineral density, nausea.
Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF)
Short MOA: Targeted nucleotide analog delivering active drug efficiently to hepatocytes.
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B with reduced renal/bone toxicity.
ADRs: Headache, abdominal pain, fatigue, nausea (lower renal/bone impact than TDF).
Adefovir Dipivoxil
Short MOA: Acyclic nucleotide analog inhibiting HBV DNA polymerase.
Uses: Chronic hepatitis B, including lamivudine-resistant strains.
ADRs: Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity, hypophosphatemia, asthenia, headache.

3. Immune Modulators for HBV

Peginterferon Alfa-2a
Short MOA: Stimulates host cell-mediated immune response and suppresses viral transcription.
Uses: HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B.
ADRs: Flu-like symptoms, severe depression, bone marrow suppression, alopecia, thyroid disorders.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.T. L.A.P."

Entecavir • Tenofovir • Lamivudine • Adefovir • Peginterferon.

Classification: Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) for Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) Infections

Targeting viral non-structural proteins to achieve permanent virologic cure.

📂 Drug Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Sofosbuvir
Short MOA: Nucleotide analog inhibitor of NS5B RNA polymerase.
Uses: Chronic HCV genotypes 1-6 in combination regimens.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, insomnia, severe bradycardia if combined with amiodarone.
Ledipasvir / Velpatasvir / Pibrentasvir
Short MOA: Inhibits NS5A viral phosphoprotein required for replication complex assembly.
Uses: Pan-genotypic chronic HCV eradication.
ADRs: Headache, fatigue, diarrhea, nausea, dizziness.
Glecaprevir / Voxilaprevir
Short MOA: Inhibits NS3/4A serine proteases needed for viral polyprotein cleavage.
Uses: HCV DAA-failure cases and compensated cirrhosis.
ADRs: Fatigue, headache, nausea, pruritus, transient bilirubin elevation.
Ribavirin
Short MOA: Guanosine analog enhancing viral mutation rates and depleting intracellular pools.
Uses: Combination HCV and severe RSV regimens.
ADRs: Hemolytic anemia, severe teratogenicity (Category X), fatigue, rash.

💡 Mnemonic: "S.O.F. + Asvir / Previr"

Sofosbuvir paired with -asvir (NS5A) and -previr (Protease inhibitors).

Classification: Drugs for Herpesvirus and Cytomegalovirus (CMV)

Nucleoside and nucleotide analogs for HSV-1, HSV-2, VZV, and CMV.

📂 Drug Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir
Short MOA: Viral DNA polymerase inhibitor activated by viral thymidine kinase.
Uses: Genital herpes, shingles (VZV), herpes encephalitis.
ADRs: Nausea, headache, crystalline nephropathy and neurotoxicity (with rapid IV bolus).
Ganciclovir / Valganciclovir
Short MOA: Specialized nucleoside analog inhibiting CMV DNA polymerase.
Uses: Treatment and prevention of CMV retinitis in immunocompromised patients.
ADRs: Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, thrombocytopenia), fever, rash.
Cidofovir
Short MOA: Nucleotide analog inhibiting viral DNA polymerase directly.
Uses: Resistant CMV retinitis in AIDS patients.
ADRs: Severe nephrotoxicity (requires probenecid/saline hydration), proteinuria, neutropenia.
Foscarnet
Short MOA: Pyrophosphate analog inhibiting viral DNA polymerase binding site.
Uses: Acyclovir-resistant HSV/VZV and ganciclovir-resistant CMV.
ADRs: Nephrotoxicity, severe electrolyte disturbances (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia), seizures.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.V.G.C.F."

Acyclovir • Valacyclovir • Ganciclovir • Cidofovir • Foscarnet.

Classification: HIV Antiretrovirals (NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors & Entry Inhibitors)

Classes targeting early and middle stages of the HIV life cycle.

📂 Drug Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Efavirenz / Rilpivirine / Doravirine
Short MOA: Binds directly to reverse transcriptase to induce conformational halt.
Uses: Combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) for HIV-1.
ADRs: CNS symptoms (vivid dreams, dizziness), rash, elevated liver enzymes, QT prolongation.
Darunavir / Atazanavir / Lopinavir
Short MOA: Binds HIV protease active site to prevent viral polyprotein cleavage.
Uses: HIV-1 treatment-naive and experienced patient management.
ADRs: Dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, lipodystrophy, indirect hyperbilirubinemia.
Maraviroc
Short MOA: CCR5 coreceptor antagonist blocking viral attachment to CD4 cells.
Uses: Treatment-experienced adults with CCR5-tropic HIV-1.
ADRs: Hepatotoxicity (with allergic rash), upper respiratory infections, orthostatic hypotension.
Enfuvirtide
Short MOA: Fusion inhibitor preventing gp41 conformational changes.
Uses: Salvage therapy for treatment-experienced HIV patients.
ADRs: Painful subcutaneous injection site reactions, increased bacterial pneumonia risk.

💡 Mnemonic: "E.D.A.M."

Efavirenz • Darunavir • Atazanavir • Maraviroc.

Classification: HIV Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)

Frontline anchor class in modern antiretroviral therapy regimens.

📂 Drug Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Dolutegravir / Bictegravir
Short MOA: Chelates active metals in integrase to block viral DNA strand transfer.
Uses: Preferred frontline anchor agents for HIV-1.
ADRs: Insomnia, headache, weight gain, nausea, rare neural tube defect risk at conception.
Raltegravir
Short MOA: Inhibits viral DNA insertion into human chromosomes.
Uses: Treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination regimens.
ADRs: Insomnia, nausea, headache, diarrhea, severe hypersensitivity (DRESS).
Cabotegravir
Short MOA: Long-acting integrase strand transfer inhibition.
Uses: Long-acting HIV treatment and pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP).
ADRs: Injection site pain/nodules, pyrexia, fatigue, headache, musculoskeletal pain.

💡 Mnemonic: Ending in "-tegravir"

Bictegravir • Cabotegravir • Dolutegravir • Raltegravir.

Classification: Pharmacokinetic Enhancers for HIV (Boosters)

Agents used to inhibit CYP3A4 enzymes and boost co-administered antiretroviral levels.

📂 Drug Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Ritonavir
Short MOA: Potent CYP3A4 inhibitor used at low doses to boost protease half-lives.
Uses: Pharmacokinetic enhancer for protease inhibitors.
ADRs: GI distress (nausea, diarrhea), dyslipidemia, elevated transaminases.
Cobicistat
Short MOA: Selective CYP3A enzyme inhibitor with zero intrinsic anti-HIV activity.
Uses: Booster for elvitegravir and darunavir in single tablets.
ADRs: Benign serum creatinine increase (tubular inhibition), nausea, jaundice.

💡 Mnemonic: "R.C. Boosters"

Ritonavir • Cobicistat.

Classification: HIV Fixed-Dose Combinations (Single-Tablet Regimens)

Advanced single-tablet regimens combining multiple classes into one daily pill.

📂 Regimen Inventory: Short MOA, Uses & ADRs

Biktarvy (Bictegravir / Emtricitabine / TAF)
Short MOA: Multitarget synergy (INSTI + 2 NRTIs) blocking integration and reverse transcription.
Uses: Frontline complete single-tablet regimen for HIV-1.
ADRs: Diarrhea, nausea, headache, mild lipid changes; minimal renal/bone risk.
Triumeq (Dolutegravir / Abacavir / Lamivudine)
Short MOA: Triple-drug blockade via integrase and reverse transcriptase inhibition.
Uses: Complete ART regimen (requires negative HLA-B*5701 screen).
ADRs: Insomnia, fatigue, nausea, headache, severe hypersensitivity if genetically susceptible.
Dovato (Dolutegravir / Lamivudine)
Short MOA: Two-drug combination inhibiting integrase and reverse transcription.
Uses: Initial and switch maintenance therapy for HIV suppression.
ADRs: Headache, diarrhea, nausea, insomnia; reduces long-term toxicity.

🚫 Clinical Warnings & Nursing Interventions

Always perform baseline hepatitis B screening and check renal function (eGFR) before administering tenofovir regimens. Screen patients genetically for HLA-B*5701 before prescribing abacavir to prevent fatal hypersensitivity reactions.
