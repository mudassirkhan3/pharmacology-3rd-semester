🫁 1. Respiratory Virus Antivirals (Influenza, RSV, COVID-19)

Respiratory System

Target viral entry, uncoating, replication, or cellular release in upper and lower respiratory tract infections.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: Neuraminidase Inhibitors (Oseltamivir)
1
Viral Replication
Influenza multiplies inside respiratory cells.
2
Enzyme Blockade (Target)
Drug blocks neuraminidase enzyme from snipping anchors.
3
Virions Trapped
New viruses stay glued to cells and cannot spread.
Neuraminidase Inhibitor
Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) High Yield
Mechanism of Action: Competitively inhibits viral neuraminidase, preventing viral release from host cells.
Primary Uses: Treatment & prophylaxis of Influenza A & B (must start within 48h of symptoms).
Side Effects (ADRs): Nausea, vomiting, neuropsychiatric events (delirium).
Nursing Pearl: Administer with food to minimize GI distress.
Endonuclease Inhibitor
Baloxavir Marboxil
Mechanism of Action: Inhibits cap-dependent endonuclease within the viral polymerase complex.
Primary Uses: Single-dose oral treatment for acute uncomplicated influenza.
Side Effects (ADRs): Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, headache.
Nursing Pearl: Taken as a single oral dose; avoid co-administration with dairy/polyvalent cations.
RNA Polymerase Inhibitor
Remdesivir
Mechanism of Action: Adenosine analog that prematurely terminates viral RNA replication (RdRp).
Primary Uses: Hospitalized COVID-19 patients requiring supplemental oxygen.
Side Effects (ADRs): Elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST), nausea, hypersensitivity.
Nursing Pearl: Monitor renal function (eGFR) and liver function panels closely.

💡 Mnemonic for Respiratory Agents: "O.B.R."

Oseltamivir (give in 48h) | Baloxavir (single dose) | Remdesivir (COVID RdRp blocker)

🎗️ 2. HIV Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs vs. NtRTIs)

HIV Core

Fake building blocks incorporated into growing viral DNA strands to trigger immediate DNA chain termination.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: NRTI Chain Termination
1
Intracellular Activation
NRTIs require 3 phosphorylation steps; NtRTIs need only 2.
2
Fake Incorporation (Target)
Reverse transcriptase inserts drug into viral DNA chain.
3
Chain Terminated
Lacking the 3'-OH tail, further DNA extension stops completely.
Nucleoside RTI (NRTI)
Abacavir (ABC) Critical Test Alert
Mechanism of Action: Competitively inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase after tri-phosphorylation.
Primary Uses: Cornerstone backbone agent in cART combination regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Severe hypersensitivity reaction, lactic acidosis, GI upset.
Nursing Pearl: Mandatory HLA-B*5701 genetic testing prior to prescribing!
Nucleotide RTI (NtRTI)
Tenofovir (TDF / TAF)
Mechanism of Action: Pre-phosphorylated nucleotide analog causing viral DNA chain termination.
Primary Uses: Frontline HIV cART, PrEP, and chronic Hepatitis B suppression.
Side Effects (ADRs): Renal proximal tubule failure (Fanconi syndrome) & bone mineral loss (TDF).
Nursing Pearl: Monitor serum creatinine, BUN, and urine glucose/protein regularly.
Nucleoside RTI (NRTI)
Zidovudine (AZT)
Mechanism of Action: Thymidine analog stopping reverse transcriptase.
Primary Uses: Prevention of perinatal maternal-fetal HIV transmission during labor.
Side Effects (ADRs): Severe macrocytic anemia, bone marrow suppression, myopathy.
Nursing Pearl: Monitor complete blood count (CBC) weekly for hemoglobin drops.

💡 Mnemonic for NRTIs / NtRTIs: "BACKBONE"

Builds cART foundation | Abacavir (check HLA-B*5701) | Chain termination | Kills viral replication

🧬 3. Hepatitis B & C Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs)

Hepatitis

Target viral polymerases, replication complexes, and protease enzymes to achieve permanent virologic cure (SVR).

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: HCV DAA Targets (-previr, -asvir, -buvir)
1
NS3/4A Protease (-previr)
Blocks viral polyprotein cleavage.
2
NS5A Complex (-asvir)
Stops RNA replication assembly.
3
NS5B Polymerase (-buvir)
Stops RNA chain extension.
NS5B Polymerase Inhibitor
Sofosbuvir Black Box Warning
Mechanism of Action: Nucleotide analog inhibiting HCV NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
Primary Uses: Pan-genotypic backbone for curative Hepatitis C treatment regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Fatigue, headache, severe symptomatic bradycardia.
Nursing Pearl: NEVER combine with Amiodarone (causes fatal heart block!).
Guanosine Analog Adjunct
Ribavirin
Mechanism of Action: Depletes GTP pools, causing lethal mutations in viral RNA.
Primary Uses: Combination therapy for HCV and severe viral infections.
Side Effects (ADRs): Dose-dependent hemolytic anemia, severe teratogenicity.
Nursing Pearl: Pregnancy Category X; requires 2 forms of birth control during & 6 months post-treatment.
HBV Polymerase Blocker
Entecavir
Mechanism of Action: Inhibits HBV polymerase priming, reverse transcription, and DNA strand elongation.
Primary Uses: First-line oral therapy for chronic Hepatitis B suppression.
Side Effects (ADRs): Headaches, fatigue, elevated liver transaminases, lactic acidosis.
Nursing Pearl: Take on an empty stomach for optimal systemic absorption.

💡 Mnemonic for HCV Suffixes: "P.A.B."

Previr = Protease | Asvir = Assembly (NS5A) | Buvir = Building block (NS5B Polymerase)

🔬 4. Herpesvirus (HSV/VZV) & Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Agents

Herpes / CMV

Nucleoside analogues requiring viral or cellular kinase activation to inhibit viral DNA polymerase in HSV, VZV, and CMV.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: Acyclovir Kinase Activation
1
Viral Kinase Entry
Activated by viral Thymidine Kinase inside infected cells.
2
Triphosphate Conversion (Target)
Host enzymes convert drug into active triphosphate form.
3
DNA Polymerase Blocked
Stops viral DNA chain extension and replication.
Thymidine Kinase-Dependent
Acyclovir Renal Hazard
Mechanism of Action: Viral thymidine kinase activation followed by selective viral DNA polymerase inhibition.
Primary Uses: Genital herpes outbreaks, chickenpox, shingles (VZV), HSV encephalitis.
Side Effects (ADRs): Crystalline nephropathy (IV administration), neurotoxicity, nausea.
Nursing Pearl: Run IV normal saline hydration alongside infusion to prevent kidney crystal damage!
UL97 Kinase-Dependent
Ganciclovir
Mechanism of Action: Activated by viral UL97 kinase in CMV to inhibit viral DNA polymerase.
Primary Uses: Treatment and prophylaxis of severe CMV retinitis in immunocompromised hosts.
Side Effects (ADRs): Severe bone marrow suppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia).
Nursing Pearl: Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) with differential weekly during therapy.
Non-Kinase Dependent
Foscarnet
Mechanism of Action: Direct viral DNA polymerase inhibitor requiring zero kinase activation.
Primary Uses: Ganciclovir-resistant CMV retinitis and acyclovir-resistant HSV infections.
Side Effects (ADRs): Severe nephrotoxicity, profound electrolyte wasting (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia).
Nursing Pearl: Monitor serum electrolytes continuously and administer aggressive IV pre-hydration.

💡 Mnemonic for Herpes/CMV: "CRYSTALS" (Acyclovir ADR)

Crystals in renal tubules | Reduced blood counts (Ganciclovir) | Yields electrolyte waste (Foscarnet)

🎯 5. Advanced HIV Classes (INSTIs, PIs & NNRTIs)

Advanced HIV

Inhibit viral integration into human chromosomes, block protein cleavage, or bind allosterically to reverse transcriptase.

⚡ Simplified MOA Flowchart: Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)
1
Viral cDNA Formed
Reverse transcriptase creates double-stranded viral DNA.
2
Magnesium Chelated (Target)
INSTIs bind Mg²⁺ ions at the active site of integrase.
3
Integration Blocked
Viral DNA cannot splice into human host chromosomes.
Integrase Inhibitor (INSTI)
Dolutegravir First-Line Anchor
Mechanism of Action: Chelates magnesium ions at the catalytic core of HIV integrase enzyme.
Primary Uses: Preferred frontline anchor agent in modern combination cART regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Insomnia, headache, weight gain, elevated serum creatinine.
Nursing Pearl: High genetic barrier to viral drug resistance; safe in pregnancy.
Protease Inhibitor (PI)
Darunavir
Mechanism of Action: Inhibits HIV protease from cleaving Gag-Pol polyproteins into mature units.
Primary Uses: Treatment-experienced HIV infection and salvage regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Hyperglycemia/diabetes, lipodystrophy ("buffalo hump"), dyslipidemia.
Nursing Pearl: Always co-administered with a pharmacokinetic booster (Ritonavir or Cobicistat).
Non-Nucleoside RTI (NNRTI)
Efavirenz
Mechanism of Action: Binds allosterically to reverse transcriptase pocket, locking enzyme shape.
Primary Uses: Second-line or resource-limited HIV cART regimens.
Side Effects (ADRs): Vivid nightmares, insomnia, depression, Stevens-Johnson rash.
Nursing Pearl: Administer at bedtime on an empty stomach to minimize CNS psychiatric side effects.

🚫 Critical Nursing Exam Safety Warnings

• Hepatitis B Reactivation: Test for HBV before starting or stopping Tenofovir/Lamivudine regimens.
• Acyclovir Hydration: Prevent acute tubular kidney injury with high-volume IV normal saline.
• Sofosbuvir + Amiodarone: Absolute contraindication due to lethal cardiac arrest/bradycardia risk.
