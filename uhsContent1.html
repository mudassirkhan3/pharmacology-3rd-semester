1 Essential Pharmacology Terminologies

UHS SLO #1

Building a professional nursing foundation requires absolute precision in medical terminology.

Pharmacology (Pharmacon + Logos)

The study of drugs, their properties, biochemical effects, mechanisms of action, and therapeutic uses in living organisms.

Drug / Medication

Any chemical substance that alters biological function to diagnose, cure, treat, mitigate, or prevent disease.

Pharmacokinetics (PK)

What the body does to the drug (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion - ADME).

Pharmacodynamics (PD)

What the drug does to the body (Receptor binding, mechanism of action, therapeutic & toxic effects).

Pharmacotherapeutics

The clinical application of drugs to treat, prevent, or diagnose diseases in human patients.

Toxicology

The study of adverse, harmful, or lethal effects of chemical agents and drug overdoses on biological systems.

Pharmacy

The health science discipline concerned with preparing, compounding, dispensing, and ensuring safe drug usage.

Posology

The branch of pharmacology devoted to calculating appropriate drug doses based on age, body mass, and health condition.

2 Brief History & Milestones of Pharmacology

UHS SLO #2
Ancient Era (3000 BCE)

Ebers Papyrus & Natural Herbal Remedies

Ancient civilizations recorded herbal treatments on papyrus scripts. Remedies were empirical, based on plant extracts, minerals, and animal tissues without knowing molecular mechanisms.

Middle Ages (800–1200 CE)

Islamic Golden Age & Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

Ibn Sina wrote The Canon of Medicine, establishing standardized clinical drug testing, dosage guidelines, and systematic apothecary practices across the civilized world.

16th Century

Paracelsus — Father of Toxicology

Pioneered dose-response principles: "All substances are poisons; there is none which is not a poison. The right dose differentiates a poison from a remedy."

19th Century (1805)

Morphine Isolation & Modern Experimental Pharmacology

Friedrich Sertürner isolated pure morphine crystals from opium poppy, launching modern pure chemical therapeutics and laboratory pharmacology.

20th Century to Present

Antibiotics, Recombinant DNA & Targeted Therapies

Alexander Fleming discovered Penicillin (1928). Genetic engineering produced human insulin, monoclonal antibodies, and personalized genomic medicine.

3 Purposes of Medication Administration

UHS SLO #3
🔍

1. Diagnostic Purpose

Used to confirm or identify pathological conditions (e.g., Radio-opaque contrast media for X-rays/CT scans, Tuberculin PPD skin test).

🦠

2. Curative Purpose

Eliminates pathogen or disease completely from the body (e.g., Antibiotics clearing bacterial infections like Penicillin for Strep throat).

🩺

3. Symptomatic / Palliative

Relieves pain and distressing symptoms without curing underlying cause (e.g., Paracetamol for fever, Morphine in end-stage cancer).

🛡️

4. Prophylactic / Preventive

Prevents onset of disease or surgical complications (e.g., Vaccines, Pre-operative prophylactic IV antibiotics).

🧪

5. Replacement / Substitutive

Replaces essential endogenous molecules deficient in the body (e.g., Insulin in Type 1 Diabetes, Levothyroxine in hypothyroidism).

⚙️

6. Health Maintenance / Supportive

Maintains normal physiological functions until body recovers (e.g., Antihypertensives, Multivitamins, Electrolyte fluids).

5 Classification Systems of Drugs

UHS SLO #5
1. By Body System Target

Groups drugs based on the principal anatomical system affected.

  • Cardiovascular Drugs (Antihypertensives)
  • Central Nervous System Drugs (Sedatives)
  • Gastrointestinal Drugs (Antacids)
2. Therapeutic Classification

Groups drugs based on the clinical intent or usefulness in treating disease.

  • Antihypertensives (Lowers BP)
  • Analgesics (Relieves Pain)
  • Antipyretics (Reduces Fever)
3. Pharmacological Classification

Groups drugs based on precise biochemical mechanisms of action at tissue level.

  • Beta-Blockers (Propranolol)
  • ACE Inhibitors (Captopril)
  • Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine)

Understanding the 3 Types of Drug Names

1. Chemical Name: Atomic/molecular structure layout (e.g., N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetamide).
2. Generic (Non-Proprietary) Name: Official official name given by USAN/WHO (e.g., Paracetamol or Acetaminophen).
3. Brand (Trade/Proprietary) Name: Trademark name assigned by manufacturer (e.g., Panadol®, Calpol®).

6 The 3 Types of Hospital Drug Supply Systems

UHS SLO #6
System 1

Stock Supply System (Floor Stock)

Medications are stocked in bulk on nursing units/wards. Nurses retrieve doses directly from ward stock cabinets for assigned patients.

✔ Advantage: Immediate availability for emergency needs.

✖ Disadvantage: High risk of medication errors, drug waste, and inventory loss.

System 2

Individual Prescription Supply

Hospital pharmacy dispenses a 3 to 7-day drug supply for each specific patient upon receiving physician's prescription order.

✔ Advantage: Pharmacist reviews prescription before dispensing.

✖ Disadvantage: Delays in initial dose administration; returned unused drugs.

System 3

Unit-Dose Supply System (Gold Standard)

Pharmacy dispenses individually packaged, pre-labeled single doses for a 24-hour period, placed in patient-specific drawer carts.

✔ Advantage: Lowest medication error rate (<1%), maximum safety!

✖ Disadvantage: Requires high pharmacy staffing & specialized equipment.

7 Drug Standards & Legislation Safety

UHS SLO #7

1. Official Pharmacopeia Standards

Official legal books containing national standards for drug purity, potency, strength, identity, and quality control:

  • USP (United States Pharmacopeia): Official US quality benchmark.
  • BP (British Pharmacopoeia): Official UK quality standard.
  • IP (International Pharmacopoeia): Published by WHO.

2. Drug Legislation & Controlled Substances

Laws regulate drug manufacturing, sales, prescribing authority, and abuse prevention:

  • Controlled Substances Act: Categorizes narcotics/addictives into Schedules (Schedule I to V) based on abuse potential.
  • DRAP (Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan): Regulates drug registration, quality, pricing, and safety laws in Pakistan under the Drugs Act 1976.

9 Preparing Clinical Nursing Drug Cards

UHS SLO #9 & Clinical Skill

Drug cards are concise study and bedside reference tools mandatory for nursing clinical ward rotations. Here is the standardized format required by nursing faculty:

DRUG CARD: METOPROLOL TARTRATE Classification: Therapeutic: Antihypertensive | Pharmacological: Beta-1 Blocker
Trade: Lopressor®
1. Route & Dose Range

50–100 mg PO daily in 1 or 2 divided doses. Emergency IV: 5 mg every 2 mins (Max 15 mg).

2. Action / Mechanism

Selectively blocks Beta-1 adrenergic receptors in heart muscle, reducing heart rate & cardiac output.

3. Indications

Hypertension, Angina Pectoris, Post-Myocardial Infarction cardioprotection.

4. Contraindications

Sinus Bradycardia (<50 bpm), Cardiogenic shock, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block, Severe asthma.

5. Key Adverse Effects

Bradycardia, Hypotension, Fatigue, Dizziness, Erectile dysfunction, Masked hypoglycemia symptoms.

6. High-Yield Nursing Responsibilities
  • Pre-Assessment: Check apical pulse & BP before giving. Hold dose if HR < 60 bpm or Systolic BP < 90 mmHg.
  • Patient Teaching: Warn patient never to stop drug abruptly (prevents rebound hypertension/angina). Teach orthostatic blood pressure change precautions.
