3 Mechanism of Action (MOA)

Interactive Pathways

Monoclonal antibodies target specific cell receptors, soluble growth factors, or immune checkpoints. Select a class below to view its specific mechanism:

Pathway: Rituximab (The Flag & Recruit)
Step 1: CD20 Binding Binds directly to surface CD20 antigens on B-cells.
Step 2: Immune Flag Flags cells for destruction via Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC).
Step 3: Cellular Lysis Triggers ADCC, leading to rapid B-cell lysis and tumor destruction.
Pathway: Trastuzumab (The Signal Jammer)
Step 1: HER2 Attachment Attaches to surface HER2 receptors on breast and gastric cancer cells.
Step 2: Factor Block Blocks growth factor binding and halts intracellular survival signals.
Step 3: Cell Cycle Arrest Arrests cell cycle progression and induces targeted apoptosis.
Pathway: Bevacizumab (The Angiogenesis Inhibitor)
Step 1: VEGF Neutralization Binds directly to circulating Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF).
Step 2: Receptor Block Prevents VEGF from interacting with endothelial cell receptors.
Step 3: Tumor Starvation Inhibits new blood vessel formation, starving the tumor of oxygen/nutrients.
Pathway: Checkpoint Inhibitors (The Brake Release)
Step 1: Target Checkpoint Pembrolizumab/Nivolumab block PD-1 checkpoints on T-cells.
Step 2: Brake Release Releases inhibitory immune "brakes" preventing T-cell inactivation.
Step 3: Immune Attack Unleashes cytotoxic T-cells to recognize and destroy cancer cells.

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

Monoclonal antibodies carry targeted toxicities including immune reactions and organ-specific strains:

1. Infusion-Related Reactions — Common

Fever, chills, rigors, flushing, bronchospasm, and hypotension (most common with Rituximab and Cetuximab during first infusions).

2. Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs) — Important / Serious

Autoimmune inflammatory conditions affecting the lungs (pneumonitis), colon (colitis), skin, and endocrine glands (Checkpoint inhibitors).

3. Cardiotoxicity — Important / Serious

Decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and heart failure (Trastuzumab).

4. Vascular & Dermatologic Toxicities — Common

Severe hypertension, proteinuria, delayed wound healing (Bevacizumab); acneiform rash (Cetuximab).

8 Important Complications & Management Protocols

Serious Alerts
⚠️ Infusion Reaction Protocol

Severe reactions require immediate infusion interruption, antihistamines, corticosteroids, and antipyretics before restarting at a slower rate once symptoms resolve.

🛡️ Managing Checkpoint Toxicity

Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) caused by checkpoint inhibitors are managed by holding immunotherapy doses and administering systemic high-dose corticosteroids based on severity grade.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Rituximab

CD20 Targeted mAb
Key Info: Anti-CD20 targeted monoclonal antibody used primarily in B-cell malignancies.
MOA: Binds CD20 on B-cells, triggering Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC) and ADCC.
Uses: B-cell lymphomas and leukemias.
ADRs & Complications: Infusion-related reactions (rigors, hypotension, fever), tumor lysis syndrome risk.

Trastuzumab

HER2 Targeted mAb
Key Info: Targeted surface receptor antibody for HER2-overexpressing tumors.
MOA: Binds HER2 receptors, blocking growth factor signaling and inducing apoptosis.
Uses: HER2-positive breast and gastric cancers.
ADRs & Complications: Cardiotoxicity (decreased LVEF / heart failure), infusion reactions.
