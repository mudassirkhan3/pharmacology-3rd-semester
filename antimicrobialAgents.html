3 H. pylori Eradication Therapies

Protocols

Because single antibiotics fail to cure H. pylori due to resistance and acidity, multi-drug combinations are mandatory:

💊 Standard Triple Therapy (14 Days)

Combination: PPI (e.g., Omeprazole) + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin.

Role: PPI raises gastric pH to stabilize antibiotics. Amoxicillin weakens cell walls; Clarithromycin halts protein synthesis.

🛡️ Bismuth Quadruple Therapy (14 Days)

Combination: PPI + Bismuth Subsalicylate + Metronidazole + Tetracycline.

Role: First-line option when clarithromycin resistance is high. Bismuth coats ulcer beds with dual antibacterial coverage.

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects
1. Gastrointestinal Distress

Nausea, abdominal cramping, metallic taste, and antibiotic-associated diarrhea.

2. Bismuth Subsalicylate Effect

Harmless darkening of the stool and black tongue due to bismuth sulfide formation.

3. Metronidazole & Alcohol Interaction

Severe flushing, vomiting, and headache if alcohol is consumed during therapy (disulfiram-like reaction).

8 Pharmacokinetics

PK Profiles
💊 Oral Administration

Regimens are administered orally. Luminal drugs (like Rifaximin and oral Vancomycin) remain unabsorbed with minimal systemic bioavailability (<1%).

⚙️ Hepatic Metabolism

Systemic components like Clarithromycin and Metronidazole undergo hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4 enzymes.

9 Key Nursing Takeaways & Patient Safety

Patient Safety
1. Enforce Complete Regimen Adherence

Emphasize that patients must complete the full 14-day course of triple or quadruple therapy even if symptoms improve, to prevent treatment failure.

2. Explain Harmless Bismuth Effects

Reassure patients that black stools and dark tongues are normal, harmless side effects of bismuth subsalicylate.

3. Verify Oral Route for C. diff

Confirm that Vancomycin is ordered orally (never IV) for treating *C. diff* colitis.

4. Prohibit Alcohol with Metronidazole

Warn patients to avoid all alcohol during and for 48 hours post-Metronidazole therapy to avoid severe disulfiram reactions.
