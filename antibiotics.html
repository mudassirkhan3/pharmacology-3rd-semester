Anticancer Antibiotics

Cytotoxic agents derived from soil microorganisms that exert potent anti-tumor effects through DNA intercalation, topoisomerase inhibition, and free radical generation.

  • Mitomycin C

Clinical Notes: Acts as a bifunctional DNA cross-linking agent; highly effective against hypoxic tumor cells.

3 Mechanism of Action (MOA)

Interactive Pathways

Anticancer antibiotics are largely cell-cycle phase non-specific agents that disrupt DNA integrity through distinct chemical pathways. Select a class below to view its specific mechanism:

Pathway: Anthracyclines (DNA Wedge & Enzyme Trap)
Step 1: Intercalation Slips directly between DNA base pairs, unwinding the double helix structure.
Step 2: Topoisomerase II Trap Traps Topoisomerase II enzyme, causing lethal DNA double-strand breaks.
Step 3: Free Radicals Generates destructive iron-mediated reactive oxygen species that stress cell structures.
Pathway: Bleomycin (Oxidative Slicer)
Step 1: Complex Formation Binds directly to DNA and intracellular iron molecules.
Step 2: Strand Slicing Generates superoxide and hydroxide free radicals that slice open DNA strands.
Step 3: G2 Phase Arrest Cell stalls and is trapped in the G2 phase, unable to complete mitosis.
Pathway: Actinomycin D (Transcription Block)
Step 1: GC Binding Intercalates tightly between adjacent guanine-cytosine base pairs.
Step 2: Polymerase Barrier Acts as a physical barrier blocking RNA polymerase movement.
Step 3: Protein Halt Completely halts messenger RNA synthesis and vital protein production.

4 Clinical Uses

Indications

Anticancer antibiotics are foundational elements in curative combination regimens for various cancers:

01 Solid Tumors: Breast cancer, sarcomas, and lung cancer (Doxorubicin)
02 Hematological Malignancies: Acute leukemias, Hodgkin and Non-Hodgkin lymphomas
03 Testicular Cancer & Squamous Cell Carcinomas (Bleomycin)
04 Pediatric Malignancies: Wilms tumor, Rhabdomyosarcoma, and Ewing sarcoma (Actinomycin D)

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

These agents produce significant organ-specific toxicities that require careful clinical monitoring:

1. Cardiotoxicity — Important / Serious

Acute ECG changes and cumulative, irreversible congestive heart failure (Anthracyclines).

2. Pulmonary Toxicity — Important / Serious

Progressive interstitial pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis (Bleomycin).

3. Bone Marrow Suppression — Common

Severe myelosuppression, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia (except Bleomycin, which spares bone marrow).

4. Dermatological & Local Toxicity — Common

Severe vesicant tissue necrosis upon extravasation, complete alopecia, and radiation recall reactions.

8 Important Complications & Rescues

Serious Alerts
🛡️ Dexrazoxane Cardioprotection

To prevent cumulative, irreversible Doxorubicin-induced cardiomyopathy in patients requiring high lifetime doses, the iron-chelating agent Dexrazoxane is administered as a cardioprotectant.

⚠️ Severe Extravasation Risk

Anthracyclines and Mitomycin C are potent vesicants. Infiltration into subcutaneous tissue causes severe local tissue destruction, necrosis, and ulceration requiring immediate management.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Doxorubicin

Anthracycline Subclass
Key Info: Highly potent broad-spectrum antineoplastic antibiotic with a cumulative lifetime dose limit.
MOA: DNA intercalation, topoisomerase II inhibition, and reactive oxygen species generation.
Uses: Breast cancer, sarcomas, lymphomas, and acute leukemias.
ADRs & Complications: Irreversible congestive heart failure, severe myelosuppression, complete alopecia, red-orange urine discoloration, and vesicant necrosis.

Bleomycin

Glycopeptide Subclass
Key Info: Radiomimetic peptide that uniquely spares the bone marrow.
MOA: Generates oxygen free radicals that slice open DNA strands, arresting cells in the G2 phase.
Uses: Testicular cancer, squamous cell carcinomas, and Hodgkin lymphoma.
ADRs & Complications: Progressive pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial pneumonitis, mucocutaneous reactions, and minimal myelosuppression.
