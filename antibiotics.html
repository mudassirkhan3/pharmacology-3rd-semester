[CLASS DEFINITION]

1. Introduction & Overview

Engineered laboratory-produced proteins designed to bind specifically to antigens on cancer cell surfaces or immune checkpoint proteins, delivering precise targeted cytotoxicity, immune-mediated destruction, or receptor blockade.

Monoclonal antibodies represent a pinnacle of targeted oncologic therapy. Unlike traditional cytotoxic chemotherapy that affects all rapidly dividing cells, mAbs are programmed to recognize specific molecular targets on cancer cells or unleash the patient's own immune system to destroy tumors while sparing normal healthy tissue.

[CLASS OVERVIEW]

2. Classification & Prototype Drugs

Monoclonal antibodies are categorized based on their structural targets and mechanism of action:

🎯 Targeted Surface Antigen mAbs

Prototypes: Rituximab (CD20), Trastuzumab (HER2), Cetuximab (EGFR).

(Bind directly to growth receptors or tumor-specific proteins to block proliferation and trigger immune attack.)

🩸 Angiogenesis Inhibitor mAbs

Prototype: Bevacizumab.

(Binds to Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor [VEGF], preventing blood vessel formation supplying the tumor.)

⚡ Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

Prototypes: Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab (PD-1 inhibitors), Ipilimumab (CTLA-4 inhibitor).

(Block inhibitory pathways on T-cells, enabling the immune system to attack cancer cells.)

🚀 Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

Prototypes: Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), Brentuximab vedotin.

(mAbs linked to potent cytotoxic payloads, acting as targeted "guided missiles".)

Rituximab

CD20 TARGETED MAB

CLASSIFICATION: Targeted Surface Antigen Monoclonal Antibody (Anti-CD20)

[MOA]

Mechanism of Action

The Flag & Recruit: Rituximab binds to CD20 on B-cells, flagging them for destruction via Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC) and Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC).

[MOA]

Binds directly to Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF), preventing VEGF from interacting with its endothelial receptors. This starves the tumor by inhibiting tumor blood vessel formation (angiogenesis).

[DRUG GROUP 05]

Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab & Ipilimumab

IMMUNE CHECKPOINT INHIBITORS

CLASSIFICATION: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Monoclonal Antibodies (Anti-PD-1 / Anti-CTLA-4)

[MOA]

Mechanism of Action

The Brake Release: Pembrolizumab and Nivolumab block PD-1 checkpoints (while Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4), releasing the "brakes" on T-cells so they can recognize and destroy cancer cells.

[ADRs & COMPLICATIONS]

Adverse Drug Reactions & Management

  • Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs): Autoimmune inflammatory conditions affecting the lungs (pneumonitis), colon (colitis), skin, and endocrine glands.
⚠️ Managing Checkpoint Toxicity: Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) caused by checkpoint inhibitors are managed by holding immunotherapy doses and administering systemic high-dose corticosteroids depending on organ severity grade.
⚡ 30-SECOND REVISION
Pembrolizumab/Nivolumab → PD-1 Checkpoint Inhibitors → Removes T-cell brakes → Treats Melanoma, Lung & Renal Cancers → Key Risk: Autoimmune irAEs (Pneumonitis, Colitis) managed via Corticosteroids.
[DRUG GROUP 06]

Trastuzumab Deruxtecan & Brentuximab Vedotin

ANTIBODY-DRUG CONJUGATES (ADCS)

CLASSIFICATION: Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

[MOA]

Mechanism of Action

Monoclonal antibodies linked to potent cytotoxic payloads, acting as targeted "guided missiles" that deliver high concentrations of chemotherapy directly into antigen-expressing tumor cells while avoiding healthy tissues.

⚡ 30-SECOND REVISION
ADCs → mAb + Cytotoxic Payload → Targeted "guided missile" delivery → Delivers potent chemo exclusively inside antigen-positive tumor cells.

[PHARMACOKINETICS]

6. Pharmacokinetics

  • Administration: Administered via intravenous (IV) infusion or subcutaneous (SC) injection. Large protein structures prevent oral absorption.
  • Distribution: Confined primarily to vascular and interstitial spaces with slow clearance mediated by target binding and the reticuloendothelial system.
  • Elimination: Degraded into peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways rather than hepatic CYP450 metabolism, resulting in long half-lives (often weeks).
[MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS]

7. Agent-Specific Complications & Management

⚠️ Infusion Reaction Protocols

Severe infusion reactions require immediate interruption of the infusion, administration of antihistamines, corticosteroids, and antipyretics, and supportive care before restarting at a slower rate once symptoms resolve.

🛡️ Managing Checkpoint Toxicity

Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) caused by checkpoint inhibitors are managed by holding immunotherapy doses and administering systemic high-dose corticosteroids depending on organ severity grade.

[PATIENT SAFETY]

8. Key Nursing Takeaways & Patient Safety

  • Monitor Infusion Sites Closely: Stay vigilant during initial administrations, checking vital signs frequently and pre-medicating as ordered (e.g., diphenhydramine, acetaminophen).
  • Assess Baseline Cardiac Function: Perform baseline and serial echocardiograms or MUGA scans to monitor LVEF in patients receiving Trastuzumab.
  • Screen for Immune-Mediated Red Flags: Educate checkpoint inhibitor patients to report new or worsening shortness of breath, severe diarrhea, or rash immediately.
  • Monitor Blood Pressure & Urine Protein: Check blood pressure regularly and screen for proteinuria before administering Bevacizumab infusions.
  • Verify Pre-Medication Protocols: Ensure all mandated anti-allergic pre-medications are given prior to starting antibody infusions.
