9 Therapeutic Index & Therapeutic Margin

Drug Safety Evaluation

1. Therapeutic Index (TI)

The Therapeutic Index (TI) is a quantitative measure of a drug's relative safety. It compares the amount of a therapeutic agent that causes toxicity or harm to the amount that causes the desired therapeutic effect.

Mathematical Formula
Therapeutic Index (TI) = TD50 / ED50
ED50 (Median Effective Dose)

The dose of a drug required to produce the specified therapeutic response in 50% of the population.

TD50 (Median Toxic Dose)

The dose of a drug at which 50% of the population experiences toxic adverse effects or adverse symptoms.

The horizontal gap between the effective response curve (ED50) and the toxicity curve (TD50) represents the Therapeutic Index safety margin.

2. Therapeutic Window & Margin of Safety

The Therapeutic Window (or Margin of Safety) refers to the range of drug doses or blood plasma concentrations that provides safe therapeutic efficacy without inducing toxic side effects.

💡 Key Distinction: While Therapeutic Index (TI) is a ratio ($\text{TD}_{50} / \text{ED}_{50}$), the Therapeutic Window represents the actual range of plasma concentration (e.g., $10\text{--}20\text{ mcg/mL}$) between the minimum effective concentration and toxic threshold.

3. Clinical Application & Drug Classification

Wide Therapeutic Index Drugs

Safe to administer with standard dosing. Large dosage increases rarely cause life-threatening toxicity.

• Examples: Penicillin, Amoxicillin, Paracetamol (Acetaminophen).
Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs

High-risk medications where minor dosage variations or drug interactions lead directly to toxic reactions or treatment failure.

• Examples: Digoxin, Warfarin, Lithium, Theophylline, Phenytoin.
Nursing Practice Application: Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM)

For patients prescribed drugs with a Narrow Therapeutic Index, nurses must perform Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM):

  • Trough Level: Draw blood samples immediately before administering the next dose (measures lowest drug concentration).
  • Peak Level: Draw blood samples at a specified time post-administration (measures maximum drug concentration to avoid toxicity).
