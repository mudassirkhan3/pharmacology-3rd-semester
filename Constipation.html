Laxatives & Cathartics (Constipation Management)

Essential pharmacological agents designed to facilitate defecation by softening stools, increasing fecal mass, drawing water into the intestinal lumen, or stimulating bowel motility to relieve acute and chronic constipation.

1. Introduction to Bowel Motility & Defecation

Constipation is characterized by infrequent bowel movements, hard stools, or difficulty with defecation resulting from slow colonic transit, excessive water absorption, or pelvic floor dysfunction. Laxatives and cathartics restore regular bowel function through distinct physiological mechanisms tailored to short-term relief or chronic management.

2. Classification & Prototype Drugs

🌾 Bulk-Forming Laxatives

Prototypes: Psyllium, Methylcellulose, Polycarbophil.

Characteristics: Natural or synthetic fibers that absorb water in the intestine, swelling to form a viscous gel that promotes peristalsis.

🧪 Osmotic & Saline Laxatives

Prototypes: Polyethylene Glycol (PEG), Lactulose, Magnesium Hydroxide, Sodium Phosphate.

Characteristics: Non-absorbable compounds that draw water into the lumen via osmotic pressure, softening stool and increasing pressure.

⚡ Stimulant Laxatives

Prototypes: Bisacodyl, Senna.

Characteristics: Direct irritants to the intestinal mucosa and enteric nerves that accelerate colonic peristalsis and fluid secretion.

💧 Stool Softeners & Lubricants

Prototypes: Docusate Sodium, Mineral Oil.

Characteristics: Surfactants that lower surface tension, allowing water and fat to penetrate stool, plus lubricants that coat fecal mass.

3. Mechanisms of Action

🌱 Bulk & Osmotic Actions

Bulk-formers retain water in stool, enlarging mass to stretch the bowel wall and trigger peristaltic reflexes. Osmotics create an osmotic gradient that pulls water into the colon.

⚡ Stimulants & Surfactants

Stimulants act directly on intestinal smooth muscle and nerve plexuses. Docusate acts as a detergent, lowering surface tension so water enters the fecal mass more easily.

4. Clinical Uses & Therapeutic Indications

  • Treatment of chronic idiopathic constipation and pregnancy-related constipation
  • Prevention and management of Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC)
  • Bowel evacuation and preparation prior to diagnostic colonoscopy, GI surgery, or radiological procedures
  • Prevention of straining in patients with myocardial infarction, aneurysms, or recent rectal surgery
  • Reduction of blood ammonia levels in hepatic encephalopathy (Lactulose)

💡 Mnemonic for Clinical Indications: "B-O-W-E-L"

Bowel prep before procedures | Opioid-induced constipation | Ward off straining after surgery | Encephalopathy ammonia reduction (Lactulose) | Long-term chronic constipation support

5. Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

⚠️ Gastrointestinal Side Effects

Abdominal cramping, flatulence, bloating, nausea, and diarrhea resulting from rapid fluid shifts or active peristalsis.

⚠️ Systemic & Electrolyte Risks

Electrolyte imbalances (dehydration, hypermagnesemia with magnesium-based laxatives), dependence, and atonic colon with chronic misuse of stimulant laxatives.

💡 Mnemonic for Laxative Complications: "C-R-A-M-P"

Cramping and abdominal pain | Rebound constipation from chronic stimulant abuse | Acute electrolyte shifts | Magnesium toxicity risk in renal impairment | Peristalsis over-stimulation

6. Pharmacokinetics & Absolute Contraindications

  • Onset Variations: Bulk-forming agents take 1 to 3 days to work. Osmotics take 1 to 2 days (or hours for high-dose bowel preps). Stimulants typically act within 6 to 12 hours.
  • Absolute Contraindications: Never administer laxatives in the presence of undiagnosed abdominal pain, acute surgical abdomen, appendicitis, nausea/vomiting, or mechanical bowel obstruction due to the risk of intestinal perforation.

7. Key Nursing Takeaways & Patient Safety

  • Promote Adequate Hydration: Instruct patients taking bulk-forming agents (like psyllium) to drink a full glass of water with each dose; inadequate fluid intake can cause esophageal or intestinal obstruction.
  • Assess Abdominal Status: Always auscultate bowel sounds, palpate the abdomen, and check the patient's last bowel movement history before administering any laxative.
  • Avoid Crush/Chew Errors: Educate patients not to crush or chew enteric-coated stimulant tablets (such as bisacodyl), as this destroys the protective coating and causes severe gastric irritation.
  • Discourage Chronic Stimulant Use: Advise against long-term, daily use of stimulant laxatives to prevent loss of normal bowel tone and laxative dependence.
