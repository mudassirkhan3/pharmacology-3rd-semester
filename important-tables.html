🦠 Chapter 31: Therapeutic Applications of Cotrimoxazole

Summary of clinical indications and therapeutic targets for sulfamethoxazole plus trimethoprim.

MRSA Infections

Highly effective for methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus skin and soft-tissue infections.

Respiratory Infections

Effective against Haemophilus influenzae and serves as an alternative treatment for Legionella pneumophila.

Pneumocystis jirovecii Pneumonia (PJP)

Primary choice therapy for AIDS-related PJP; standard prophylaxis for HIV patients with CD4+ counts <200 cells/mL.

Listeriosis

Effective in treating septicemia and meningitis caused by Listeria monocytogenes.

Urinary Tract & Prostate Infections

Concentrates in prostatic and vaginal fluids; highly effective for chronic lower urinary tract infections.

Gastrointestinal Infections

Indicated for shigellosis, nontyphoid salmonella, and chronic management of S. typhi carriers.

Characteristics, toxicities, and monitoring parameters for second-line tuberculosis drugs.

Drug Class / Agent Adverse Effects Clinical Comments & Monitoring
Fluoroquinolones GI intolerance, tendonitis, CNS toxicity, caffeine-like effects. Monitor LFTs, serum creatinine/BUN, and QT interval. Avoid concurrent ingestion with di- or trivalent cations (antacids/multivitamins).
Aminoglycosides & Capreomycin Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity (vestibular and auditory). Not available orally. Requires serial monitoring of renal function, vestibular function, and hearing.

Cycloserine Severe CNS toxicity (depression, anxiety, confusion, seizures). Requires strict psychiatric monitoring. Exacerbates seizures in epileptic patients. Monitor serum creatinine.
