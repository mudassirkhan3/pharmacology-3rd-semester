💉 Chapter 30: Therapeutic Applications of Aminoglycosides

Summary of clinical indications, synergy regimens, and specialized pathogen targets.

Tularemia

Acquired during rabbit-hunting season by hunters skinning infected animals. Pneumonic tularemia results from respiratory transmission or bacteremic lung seeding.

Gentamicin (Effective therapy)

Synergy with Beta-Lactams

Aminoglycosides are combined with beta-lactams to achieve bactericidal synergy for select serious infections.

Enterococcus (Ampicillin + Gentamicin) S. agalactiae (Ampicillin + Gentamicin) Brucella (Gentamicin + Doxycycline) Y. pestis (Streptomycin) F. tularensis (Gentamicin)

Pseudomonas aeruginosa Infections

Rarely attacks healthy individuals; occurs in patients with risk factors like recent antibiotic exposure, prolonged hospitalization, or bronchiectasis.

Tobramycin (UTI) Tobramycin + Antipseudomonal Beta-Lactam (Pneumonia)

💊 Chapter 30: Therapeutic Applications of Macrolides

Clinical indications and preferred regimens for atypical pathogens, respiratory infections, and MAC.

Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Erythromycin or penicillin is utilized to successfully eliminate the carrier state.

Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Chlamydial Infections

Azithromycin or doxycycline serve as the preferred therapeutic options across multiple chlamydial strains.

C. pneumoniae C. psittaci C. trachomatis

Legionnaires Disease (Legionellosis)

Undiagnosed and asymptomatic infections are common. Treated with preferred macrolide or fluoroquinolone regimens.

Legionella pneumophila Bordetella pertussis Campylobacter jejuni

Mycoplasma Pneumonia

Referred to as "atypical" pneumonia because causative mycoplasmas escape isolation via standard bacteriologic techniques.

Mycoplasma pneumoniae Ureaplasma urealyticum

Mycobacterium Avium Complex (MAC)

Clarithromycin + rifampin + ethambutol is preferred. Azithromycin serves as an alternative or once-weekly prophylaxis for AIDS patients.

Mycobacterium avium complex

🦠 Chapter 31: Therapeutic Applications of Cotrimoxazole

Clinical spectrum of sulfamethoxazole plus trimethoprim in systemic, urinary, and opportunistic infections.

MRSA Infections

Highly effective for methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus skin and soft-tissue infections.

S. aureus (MRSA)

Respiratory Infections

Effective against Haemophilus influenzae; serves as an alternative treatment choice for Legionella pneumophila.

H. influenzae Legionella pneumophila

Pneumocystis Jirovecii Pneumonia (PJP)

Primary choice therapy for AIDS-related PJP; standard prophylaxis for HIV patients with CD4+ counts <200 cells/mL.

P. jirovecii Toxoplasmosis gondii

Listeriosis

Ampicillin or cotrimoxazole is effective in treating septicemia and meningitis caused by Listeria monocytogenes.

Listeria monocytogenes

Prostate & Urinary Tract Infections

Trimethoprim concentrates in prostatic and vaginal fluids. Chronic urinary tract infections respond well to cotrimoxazole.

E. coli Proteus mirabilis Klebsiella

Gastrointestinal Infections

Useful in treating shigellosis, nontyphoid salmonella, and managing chronic carriers of Salmonella typhi.

Shigella species Salmonella typhi
