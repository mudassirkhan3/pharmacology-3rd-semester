Unit I: Introduction to Pharmacology — High-Yield Tables

Core pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic principles, drug standards, and medication supply systems extracted from Lippincott Pharmacology.

Table 1.1: Basic Pharmacological Terminologies & Principles

Terminologies Lippincott Definition & Clinical Principle Nursing Implication
Pharmacokinetics (PK) What the body does to the drug (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion - ADME). Monitor organ function (liver/kidneys) as altered ADME leads to toxicity.
Pharmacodynamics (PD) What the drug does to the body (Mechanism of action, receptor binding, dose-response). Assess therapeutic efficacy and monitor for expected vs. adverse physiological responses.
Agonist vs. Antagonist Agonist: Binds to receptor and activates a response.
Antagonist: Binds to receptor and blocks a response.		 Understand reversal agents (e.g., Naloxone as an opioid antagonist).
First-Pass Effect Hepatic inactivation of oral drugs before they reach systemic circulation. Oral doses are typically much higher than IV doses for drugs with high first-pass metabolism.

Table 1.2: Drug Legislation, Standards & Supply Systems

System / Standard Description & Mechanism Clinical Safety Focus
Unit-Dose Drug Distribution Pharmacy dispenses individual, pre-packaged doses labeled for specific patients for a 24-hour cycle. Reduces medication administration errors and saves nurse preparation time.
Stock Supply System Bulk medication containers stored on the nursing unit for general patient use. Higher risk of calculation and administration errors; requires strict inventory controls.
Controlled Substances Act Legal classification and scheduling of drugs with potential for abuse or dependence (Schedules I–V). Requires double-witness signature, secure count logs, and strict wastage documentation.

Unit II: Anti-Infective Pharmacology — High-Yield Tables

Major antibiotic classes, antiviral/antifungal mechanisms, and essential patient education guidelines.

Table 2.1: Major Antibacterial Drug Classes & Mechanisms

Antibiotic Class Mechanism of Action Prototype Drug Key Adverse Effects & Nursing Focus
Penicillins (Beta-Lactams) Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). Amoxicillin, Ampicillin, Piperacillin Allergic reactions / anaphylaxis. Assess penicillin allergy prior to first dose.
Cephalosporins Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis (bactericidal); broader spectrum across generations. Ceftriaxone, Cefepime Cross-allergenicity with penicillins (~10%). Monitor renal function.
Aminoglycosides Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding irreversibly to the 30S ribosomal subunit. Gentamicin, Tobramycin Ototoxicity and Nephrotoxicity. Monitor peak/trough serum levels and urine output.
Fluoroquinolones Inhibits bacterial DNA synthesis by targeting DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin Tendonitis and Achilles tendon rupture. Avoid in children and pregnant women.

Table 2.2: Antifungal, Antiviral & Antiparasitic Agents

Drug Category Prototype Drug Primary Clinical Indication Essential Nursing Interventions
Antifungal (Polyene) Amphotericin B Severe systemic fungal infections (cryptococcal meningitis, histoplasmosis). Infusion-related fever/chills ("shake and bake"). Premedicate with antipyretics/antihistamines; monitor K+ and renal function.
Antiviral (Purine Analog) Acyclovir Herpes simplex virus (HSV-1, HSV-2) and Varicella-Zoster virus (shingles). Ensure adequate hydration during IV infusion to prevent renal tubular crystal precipitation.
Antimalarial Chloroquine, Artemether-lumefantrine Prevention and treatment of acute malaria caused by Plasmodium species. Monitor baseline ophthalmologic exams during long-term therapy (retinal toxicity).

Unit III: Gastrointestinal System Drugs — High-Yield Tables

Pharmacological management of gastric acid disorders, motility disturbances, and elimination issues.

Table 3.1: Anti-Ulcer & Gastric Acid Secretion Modulators

Drug Class Mechanism of Action Prototype Drug Clinical Pearls & Nursing Responsibilities
Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) Irreversibly inhibits the H+/K+ ATPase enzyme system on parietal cells. Omeprazole, Pantoprazole Administer 30–60 minutes before breakfast. Long-term use linked to osteoporosis and C. diff risk.
H2 Receptor Blockers Competitively blocks histamine H2 receptors on parietal cells, reducing gastric acid secretion. Famotidine, Ranitidine Adjust dose in renal impairment; monitor for confusion in elderly patients.
Antacids Neutralizes gastric hydrochloric acid to form salt and water. Aluminum Hydroxide, Magnesium Hydroxide Separate from other oral medications by 1–2 hours to prevent altered drug absorption.

Table 3.2: Gastrointestinal Motility & Anti-Emetic Agents

Subclass Prototype Drug Mechanism / Indication Adverse Reactions & Cautions
Serotonin (5-HT3) Antagonists Ondansetron Blocks 5-HT3 receptors centrally in the chemoreceptor trigger zone (chemo-induced nausea). Headache, dizziness, and QT interval prolongation. Monitor cardiac rhythm in high-risk patients.
Osmotic Laxatives Polyethylene Glycol, Lactulose Draws water into the intestinal lumen via osmosis to soften stool and stimulate peristalsis. Electrolyte imbalances, abdominal cramping, and gas. Ensure high fluid intake.

Unit IV: Hematology System Drugs — High-Yield Tables

Coagulation regulators, antiplatelet therapies, and hematopoietic stimulants based on Lippincott clinical standards.

Table 4.1: Anticoagulants, Antiplatelets & Thrombolytics

Category Prototype Drug Mechanism of Action Monitoring Parameter & Reversal Agent
Anticoagulant (Injectable) Heparin (Unfractionated) Potentiates antithrombin III, inactivating thrombin and factor Xa. Monitor: aPTT (Target: 1.5–2.5x control).
Reversal: Protamine Sulfate.
Anticoagulant (Oral) Warfarin Inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase, blocking synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X. Monitor: PT / INR (Target: 2.0–3.0).
Reversal: Vitamin K / Fresh Frozen Plasma.
Antiplatelet Aspirin, Clopidogrel Irreversibly inhibits platelet cyclooxygenase, preventing aggregation. Monitor for GI bleeding, petechiae, and bruising. Discontinue 7 days prior to surgery.

Table 4.2: Hematopoietic Growth Factors & Anemia Therapy

Agent Prototype Drug Clinical Indication Key Nursing Assessments
Erythropoiesis-Stimulating Agent Epoetin Alfa Anemia associated with chronic renal failure or chemotherapy. Monitor blood pressure (risk of severe hypertension) and hemoglobin levels (do not exceed 11 g/dL).
Parenteral Iron Iron Dextran, Ferric Gluconate Iron deficiency anemia when oral iron is poorly tolerated or ineffective. Administer test dose for iron dextran to check for severe anaphylaxis; use Z-track method for IM injections.

Unit V: Anti-Neoplastic Drugs — High-Yield Tables

Cytotoxic chemotherapy classes, cellular targets, toxicities, and specialized nursing safety measures.

Table 5.1: Cytotoxic Anti-Neoplastic Drug Classes

Chemo Class Mechanism of Action Prototype Drug Dose-Limiting Toxicity
Alkylating Agents Cross-links DNA strands, inhibiting DNA and RNA synthesis during all cell cycle phases. Cyclophosphamide, Cisplatin Bone marrow suppression and hemorrhagic cystitis (for cyclophosphamide). Ensure hydration and mesna therapy.
Antimetabolites Mimics normal metabolites, disrupting DNA synthesis during the S-phase. Methotrexate, 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) Stomatitis, GI mucositis, and myelosuppression. Leucovorin rescue given with high-dose methotrexate.
Anthracyclines (Antibiotics) Intercalates between DNA base pairs and inhibits topoisomerase II, generating free radicals. Doxorubicin Cumulative, irreversible Cardiotoxicity. Monitor baseline and serial ejection fractions (MUGA scan / echocardiogram).

Table 5.2: Management of Chemotherapy Toxicities & Nursing Responsibilities

Toxicity / Complication Clinical Presentation Specialized Nursing Interventions
Myelosuppression (Neutropenia) Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) < 500/mm³, fever, risk of life-threatening sepsis. Institute neutropenic precautions (no fresh flowers, raw fruits/vegetables, strict hand hygiene); administer Filgrastim as ordered.
Extravasation (Vesicants) Pain, stinging, swelling, and potential tissue necrosis at IV administration site (e.g., Doxorubicin). Stop infusion immediately, leave cannula in place, aspirate residual drug, apply cold/warm compress per specific protocol, and notify oncologist.
Nausea & Vomiting (CINV) Acute or delayed emetic episodes triggered by chemoreceptor stimulation. Administer antiemetic prophylaxis (5-HT3 antagonist + NK1 receptor antagonist + Dexamethasone) 30 mins prior to chemotherapy infusion.
