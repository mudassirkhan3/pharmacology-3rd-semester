Alkylating Agents

High-yield study sheet covering mechanisms, prototype drugs, subclass specifics, toxicities, and essential nursing protocols.

1 Overview & Subclasses

Basics

Alkylating agents are cell-cycle phase non-specific chemotherapy drugs that attach alkyl groups to nuclear DNA, damaging fast-growing cancer and normal cells alike.

Subclass Prototypes Core Feature
Nitrogen Mustards Cyclophosphamide, Ifosfamide Broad-spectrum; requires hepatic bioactivation.
Nitrosoureas Carmustine (BCNU) Highly lipophilic; crosses the Blood-Brain Barrier.
Platinum Complexes Cisplatin, Carboplatin Heavy metal compounds creating rigid DNA cross-links.

2 Mechanism of Action (How They Work)

Pharmacology

Alkylating drugs transfer alkyl groups to the N7 position of guanine bases within DNA. Because they have dual reactive arms, they form intra- and inter-strand cross-links that lock the double helix together, halting replication and causing cell apoptosis.

1. Cyclophosphamide

Liver CYP450 enzymes convert this prodrug into active phosphoramide mustard (kills cancer) and acrolein (toxic bladder byproduct).

2. Nitrosoureas

Fat-soluble structure slips past the Blood-Brain Barrier to alkylate DNA and carbamoylate brain tumor proteins.

3. Cisplatin

Platinum atom binds guanine bases, creating rigid physical kinks in DNA that disrupt transcription.

Quick Visual: Cyclophosphamide Pathway
Step 1: Administered Safe, inactive prodrug form.
Step 2: Liver Activation CYP450 enzymes split drug.
Step 3: Active Results Kills DNA & releases acrolein.

3 Clinical Uses & Adverse Reactions

High-Yield
Main Indications

Leukemias, lymphomas (Hodgkin/Non-Hodgkin), multiple myeloma, and solid tumors (breast, ovary, lung, brain).

Mnemonic: "CANCER BLOC" CLL, Acute Leukemias, Non-Hodgkin, Carcinomas, Ependymomas, Rhabdomyosarcoma, Brain cancers, Lymphomas, Ovarian, Circulating myeloma.
Common Side Effects

Myelosuppression (nadir 7-14 days), severe GI nausea/mucositis, alopecia, and sterility.

Mnemonic: "BOMBS" Bone marrow depression | Oral mucositis | Mucositis | Bladder toxicity | Sterility.

4 Critical Toxicities & Nursing Takeaways

Clinical Safety
🩸 Hemorrhagic Cystitis

Caused by Acrolein buildup in the bladder with Cyclophosphamide. Prevented using aggressive fluids + Mesna uroprotection.

⚡ Organ Damage Alerts

Cisplatin causes nephrotoxicity & hearing loss; Busulfan causes lung fibrosis.

💧 Aggressive Hydration

Flush kidneys with copious IV/oral fluids before and after Cisplatin/Cyclophosphamide infusions.

🛡️ Premedicate Antiemetics

Give 5-HT3 antagonists (e.g., Ondansetron) proactively since agents like Cisplatin are highly emetogenic.
