2 Classification & Subclasses

Visual Flow

Click or scan through the subclasses below to review how alkylating agents are structured structurally and clinically:

Prototype Drugs: Cyclophosphamide, Ifosfamide, Melphalan, Chlorambucil, Bendamustine.

Clinical Notes: The most common broad-spectrum class; heavily utilized in lymphomas, leukemias, and solid organ malignancies.

Prototype Drugs: Carmustine (BCNU), Lomustine (CCNU), Streptozocin.

Clinical Notes: Highly fat-soluble (lipophilic), uniquely capable of crossing the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) to treat primary and metastatic brain tumors.

Prototype Drugs: Cisplatin, Carboplatin, Oxaliplatin.

Clinical Notes: Heavy metal coordination compounds that act similarly to classic alkylators by forming rigid DNA cross-links.

Prototype Drugs: Busulfan, Temozolomide, Dacarbazine.

Clinical Notes: Busulfan is used in bone marrow transplant conditioning regimens; Temozolomide treats brain tumors and melanoma.

4 Clinical Uses

Indications

Alkylating agents are foundational in treating hematological malignancies and solid tumors:

01 Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) & Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)
02 Hodgkin and Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas
03 Multiple Myeloma (e.g., Melphalan)
04 Solid tumors including ovarian, testicular, breast, lung, and brain tumors (gliomas).

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

Due to their non-specific mechanism, fast-renewing normal tissues are heavily impacted:

1. Bone Marrow Suppression (Myelosuppression) — Important / Serious

Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. Nadir typically occurs in 7–14 days.

2. Gastrointestinal Distress — Common

Severe nausea, vomiting, and stomatitis/mucositis.

3. Alopecia & Gonadal Dysfunction — Common

Complete or partial hair loss, permanent or temporary sterility, azoospermia, and premature ovarian failure.

4. Teratogenicity — Important / Serious

Highly embryotoxic and mutagenic; strictly contraindicated in pregnancy.

8 Important Complications

Serious Alerts
🩸 Hemorrhagic Cystitis

Caused by the metabolite Acrolein accumulating in the bladder with Cyclophosphamide and Ifosfamide. Leads to gross hematuria and bladder fibrosis.

⚡ Organ-Specific Toxicities

Cisplatin: Nephrotoxicity & ototoxicity.
Busulfan: Pulmonary fibrosis ("Busulfan lung").
Oxaliplatin: Acute cold-induced paresthesias.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Cyclophosphamide

Nitrogen Mustard Subclass
Key Info: Broad-spectrum alkylating prodrug requiring hepatic CYP450 bioactivation.
MOA: Metabolized to phosphoramide mustard (attacks tumor DNA).
Uses: Lymphomas, leukemias, breast and ovarian cancer.
ADRs & Complications: Myelosuppression, alopecia, and severe hemorrhagic cystitis caused by acrolein.

Cisplatin

Platinum Coordination Complex
Key Info: Heavy metal compound forming rigid intra-strand DNA cross-links.
MOA: Binds guanine bases, bending the DNA helix to induce apoptosis.
Uses: Testicular, ovarian, bladder, and lung cancers.
ADRs & Complications: Highly emetogenic, nephrotoxic, and ototoxic (hearing loss).

10 Specific Drug ADRs Summary

Targeted ADRs
Drug Name Important Specific ADRs
Cyclophosphamide / Ifosfamide Hemorrhagic cystitis, sterility, profound myelosuppression.
Cisplatin Severe nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity (tinnitus/hearing loss), severe nausea/vomiting.
Busulfan Pulmonary fibrosis ("Busulfan lung"), prolonged myelosuppression.
Oxaliplatin Acute cold-induced peripheral paresthesias (neuropathy).
