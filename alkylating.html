Alkylating Agents

The oldest and most widely used class of chemotherapy drugs. Learn their mechanisms, subclasses, organ side effects, and essential nursing protocols.

1 Introduction & Core Concepts

Basics

Alkylating agents were derived from sulfur mustard gases used in World War II. They work by attaching alkyl groups directly to cellular macromolecules, with nuclear DNA being the primary target.

Cell Cycle Non-Specific

Unlike other agents that attack cells only in a specific phase, alkylating drugs attack cells throughout all phases of the cell cycle. They destroy fast-growing cancer cells but also damage healthy fast-growing tissues (bone marrow, GI tract, hair follicles).

2 Classification & Prototype Drugs

Subclasses

The drug class is divided into five main structural subclasses:

Subclass Prototype Drugs Key Clinical Notes
Nitrogen Mustards Cyclophosphamide, Ifosfamide, Melphalan Most common broad-spectrum class for lymphomas & solid tumors.
Nitrosoureas Carmustine (BCNU), Lomustine Fat-soluble; uniquely crosses the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB).
Platinum Complexes Cisplatin, Carboplatin, Oxaliplatin Heavy metal compounds; form rigid DNA cross-links.
Alkyl Sulfonates Busulfan Used in bone marrow transplant conditioning regimens.
Triazines Temozolomide, Dacarbazine Oral/IV agents for melanoma and brain tumors.

3 Mechanism of Action & Simple Flowchart

Core Biology

Alkylating drugs attach alkyl groups to nuclear DNA strands, linking them together so the cell cannot replicate or divide.

Easy Study Flowchart

How Cyclophosphamide Works (Prodrug Activation)

1
Administered as Inactive Prodrug The drug is harmless when first injected into the bloodstream. It needs activation.
2
Hepatic CYP450 Activation Liver enzymes metabolize the drug, turning it into active components.
3
Dual Pathway Result (The Good & The Bad) • Phosphoramide Mustard: Enters cancer cells and destroys DNA.
• Acrolein: Toxic byproduct that irritates the bladder (causes hemorrhagic cystitis).
🧠 Nitrosoureas (Carmustine)

High fat solubility allows them to cross the Blood-Brain Barrier to treat brain tumors directly.

⚡ Platinum Complexes (Cisplatin)

Platinum binds to DNA guanine bases, creating heavy cross-links that bend and break DNA strands.

4 Clinical Uses & Therapeutic Indications

Indications

Alkylating agents are widely used in modern oncology protocols:

  • • Leukemias (CLL, CML) and Lymphomas (Hodgkin & Non-Hodgkin)
  • • Multiple Myeloma
  • • Solid tumors of the breast, ovary, lung, testes, and brain (gliomas).

High-Yield Mnemonic: "CANCER BLOC"

CLL | Acute Leukemias | Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma | Carcinomas | Ependymomas | Rhabdomyosarcoma | Brain Cancers | Lymphomas | Ovarian | Circulating Myeloma

5 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

Because normal fast-growing cells are affected alongside cancer cells, patients experience widespread toxicities:

  • ▪ Myelosuppression: Low white blood cells, platelets, and red blood cells (Nadir in 7–14 days).
  • ▪ Gastrointestinal Issues: Severe nausea, vomiting, and mouth sores (mucositis).
  • ▪ Alopecia: Temporary or complete hair loss.
  • ▪ Sterility: Risk of permanent or temporary infertility.
  • ▪ Teratogenicity: Highly toxic to fetuses; strict contraception required.

High-Yield Mnemonic: "BOMBS"

Bone marrow depression | Oral mucositis | Mucositis / Mutagenic risks | Bladder toxicity | Sterility

6 Agent-Specific Toxicities

Critical Alerts
🩸 Hemorrhagic Cystitis

Caused by Acrolein buildup in the bladder with Cyclophosphamide. Results in blood in urine and bladder irritation.

⚡ Specific Organ Damage

• Cisplatin: Kidney damage & hearing loss.
• Busulfan: Lung fibrosis.
• Oxaliplatin: Cold sensitivity.

7 Key Nursing Takeaways & Patient Safety

Clinical Practice
1. Aggressive Hydration Administer heavy IV fluids before and after Cisplatin or Cyclophosphamide to protect kidneys and flush out toxins.
2. Uroprotection with Mesna Give Mesna alongside high-dose Cyclophosphamide/Ifosfamide to bind and neutralize Acrolein in the bladder, preventing hemorrhagic cystitis.
3. Nadir & Blood Counts Monitor CBC regularly. Institute strict neutropenic precautions (handwashing, avoid sick contacts) if WBC counts drop.
4. Proactive Antiemetics Give anti-nausea meds (e.g., Ondansetron) before infusing drugs like Cisplatin, which cause severe nausea.
