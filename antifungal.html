Lippincott Framework Guide

Antifungal Pharmacology Guide

A simplified student study breakdown based on the Lippincott classification: Drugs for Systemic/Subcutaneous Mycoses vs. Drugs for Cutaneous/Superficial Mycoses.

1. Drugs for Systemic & Subcutaneous Mycoses

Used for deep-seated, life-threatening internal fungal infections (e.g., Candidiasis, Cryptococcal Meningitis, Aspergillosis) and deep subcutaneous tissue infections.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. Polyene Antibiotic
e.g. Amphotericin B
Heavy-hitter for life-threatening systemic fungal infections.
2. Pyrimidine Antimetabolite
e.g. Flucytosine (5-FC)
Synergistic partner given with Amphotericin B for fungal meningitis.
3. Systemic Azoles
Fluconazole Itraconazole Voriconazole Posaconazole
Broad-spectrum oral/IV agents.
4. Echinocandins
Caspofungin Micafungin Anidulafungin
First-line IV agents for invasive Candida & Aspergillus.

Simplified Mechanisms of Action (MOA)

⚙️ Polyene (Amphotericin B): "Poking Pores in the Membrane" IMP FOR EXAM

💡 Concept: Binds directly to fungal ergosterol (the fungal version of cholesterol) and tears open holes in the cell membrane so vital potassium leaks out.
1
Target Binding: Amphotericin B binds tightly to ergosterol in the fungal lipid membrane.
2
Pore Formation: Multiple molecules form transmembrane pores (holes) in the cell wall barrier.
3
Ion Leakage & Death: Essential ions (K+ and Mg2+) gush out, causing rapid osmotic lysis and fungal death.

⚙️ Systemic Azoles: "Blocking Ergosterol Construction"

💡 Concept: Blocks the fungal CYP450 enzyme that builds ergosterol, leaving the fungal membrane weak, leaky, and unable to grow.
1
Enzyme Blockade: Inhibits 14-alpha-demethylase (a fungal CYP450 enzyme).
2
Membrane Weakening: Halts ergosterol production, causing toxic sterol accumulation and membrane leakage (bacteriostatic/fungistatic).

⚙️ Echinocandins: "Destroying the Cell Wall Glue"

💡 Concept: Human cells have membranes, but fungal cells also have a hard cell wall. Echinocandins block the glue ($\beta$-1,3-D-glucan) that holds the cell wall together!
1
Glucan Synthase Inhibition: Blocks $\beta$-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase enzyme in the cell wall.
2
Cell Wall Collapse: Without glucan glue, the cell wall loses structural strength and ruptures under osmotic pressure.

⚙️ Pyrimidine Antimetabolite (Flucytosine): "DNA Fake-Out" IMP FOR EXAM

💡 Concept: Enters the fungus and converts into 5-FU (a chemotherapy toxin) to block fungal DNA and RNA synthesis.
1
Conversion to 5-FU: Converted inside fungal cells by cytosine deaminase into 5-fluorouracil (5-FU).
2
DNA/Protein Shut Off: Inhibits thymidylate synthase, shutting down fungal DNA and protein production.

💡 Systemic Uses Mnemonic: "S.Y.S.T.E.M."

SSystemic bloodstream Candidiasis
YYeast Cryptococcal meningitis (Amphotericin B + Flucytosine) IMP FOR EXAM
SSubcutaneous Sporotrichosis
TTough invasive Aspergillosis (Voriconazole / Caspofungin)
EEndemic Histoplasmosis & Blastomycosis
MMucormycosis fungal emergencies

💡 Systemic Side Effects Mnemonic: "S.H.A.K.E." IMP FOR EXAM

SShaking chills, fever, & rigors during IV infusion ("Shake & Bake" reaction)
HHepatotoxicity & liver enzyme spikes across azoles
AAnemia & bone marrow suppression (Flucytosine toxicity)
KKidney damage / Nephrotoxicity (Amphotericin B) IMP FOR EXAM
EElectrolyte wasting (severe hypokalemia & hypomagnesemia)

🚫 Clinical Safety Rules (Amphotericin B) IMP FOR EXAM

Pre-medicate patients with Acetaminophen, Diphenhydramine, and administer 1 Liter of IV Normal Saline bolus before infusion to protect the kidneys and reduce "shake and bake" reactions!

2. Drugs for Cutaneous & Superficial Mycoses

Target superficial fungal infections localized to keratinized tissues (skin, hair, nails) such as Ringworm, Athlete's Foot, Jock Itch, and Mucosal Yeast infections.

📂 Drug Classifications & Specific Agents

1. Squalene Epoxidase Inhibitor
Terbinafine (Lamisil)
First-line oral/topical choice for nail fungus (Onychomycosis).
2. Mitotic Spindle Disrupter
Griseofulvin
Oral drug that deposits in keratin for scalp ringworm (Tinea capitis).
3. Topical Polyene Antibiotic
Nystatin
Used strictly topically/orally for Candida thrush ("Swish and Swallow").
4. Topical Azoles
Clotrimazole Miconazole Ketoconazole
Creams for athlete's foot and vaginal yeast.

Simplified Mechanisms of Action (MOA)

⚙️ Squalene Epoxidase Inhibitor (Terbinafine): "Trapping Toxic Squalene" IMP FOR EXAM

💡 Concept: Blocks squalene epoxidase early in the ergosterol pathway. Toxic squalene builds up inside the fungus, causing rapid cell death.
1
Early Pathway Block: Inhibits squalene epoxidase enzyme early in sterol building.
2
Toxic Squalene Poisoning: Squalene accumulates toxically inside fungal cells, causing membrane rupture (fungicidal).

⚙️ Mitotic Disrupter (Griseofulvin): "Freezing Fungal Cell Division"

💡 Concept: Binds fungal microtubules and stops mitosis (cell division). It deposits into human keratin so new growing hair/nails are completely resistant to fungus!
1
Microtubule Binding: Binds fungal polymerized microtubules, arresting mitosis in metaphase.
2
Keratin Binding: Binds tightly to human keratin in hair and skin, shielding new tissue growth from infection.

⚙️ Topical Polyene (Nystatin): "Topical Membrane Hole-Puncher" IMP FOR EXAM

💡 Concept: Same mechanism as Amphotericin B (binds ergosterol to poke membrane holes), but too toxic for IV use—strictly used topically or swallowed for local gut thrush.
1
Local Surface Action: Binds surface ergosterol on Candida yeasts without systemic oral absorption.

💡 Cutaneous Uses Mnemonic: "S.K.I.N.S."

SSuperficial ringworm (Tinea corporis / Tinea cruris)
KKeratin-rich scalp infection (Tinea capitis treated with Griseofulvin)
IInterdigital Athlete's Foot (Tinea pedis)
NNail fungal infections / Onychomycosis (Terbinafine) IMP FOR EXAM
SSwish-and-swallow oral Candida thrush (Nystatin suspension) IMP FOR EXAM

💡 Cutaneous Side Effects Mnemonic: "R.A.S.H.E.S."

RRedness, burning, and local contact irritation
AAbdominal distress & metallic taste disturbance (Terbinafine)
SSunlight photosensitivity & Disulfiram-like alcohol reaction (Griseofulvin)
HHeadaches
EElevated liver enzymes (check baseline LFTs with oral Terbinafine) IMP FOR EXAM
SSkin rash & hives

🚫 Nursing Clinical Practice Rules

• Terbinafine: Monitor baseline LFTs (liver function tests) before starting oral therapy for nail fungus.
• Griseofulvin: Instruct patients to take with fatty foods (like ice cream or milk) to increase absorption! IMP FOR EXAM
• Nystatin Thrush: Instruct patient to swish suspension thoroughly in mouth for several minutes and SWALLOW (do not spit!).
