Antifungal Drugs: Systemic, Subcutaneous & Cutaneous Mycoses

Clear drug classifications, mechanisms of action, clinical indications, and exam mnemonics for fungal infections.

Drugs for Subcutaneous and Systemic Mycoses

Potent antifungal agents used to treat deep-seated, life-threatening systemic fungal infections and localized subcutaneous mycoses.

📂 Clear Drug Names & Uses

Amphotericin BGold standard intravenous polyene for severe, life-threatening systemic mycoses.
FluconazoleOral/IV triazole frontline for candidiasis and cryptococcal meningitis.
ItraconazoleBroad-spectrum azole for systemic and subcutaneous infections (e.g., sporotrichosis).
VoriconazoleFrontline triazole specifically targeting invasive aspergillosis.
Caspofungin (Echinocandins)Cell wall synthesis blocker used for invasive candidiasis and aspergillosis rescue.
FlucytosinePyrimidine antimetabolite used synergistically with Amphotericin B.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Fungal Membrane & Wall Attack

🍄
Step 1: Target Binding Drugs bind fungal cell membrane ergosterol or block beta-glucan cell wall synthesis.
🕳️
Step 2: Pore Formation Polyenes punch microscopic pores in the fungal membrane; azoles block ergosterol production.
💧
Step 3: Leakage & Burst Intracellular ions (potassium) leak out, leading to osmotic instability.
💥
Step 4: Fungal Death Resulting cellular collapse causes rapid fungal cell death (fungicidal/fungistatic).

💡 Uses with Mnemonic: "S.Y.S.T.E.M."

Systemic candidiasis • Yeast meningitis (Cryptococcus) • Subcutaneous sporotrichosis • Tough aspergillosis • Endemic histoplasmosis • Mucormycosis infections.

💡 Adverse Effects with Mnemonic: "S.H.A.K.E."

Shaking chills & fever infusion reactions (Amphotericin B "shake and bake") • Hepatic enzyme bumps • Anemia / bone marrow suppression • Kidney / nephrotoxicity • Electrolyte loss (low potassium and magnesium).

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Amphotericin B requires premedication (antipyretics, antihistamines, hydration) to manage severe infusion reactions and nephrotoxicity risks.

Drugs for Cutaneous Mycoses

Topical and oral medications designed to treat superficial fungal infections affecting skin, hair, and nails (dermatophytes and superficial yeasts).

📂 Clear Drug Names & Uses

TerbinafineSqualene epoxidase inhibitor; frontline oral/topical treatment for athlete's foot & nail fungus.
GriseofulvinOral agent binding keratin in skin, hair, and nail dermatophytosis (ringworm).
ClotrimazoleTopical azole for ringworm, jock itch, and cutaneous candidiasis.
MiconazoleTopical cream/powder for common superficial skin and vaginal yeast infections.
NystatinPolyene used topically or as a swish-and-swallow solution for oral thrush (candidiasis).
KetoconazoleTopical cream and medicated shampoo for seborrheic dermatitis and ringworm.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Superficial Ergosterol & Keratin Disruption

🧴
Step 1: Application / Ingestion Drug is applied topically to skin lesions or ingested orally to bind keratin structures.
⚙️
Step 2: Enzyme Blockade Terbinafine blocks squalene epoxidase, causing toxic squalene accumulation in fungi.
🛡️
Step 3: Keratin Protection Griseofulvin integrates into newly growing keratinized skin and nails, preventing invasion.
✨
Step 4: Skin Clearance Eradicates superficial fungal colonies, clearing rashes and restoring skin health.

💡 Uses with Mnemonic: "S.K.I.N.S."

Superficial ringworm (tinea corporis) • Kaposi-alternative fungal rashes • Infections of athlete's foot (tinea pedis) • Nail fungus (onychomycosis) • Swish-and-swallow oral thrush (Nystatin).

💡 Adverse Effects with Mnemonic: "R.A.S.H.E.S."

Redness and local skin irritation • Abdominal stomach upset • Sunlight photosensitivity • Headache • Elevated liver enzymes (oral terbinafine/griseofulvin) • Skin contact dermatitis.

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Oral terbinafine and griseofulvin require baseline liver function checks. Avoid alcohol consumption during treatment due to potential hepatic strain.
