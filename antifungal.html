Antifungal Drugs & Detailed Mechanisms of Action (MOA)

Complete pharmacological breakdown featuring drug classifications, exact molecular mechanisms, clinical indications, and mnemonics.

Drugs for Subcutaneous and Systemic Mycoses

Potent antifungal medications targeting deep-seated, life-threatening systemic fungal infections and localized subcutaneous mycoses.

📂 Important Drug Names & Detailed Molecular MOA

Amphotericin B MOA: Polyene Membrane Pore Formers Binds irreversibly to ergosterol in the fungal cell membrane, creating pores that cause leakage of vital ions ($K^+$ and $Mg^{2+}$), leading to cell death.
Fluconazole / Itraconazole / Voriconazole MOA: Azole Demethylase Inhibitors Inhibits fungal cytochrome P450 enzyme (14-$\alpha$-demethylase), blocking lanosterol conversion to ergosterol, disrupting membrane fluidity.
Caspofungin (Echinocandins) MOA: Cell Wall Glucan Synthesis Blockers Non-competitively inhibits $\beta$-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase enzyme, preventing formation of essential fungal cell wall structural polymers.
Flucytosine MOA: Pyrimidine Antimetabolite Enters fungal cells via cytosine permease, converts into 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), and halts DNA/RNA protein synthesis.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Systemic Fungal Disruption

🍄
Step 1: Target Binding Drugs locate fungal cellular components (ergosterol, glucan synthase, or DNA precursors).
🕳️
Step 2: Structural Breakdown Membrane integrity is compromised or cell wall synthesis is abruptly halted.
💧
Step 3: Internal Leakage Essential intracellular contents and potassium leak outward into extracellular space.
💥
Step 4: Rapid Destruction Fungal cells undergo osmotic lysis and rapid clearance.

💡 Uses with Mnemonic: "S.Y.S.T.E.M."

Systemic candidiasis • Yeast meningitis (Cryptococcus) • Subcutaneous sporotrichosis • Tough aspergillosis • Endemic histoplasmosis • Mucormycosis infections.

💡 Adverse Effects with Mnemonic: "S.H.A.K.E."

Shaking chills & fever infusion reactions (Amphotericin B "shake and bake") • Hepatic enzyme bumps • Anemia / bone marrow suppression • Kidney / nephrotoxicity • Electrolyte loss (low potassium and magnesium).

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Amphotericin B requires premedication (antipyretics, antihistamines, IV normal saline hydration) to manage severe infusion reactions and nephrotoxicity risks.

Drugs for Cutaneous Mycoses

Topical and oral medications designed to treat superficial fungal infections affecting skin, hair, and nails (dermatophytes and superficial yeasts).

📂 Important Drug Names & Detailed Molecular MOA

Terbinafine MOA: Squalene Epoxidase Inhibitor Blocks squalene epoxidase enzyme early in ergosterol biosynthesis, causing toxic intracellular accumulation of squalene and fungal death.
Griseofulvin MOA: Mitotic Spindle Disrupter Binds to fungal micro-tubules, disrupting mitotic spindle formation and arresting fungal cell division (metaphase arrest).
Nystatin MOA: Polyene Membrane Binder Binds to ergosterol in fungal cell membranes, forming pores and causing ion leakage (used locally for oral/topical candidiasis).
Clotrimazole / Miconazole MOA: Topical Azole Inhibitor Inhibits 14-$\alpha$-demethylase locally in superficial layers, blocking ergosterol synthesis in ringworm and yeast rashes.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Superficial Ergosterol & Keratin Protection

🧴
Step 1: Drug Delivery Topical application or systemic absorption delivers drug directly to keratin tissues (skin, hair, nails).
⚙️
Step 2: Enzyme or Mitotic Block Inhibits squalene epoxidase or binds tubulin to stop fungal division and growth.
🛡️
Step 3: Keratin Shielding Incoporates into keratin matrix, preventing newly growing tissue from being infected by dermatophytes.
✨
Step 4: Superficial Clearance Eradicates superficial colonies, relieving itching, scaling, and inflammation.

💡 Uses with Mnemonic: "S.K.I.N.S."

Superficial ringworm (tinea corporis) • Kaposi-alternative fungal rashes • Infections of athlete's foot (tinea pedis) • Nail fungus (onychomycosis) • Swish-and-swallow oral thrush (Nystatin).

💡 Adverse Effects with Mnemonic: "R.A.S.H.E.S."

Redness and local skin irritation • Abdominal stomach upset • Sunlight photosensitivity • Headache • Elevated liver enzymes (oral terbinafine/griseofulvin) • Skin contact dermatitis.

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Oral terbinafine and griseofulvin require baseline liver function checks. Avoid alcohol consumption during treatment due to potential hepatic strain.
