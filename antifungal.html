Antiparasitic Drugs: Mechanisms & Classification

A clear breakdown of how we treat Protozoa, Helminths, and Ectoparasites.

1. Antiprotozoal Agents

Used to treat single-celled eukaryotic organisms like Amoeba, Giardia, and Malaria parasites.

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Metronidazole MOA: DNA Strand Breaker Reduced inside the parasite to free radicals; these radicals damage parasitic DNA, preventing replication.
Chloroquine MOA: Heme Detox Inhibition Accumulates in the parasite's food vacuole, preventing the detoxification of toxic heme into hemozoin.

2. Antihelminthic Agents

Used to treat worm infections (Roundworms, Tapeworms, Flukes).

📂 Drug Names & Molecular MOA

Albendazole / Mebendazole MOA: Microtubule Inhibitor Binds to parasite beta-tubulin, preventing microtubule polymerization needed for cell structure and glucose intake.
Ivermectin MOA: Chloride Channel Agonist Binds to glutamate-gated chloride channels in nerve/muscle cells, causing hyperpolarization and paralysis.
Praziquantel MOA: Calcium Flux Inducer Increases permeability of the cell membrane to calcium, resulting in tetanic contraction and worm paralysis.

⚡ Animated MOA Workflow: Parasitic Destruction

💊
Step 1: Ingestion Drug is absorbed and transported to the site of parasitic infection.
🎯
Step 2: Pathway Block Drug binds to tubulin, ion channels, or DNA-repair enzymes.
⚡
Step 3: Paralysis Metabolism fails or muscles paralyze, stopping movement.
✨
Step 4: Clearance The paralyzed parasite is expelled by host peristalsis or immune cells.

3. Ectoparasiticides & Mnemonics

Treating external infestations like lice and scabies.

📂 Common Topical Treatments

Permethrin MOA: Sodium Channel Disruption Neurotoxic to parasites; inhibits sodium channel repolarization, causing paralysis of lice/scabies mites.

💡 Drug Classifications: "A.P.E."

Antiprotozoals (Amoeba/Malaria) • Parallel Antihelminthics (Worms) • Ectoparasiticides (Lice/Scabies).

💡 Side Effects: "G.I. + N.E.R.V."

Gastro-intestinal upset (Nausea/Vomiting) • Inflammation • Neurological (Dizziness) • Elevated enzymes (Liver) • Rashes (allergic) • Vertigo.

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Always monitor for hypersensitivity reactions (fever/rash) caused by the release of antigens from dying parasites (Mazzotti reaction). Avoid in pregnancy unless absolutely necessary.
