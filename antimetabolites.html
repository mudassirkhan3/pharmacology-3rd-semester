Antimetabolites

Cytotoxic agents that structurally mimic essential natural metabolites required for nucleic acid synthesis, effectively halting cancer cell replication during the S-phase.

3 Mechanism of Action (MOA)

Interactive Pathways

Antimetabolites are cell-cycle phase specific (S-phase) agents that trick cancer cells by substituting themselves into normal metabolic pathways or locking critical enzymes. Select a class below to view its specific mechanism:

Pathway: Methotrexate (Enzyme Lock)
Step 1: Folate Need Cells require active tetrahydrofolate to synthesize purines and thymidylate for DNA.
Step 2: DHFR Inhibition Methotrexate tightly binds and inhibits the enzyme Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR).
Step 3: Cell Starvation Active folate runs out, freezing DNA replication and inducing cell death.
Pathway: 5-Fluorouracil & Capecitabine (Imposter Nucleotide)
Step 1: False Conversion 5-FU converts intracellularly into a false nucleotide (FdUMP).
Step 2: Synthase Block FdUMP binds permanently to the enzyme Thymidylate Synthase.
Step 3: Thymineless Death Without thymine building blocks, DNA synthesis fails completely.
Pathway: 6-Mercaptopurine (False Code Insertion)
Step 1: Base Mimicry 6-MP mimics natural purine bases (adenine and guanine).
Step 2: Strand Insertion Sneaks past checkpoints and incorporates directly into new DNA/RNA strands.
Step 3: Code Scrambling Scrambles the genetic code and causes replication termination.

4 Clinical Uses

Indications

Antimetabolites are widely utilized across hematological malignancies and solid tumors:

01 Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) & Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)
02 Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas
03 Solid Tumors: Colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, and breast cancers (5-FU / Capecitabine)
04 Non-Malignant Uses: Methotrexate is also used for severe rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis.

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

Because they target fast-proliferating cells, antimetabolites produce predictable bone marrow and mucosal toxicities:

1. Bone Marrow Suppression — Important / Serious

Severe myelosuppression, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and megaloblastic anemia.

2. Gastrointestinal Toxicity — Common

Stomatitis, severe diarrhea, and painful mucosal ulcerations.

3. Dermatological Effects — Common

Hand-foot syndrome (palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia) with Capecitabine/5-FU; photosensitivity.

4. Hepatotoxicity & Neurotoxicity — Important / Serious

Elevated liver enzymes and fibrosis with chronic Methotrexate; cerebellar ataxia with high-dose Cytarabine.

8 Important Complications & Rescues

Serious Alerts
🛡️ Methotrexate & Leucovorin Rescue

High-dose methotrexate causes acute renal failure and bone marrow collapse. Leucovorin (Folinic Acid) rescue bypasses DHFR blockade to provide active folate and rescue normal cells.

🖐️ Hand-Foot Syndrome

Common with continuous infusion 5-FU or oral Capecitabine. Presents as painful redness, swelling, numbness, and blistering on palms and soles.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Methotrexate

Folic Acid Analog
Key Info: Standard prototype for folate antagonism; requires precise rescue protocols.
MOA: Competitively inhibits Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR).
Uses: ALL, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, choriocarcinoma, and rheumatoid arthritis.
ADRs & Complications: Severe myelosuppression, mucositis, hepatotoxicity, and renal toxicity (mitigated by Leucovorin & hydration).

5-Fluorouracil (5-FU)

Pyrimidine Analog
Key Info: Cornerstone treatment for gastrointestinal and solid tumors.
MOA: Metabolized to FdUMP, which locks onto Thymidylate Synthase to stop DNA production.
Uses: Colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, and breast carcinomas.
ADRs & Complications: Diarrhea, stomatitis, myelosuppression, and Hand-Foot Syndrome.
