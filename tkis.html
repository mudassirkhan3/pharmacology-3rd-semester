Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs)

Targeted small-molecule oral agents designed to inhibit specific intracellular tyrosine kinase enzymes, blocking cell signaling cascades responsible for tumor growth and division.

3 Mechanism of Action (MOA)

Interactive Pathways

TKIs are small molecules that cross cell membranes to inhibit enzymatic activity inside malignant cells. Select a pathway below to view its specific mechanism:

Pathway: ATP Site Competition (Molecular Blockade)
Step 1: Membrane Crossing Small molecules slip easily inside the target cancer cell membrane.
Step 2: Pocket Binding Binds directly to the intracellular ATP-binding pocket of target kinase domains.
Step 3: Signal Stop Prevents ATP binding, halting phosphorylation and downstream growth signals.
Pathway: BCR-ABL Inhibition (Leukemia Switch-Off)
Step 1: CML Mutation In CML, the BCR-ABL fusion protein drives uncontrolled white blood cell growth.
Step 2: Enzyme Lock Imatinib locks this abnormal fusion enzyme in an inactive state.
Step 3: Apoptosis Trigger Induces programmed cell death in the malignant leukemia clones.
Pathway: Angiogenesis Blockade (Blood Supply Cutoff)
Step 1: Multi-Kinase Target Inhibits multiple receptors including VEGFR and PDGFR simultaneously.
Step 2: Signaling Arrest Shuts down endothelial cell proliferation signaling networks.
Step 3: Tumor Starvation Prevents new blood vessel growth, starving the solid tumor core.

4 Clinical Uses

Indications

TKIs have revolutionized targeted therapy for specific genetic mutations across various malignancies:

01 Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) and Ph+ ALL (Imatinib, Dasatinib)
02 Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) with EGFR mutations (Erlotinib, Osimertinib)
03 Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (GIST) (Imatinib, Sunitinib)
04 Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma & Hepatocellular Carcinoma (Sorafenib, Sunitinib)

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

While generally better tolerated than cytotoxic chemotherapy, TKIs produce distinctive targeted toxicities:

1. Dermatological Toxicity — Common

Acneiform papulopustular rash, dry skin, and nail changes (common with EGFR inhibitors).

2. Gastrointestinal Effects — Common

Severe watery diarrhea, nausea, and stomatitis.

3. Cardiovascular Effects — Important / Serious

Hypertension, peripheral edema, and QTc prolongation / left ventricular dysfunction.

4. Metabolic & Hepatic Toxicity — Important / Serious

Elevated liver enzymes (LFT abnormalities), hypothyroidism, and fatigue.

8 Important Complications & Drug Interactions

Serious Alerts
⚠️ CYP3A4 Drug Interactions

TKIs rely heavily on CYP3A4 metabolism. Co-administration with strong inhibitors (ketoconazole, grapefruit juice) increases toxicity risk, while inducers (rifampin, St. John's wort) decrease efficacy.

🛡️ Companion Genetic Testing

Most TKIs require mandatory predictive biomarker testing (EGFR mutations, ALK rearrangements, or BCR-ABL transcripts) before therapy initiation to ensure clinical effectiveness.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Imatinib

BCR-ABL Inhibitor Subclass
Key Info: First-generation oral TKI that transformed chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) management.
MOA: Competitively inhibits the BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase enzyme, arresting Philadelphia chromosome-positive clones.
Uses: CML, Ph+ ALL, and Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (GIST).
ADRs & Complications: Periorbital edema, fluid retention, muscle cramps, nausea, and myelosuppression.

Erlotinib

EGFR Inhibitor Subclass
Key Info: Oral small-molecule inhibitor targeting epidermal growth factor receptor mutations.
MOA: Reversibly blocks the ATP-binding site of EGFR tyrosine kinase.
Uses: Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) and pancreatic cancer.
ADRs & Complications: Characteristic acneiform papulopustular rash (indicates response), diarrhea, and interstitial lung disease risk.
