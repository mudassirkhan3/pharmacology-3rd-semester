Pharmacokinetics: ADME Principles, Kinetics & Dosage Design

Pharmacokinetics is the study of what the human body does to a drug from the moment it enters until it is completely cleared. Think of a medicine pill as a traveler embarking on an intricate biological journey through your organ systems. This journey follows four core milestones known as ADME.

💡 Core Mnemonic: "ADME"

Absorption $\rightarrow$ How will it get in? (Gut/Blood)
Distribution $\rightarrow$ Where will it go? (Tissues/Fluid)
Metabolism $\rightarrow$ How is it broken down? (Liver)
Excretion $\rightarrow$ How does it leave? (Kidneys/Urine)

1. Absorption, GI Mechanisms & Bioavailability

Absorption is the movement of a drug from its site of administration into the systemic bloodstream. Intravenous (IV) administration bypasses absorption entirely, providing 100% immediate bioavailability.

🧬 GI Absorption Routes ⭐ High-Yield

  • Passive Diffusion: Most common. Lipophilic (fat-soluble) drugs dissolve directly across cell membranes down a concentration gradient.
  • Paracellular Transport: Small, water-soluble molecules slip through tight junctions between enterocytes.
  • Active Transport: Transporter proteins actively pump nutrient-like drug molecules across membranes using energy.

📈 Bioavailability (F) ⭐ Core Formula

The fraction (%) of unchanged drug reaching the systemic circulation.

Bioavailability (F) =
AUC oral AUC IV
× 100

Key Drivers: First-pass hepatic metabolism, gastric pH destruction, and drug solubility.

🔍 Clinical Example: First-Pass Hepatic Effect
Oral drugs travel via the portal vein straight to the liver before reaching systemic circulation. For example, Nitroglycerin undergoes over 90% first-pass breakdown if swallowed. Therefore, it is given sublingually (under the tongue) so it absorbs directly into systemic veins, bypassing the liver entirely!

2. Distribution & Volume of Distribution

Distribution is the reversible movement of drug molecules between the bloodstream and body tissues (muscle, fat, brain).

🌐 Factors Influencing Distribution

  • Blood Perfusion: Heart, brain, and liver receive drugs instantly; fat and bone receive them slowly.
  • Plasma Protein Binding: Bound drug to Albumin is inactive; only free (unbound) drug produces therapeutic action.
  • Biological Barriers: Tight endothelial junctions form the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB), letting only lipophilic drugs cross.

📦 Volume of Distribution (Vd) ⭐ Key Formula

Theoretical fluid volume required to hold total drug at plasma concentration.

Vd =
Total Drug Amount in Body Plasma Concentration (C0)
  • Low Vd (~3-5 L): Drug stays in plasma (e.g., Heparin).
  • High Vd (>40 L): Drug concentrates heavily in tissues/fat (e.g., Digoxin, Morphine).

3. Metabolism & Kinetics (First-Order vs Zero-Order)

Metabolism (mainly in the liver via CYP450 enzymes) turns lipophilic drugs into hydrophilic metabolites for renal excretion.

💡 Mnemonic for Zero-Order Drugs: "PEA"

Zero-order drugs saturate enzymes easily and eliminate at a constant amount (not percentage):
P $\rightarrow$ Phenytoin
E $\rightarrow$ Ethanol (Alcohol)
A $\rightarrow$ Aspirin (at high doses)

📈 First-Order Kinetics

Eliminates a constant percentage of drug per hour (e.g., 50%/hr). Half-life ($t_{1/2}$) stays constant. Most drugs follow this model.

Rate = Constant × Concentration

📊 Zero-Order Kinetics

Eliminates a constant amount per hour (e.g., 10 mg/hr) due to enzyme saturation. Dose increases can cause sudden toxicity!

Rate = Constant Amount

5. Clearance, Half-Life & Dosage Design

⏱️ Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$) & Steady State

t1/2 =
0.693 × Vd Clearance (CL)

Rule of 4-5: It takes 4 to 5 half-lives for a drug to reach Steady State ($C_{ss}$) or to be completely cleared from the body.

🚀 Loading vs Maintenance Dose

Loading Dose: Large initial dose to reach target therapeutic level immediately.

Loading Dose = (Target Conc × Vd) / F

Maintenance Dose: Repeated doses to replace cleared drug and maintain steady state.

Maint Dose = (Target Conc × CL × Interval) / F
