General Nursing Measures & Patient Education

  • 1

    Administration Timing & Meal Interactions

    Instruct patients to take Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) 30–60 minutes before breakfast for optimal proton pump inhibition. Sucralfate should be taken on an empty stomach 1 hour before meals, while antacids should be given 1–2 hours apart from other oral drugs to prevent chelation.

  • 2

    Fluid Balance & Electrolyte Monitoring

    Continuously evaluate fluid intake/output, skin turgor, and serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) in patients experiencing acute diarrhea or severe vomiting before and during administration of antidiarrheals or antiemetics.

  • 3

    Laxative Safety & Hydration Guidance

    Warn patients taking bulk-forming laxatives (e.g., Psyllium) to ingest at least 240 mL (8 oz) of fluid per dose to prevent esophageal obstruction or intestinal impaction. Avoid long-term continuous use of stimulant laxatives to prevent cathartic colon dependency.

  • 4

    Antiemetic Timing & Prophylactic Use

    Administer prophylactic antiemetics 30 to 60 minutes prior to chemotherapy or surgical procedures. Monitor patients taking dopamine-antagonist antiemetics (e.g., Metoclopramide) for acute extrapyramidal symptoms or tardive dyskinesia.

  • 5

    Pregnancy Safety Warnings

    Verify pregnancy status before administering Misoprostol due to its potent abortifacient actions and uterine-stimulating effects. Ensure women of childbearing potential use effective contraception during PGE1 therapy.

  • 6

    Contraindications in Infectious Diarrhea

    Do not administer antimotility antidiarrheal agents (e.g., Loperamide, Diphenoxylate) to patients with high fever, bloody stool (dysentery), or confirmed C. difficile colitis to prevent pathogen retention and toxic megacolon.
