Thrombolytic Agents (Fibrinolytics)

Powerful emergency pharmacological agents designed to dissolve existing thrombi by activating the fibrinolytic system, restoring vital blood flow in acute thrombotic emergencies.

3 Mechanisms of Action (MOA)

Interactive Pathways

Thrombolytic agents activate the fibrinolytic system to break down existing clots. Select a mechanism below to view its specific pathway:

Pathway: Plasminogen Conversion
Step 1: Catalytic Action Thrombolytic agents catalyze inactive plasminogen conversion.
Step 2: Active Plasmin Transforms plasminogen into active proteolytic enzyme plasmin.
Step 3: Fibrin Cleavage Plasmin directly cleaves and digests the fibrin meshwork holding the thrombus.
Pathway: Fibrin Specificity
Step 1: Targeted Binding Agents like alteplase preferentially bind to clot-bound fibrin.
Step 2: Local Activation Activates plasminogen locally on the thrombus surface.
Step 3: Systemic Safety Reduces circulating systemic lytic state and overall bleeding risks.

4 Clinical Uses & Indications

Indications

Thrombolytics are reserved for acute, life-threatening thrombotic emergencies within strict therapeutic windows:

01 Acute ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI) when primary PCI is unavailable
02 Acute Ischemic Stroke (within 3 to 4.5 hours of symptom onset, after ruling out hemorrhage)
03 Massive Pulmonary Embolism (PE) associated with hemodynamic instability and shock
04 Severe deep vein thrombosis or clearing occluded central venous catheter lines (low-dose alteplase)

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

Thrombolytic therapy dissolves protective hemostatic plugs, causing major bleeding and specific adverse reactions:

1. Severe Hemorrhage & Bleeding — Important / Serious

The primary and most dangerous risk. Includes major internal bleeding, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, and life-threatening intracranial hemorrhage (stroke conversion).

2. Allergic & Reperfusion Reactions — Common

Streptokinase: Can cause severe allergic reactions, fever, and anaphylaxis due to bacterial protein origin. Reperfusion arrhythmias may also occur upon clot dissolution.

8 Pharmacokinetics & Contraindications

PK Profiles
⏱️ Short Half-Life & Administration

Administered via rapid intravenous bolus or short infusion due to short half-lives (minutes). Often followed by heparin administration to prevent re-occlusion.

🚫 Absolute Contraindications

Prior intracranial hemorrhage, cerebrovascular lesions, active internal bleeding, recent major surgery or trauma within 3 weeks, and severe uncontrolled hypertension.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Alteplase (tPA)

Recombinant tPA
Key Info: Recombinant tissue plasminogen activator widely used for acute ischemic stroke and STEMI.
MOA: Selectively binds fibrin, converting clot-bound plasminogen to plasmin.
Uses: Acute ischemic stroke (<4.5h), massive PE, and acute STEMI.
ADRs & Complications: Intracranial hemorrhage, major bleeding, reperfusion arrhythmias.

Streptokinase

Bacterial Activator
Key Info: Bacterial protein derived from streptococci; rarely used due to antibody neutralization and allergy risks.
MOA: Forms a complex with plasminogen to catalyze systemic plasminogen activation.
Uses: Historical or resource-limited STEMI management.
ADRs & Complications: High antigenicity, severe allergic reactions, fever, hypotension, and major bleeding.

12 Key Nursing Takeaways & Patient Safety

Patient Safety
1. Strict Time Windows

Monitor symptom onset precisely; thrombolytics for stroke must be given within 3 to 4.5 hours, and for STEMI ideally within 30 minutes of hospital arrival (door-to-needle time).

2. Neuro & Vital Sign Checks

Perform frequent neurological assessments (Glasgow Coma Scale) and check blood pressure continuously to detect early signs of intracranial hemorrhage.

3. Minimize Invasive Procedures

Avoid unnecessary arterial punctures, IM injections, and Foley catheter insertions after starting thrombolytics to prevent uncontrollable bleeding.

4. Immediate Action for Bleeding

If severe bleeding or sudden neurological decline occurs, immediately stop the infusion and prepare for emergency administration of blood products, cryoprecipitate, or antifibrinolytics (aminocaproic acid).
