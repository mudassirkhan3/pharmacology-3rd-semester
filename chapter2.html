General Nursing Measures & Patient Education

  • 1

    Culture & Sensitivity Testing First

    Always collect appropriate diagnostic specimens (blood, urine, sputum, or wound swabs) for culture and sensitivity testing before initiating the first dose of antimicrobial therapy to avoid masking true pathogen growth.

  • 2

    Strict Treatment Adherence & Resistance Prevention

    Educate patients on the critical importance of taking antimicrobial medications at evenly spaced intervals and completing the entire prescribed course—even if symptoms resolve completely—to prevent drug resistance and clinical relapse.

  • 3

    Hypersensitivity & Anaphylaxis Surveillance

    Monitor patients closely for signs of allergic reactions (e.g., urticarial rash, bronchospasm, facial angioedema) during the first 30 minutes of administration—especially with Beta-Lactam antibiotics (Penicillins and Cephalosporins).

  • 4

    Monitoring Organ Function & Toxicity Windows

    Assess baseline renal (serum creatinine, BUN) and hepatic (ALT, AST) laboratory parameters. Perform Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM) peak and trough draws for narrow therapeutic index drugs like Vancomycin and Gentamicin to avoid acute nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity.

  • 5

    Superinfection & Adverse Effect Management

    Educate patients to immediately report signs of secondary superinfections, such as persistent watery diarrhea (suggestive of C. difficile colitis) or oral white patches/vaginal discharge (fungal Candida overgrowth).

  • 6

    Administration Rules & Drug Interactions

    Verify food and drug interactions before administration (e.g., avoiding antacids/dairy products with Tetracyclines/Fluoroquinolones due to chelation, and warning against alcohol intake with Metronidazole to prevent disulfiram-like reactions).
