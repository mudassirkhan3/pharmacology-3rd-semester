Chapter 2: Drugs Used to Prevent & Treat Infection

Anti-infective agents are medications designed to target and eliminate invading microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, viruses, and parasites) without harming host human cells.

1. Select Drug Category

Antibacterial

Antibiotics, mechanism of action, bactericidal vs. bacteriostatic agents.

Antifungal

Agents targeting fungal cell membranes (ergosterol) and systemic infections.

Antiviral

Drugs inhibiting viral entry, reverse transcription, and viral replication.

Antiparasitic & Antimalarial

Treatments for protozoal, helminthic, and Plasmodium infections.

2. General Nursing Measures & Patient Education

  • Culture & Sensitivity First: Always obtain necessary cultures (blood, sputum, urine) before initiating the first antibiotic dose.
  • Complete Full Regimen: Instruct patients to finish all prescribed doses even if symptoms improve to prevent bacterial resistance.
  • Allergy Checks: Screen for drug hypersensitivities (e.g., Penicillin cross-reactivity) and monitor patients during the first 30 minutes of administration.
  • Toxicity & Monitoring: Track peak and trough serum levels for narrow therapeutic index anti-infectives (e.g., Vancomycin, Gentamicin).
  • Hydration & Renal Care: Encourage adequate fluid intake to prevent crystalluria and nephrotoxicity with agents like Sulfonamides and Acyclovir.
