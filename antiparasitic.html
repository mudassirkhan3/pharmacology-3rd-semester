Antiparasitic Pharmacology

Simplified Mechanisms, Clinical Uses, and Memory Aids.

1. Antiprotozoal Agents

Used to treat single-celled protozoa like malaria and ameba.

📂 Mechanisms of Action

Metronidazole MOA: DNA Damage Reduced inside the cell, creating radicals that fragment parasitic DNA.
Chloroquine MOA: Heme Detox Block Prevents the parasite from detoxifying heme, causing self-poisoning.

💡 Clinical Uses: "P.A.M."

Protozoal diarrhea (Giardia) • Amebiasis (Amoeba) • Malaria prophylaxis/treatment.

💡 ADRs & Mnemonic: "M.E.T.A.L."

Metallic taste • Epigastric distress (nausea) • Toxic to CNS (dizziness) • Abdominal cramps • Leukopenia (low white cell count).

2. Antihelminthic Agents

Used to treat worm infestations (Roundworms, Tapeworms, Flukes).

📂 Mechanisms of Action

Albendazole MOA: Microtubule Block Stops microtubule synthesis; worm starves to death.
Ivermectin MOA: Chloride Channels Locks chloride channels open, causing parasite paralysis.

💡 Clinical Uses: "W.O.R.M.S."

Whipworms • Oxyuriasis (Pinworms) • Roundworms • Migrating larvae (Strongyloides) • Stapeworms (Taenia).

💡 ADRs & Mnemonic: "A.B.D.O.M."

Abdominal pain/cramps • Bone marrow suppression • Diarrhea • Out of sorts (malaise) • Mazzotti reaction (hypersensitivity to dying worms).

3. Ectoparasiticides

Used to treat external skin parasites like lice and scabies.

📂 Mechanism of Action

Permethrin MOA: Sodium Channel Tox Disrupts parasite sodium channels, inducing paralysis.

💡 Clinical Uses: "L.I.C.E."

Lice (Pediculosis) • Itch (Scabies) • Crabs (Pubic lice) • Ectoparasite skin infestations.

💡 ADRs & Mnemonic: "S.K.I.N."

Stinging/Burning • Keep away from eyes (avoid contact) • Irritation of local area • Numbness/Paresthesia.

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Always wash off topical agents at the specified time to prevent chemical burns. Perform contact tracing for lice/scabies to prevent re-infestation.
