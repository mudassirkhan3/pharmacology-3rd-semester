The Antiparasitic Master Guide

Simplified logic for complex pharmacology.

1. Antiprotozoals

Intracellular Sabotage

The Concept: These drugs are chemical hunters that track down single-celled organisms like malaria or amoebas. Once they find their target, they enter the parasite cell and trigger an internal molecular breakdown. By disrupting DNA replication or toxic waste removal, they starve the parasite, effectively killing the organism from the inside out.

Common Agents:
  • Metronidazole / Tinidazole
  • Chloroquine / Hydroxychloroquine
  • Pentamidine / Pyrimethamine
  • Atovaquone
Clinical Uses:

Amoebic dysentery, Giardia, Trichomoniasis, Malaria prophylaxis, and Toxoplasmosis.

Drug Absorption into Parasite Cell
↓
Activation into Toxic Free Radicals
↓
DNA/Protein Strand Fragmentation
↓
Parasite Death
ADR Mnemonic: M.E.T.A.L.
Metallic taste, Epigastric distress, Toxic to CNS, Abdominal cramps, Leukopenia.

2. Antihelminthics

The Immobilizers

The Concept: Designed to fight larger invaders like worms, these drugs attack the structural integrity of the parasite. They work by latching onto the worm's nervous system or cellular infrastructure, making it impossible for the parasite to move or eat. Paralyzed and unable to hold onto the intestinal wall, the worm is then simply swept away by the host's normal digestion.

Common Agents:
  • Albendazole / Mebendazole
  • Ivermectin
  • Praziquantel
  • Pyrantel Pamoate
  • Diethylcarbamazine
Clinical Uses:

Pinworms, Tapeworms, Roundworms, Hookworms, Scabies (oral), and Filariasis.

Targeting Tubulin or Ion Channels
↓
Metabolic/Nerve Inhibition
↓
Starvation or Flaccid Paralysis
↓
Expulsion via Bowel Movement
Use Mnemonic: W.O.R.M.S.
Whipworms, Oxyuriasis (Pinworms), Roundworms, Migrating larvae, Stapeworms.

3. Ectoparasiticides

Surface Neurotoxins

The Concept: These are topical armor-piercing agents designed for surface-level pests like lice and mites. When applied, they absorb into the parasite's exoskeleton and act as a powerful neurotoxin. They essentially "jam" the parasite's sodium channels, causing the nerves to fire uncontrollably until the pest suffers total paralysis and dies.

Common Agents:
  • Permethrin
  • Malathion
  • Spinosad
  • Benzyl Alcohol
  • Lindane (Limited use)
Clinical Uses:

Pediculosis (Head/Body lice), Pubic lice (Crabs), and Scabies infestations.

Topical Absorption
↓
Over-stimulation of Sodium Channels
↓
Continuous Nerve Firing
↓
Death by Paralysis
ADR Mnemonic: S.K.I.N.
Stinging, Keep out of eyes, Irritation, Numbness.

Nursing & Clinical Pearls

  • Metronidazole: Teach patient to avoid alcohol for 48 hours to prevent severe nausea (Disulfiram-like effect).
  • Antihelminthics: Treat the *entire family* simultaneously, or re-infection is guaranteed.
  • Ectoparasiticides: Follow timing exactly. Don't wash it off early, or the parasite may survive.
  • Monitoring: Always screen for pregnancy, as some of these drugs cross the placenta or enter breast milk.
