💊 Antiparasitic Pharmacology

1. Antiprotozoals (e.g., Metronidazole)

Target: Protozoa (Ameba, Malaria, Giardia)

Drug Ingestion
→
Internal Reduction
→
DNA Strand Breakage
→
Parasite Death

Explanation: The drug is reduced by parasitic enzymes into toxic radicals that shred the parasite's DNA, making replication impossible.

Uses: P.A.M. (Protozoal diarrhea, Amebiasis, Malaria)
ADRs: M.E.T.A.L. (Metallic taste, Epigastric distress, Toxic to CNS, Abdominal cramps, Leukopenia)
Nursing Takeaways:
  • ALCOHOL WARNING: Absolutely no alcohol! Causes violent vomiting (Disulfiram-like reaction).
  • Patient Edu: Urine may turn red/brown (harmless).

2. Antihelminthics (e.g., Albendazole, Ivermectin)

Target: Worms (Pinworms, Roundworms)

Drug Binding
→
Metabolic Block
→
Immobilization
→
Expulsion

Explanation: The drug binds to the worm's structure or receptors. This blocks glucose absorption (starvation) or paralyzes the muscles, so the worm loses its grip on the gut and is pooped out.

Uses: W.O.R.M.S. (Whipworms, Oxyuriasis/Pinworms, Roundworms, Migrating larvae, Stapeworms)
ADRs: A.B.D.O.M. (Abdominal pain, Bone marrow suppression, Diarrhea, Out-of-sorts/malaise, Mazzotti reaction)
Nursing Takeaways:
  • Household Hygiene: Pinworms spread easily. Treat the whole family simultaneously.
  • Fecal-Oral Cycle: Teach parents to trim fingernails short to prevent eggs being picked up from scratching and transferred to the mouth.

3. Ectoparasiticides (e.g., Permethrin)

Target: External parasites (Lice, Scabies)

Topical Application
→
Sodium Channel Block
→
Continuous Firing
→
Paralysis & Death

Explanation: The drug jams open the Sodium channels in the parasite's nerve cells, causing the nerves to fire repeatedly until the parasite is paralyzed and dies.

Uses: L.I.C.E. (Lice, Itch/Scabies, Crabs/Pubic lice, Ectoparasites)
ADRs: S.K.I.N. (Stinging, Keep out of eyes, Irritation, Numbness)
Nursing Takeaways:
  • Contact Precautions: Lice survive on fabrics. Seal non-washables in plastic bags for 14 days.
  • Application: Do not wash off prematurely! Follow the "soak time" exactly (usually 8-14 hours).
