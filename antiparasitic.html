1. Antiprotozoal Agents

1. Drug Ingestion
↓
2. Conversion to Toxic Radicals
↓
3. DNA Strand Fragmentation
↓
4. Parasite Replication Death

Mechanism Explained: These drugs function as Intracellular Saboteurs. They enter the protozoan cell as an inactive compound, where the parasite's own enzymes convert the drug into highly reactive, toxic free radicals. These radicals directly assault the parasite's DNA, causing permanent strand fragmentation. Once the genetic structure is shattered, the parasite is unable to replicate or synthesize essential proteins, leading to cell death.

Common Drugs:

1. Metronidazole
2. Tinidazole
3. Chloroquine
4. Atovaquone
Mnemonic (METAL): Metallic taste, Epigastric distress, Toxic to CNS, Abdominal cramps, Leukopenia.
Nursing Pearl: Strictly avoid alcohol (Disulfiram-like reaction).

2. Antihelminthic Agents

1. Binding to Tubulin/Nerves
↓
2. Glucose Block / Starvation
↓
3. Flaccid Paralysis
↓
4. Expulsion via Peristalsis

Mechanism Explained: These agents operate as Metabolic Disruptors. They bind selectively to the parasite's microtubules (tubulin) or specific neuromuscular receptors. This action cripples the worm's ability to absorb glucose—its primary energy source—while simultaneously inducing flaccid paralysis. With the worm immobilized and energy-depleted, it can no longer maintain its grip on the intestinal wall and is naturally expelled through the host's normal bowel movements.

Common Drugs:

1. Albendazole
2. Mebendazole
3. Ivermectin
4. Praziquantel
Mnemonic (WORMS): Whipworms, Oxyuriasis, Roundworms, Migrating larvae, Stapeworms.
Nursing Pearl: Treat the entire household simultaneously to prevent re-infection.

3. Ectoparasiticides

1. Topical Shell Absorption
↓
2. Sodium Channel Lock
↓
3. Uncontrollable Nerve Firing
↓
4. Death by Paralysis

Mechanism Explained: These drugs act as Topical Neurotoxins. When applied to the skin or hair, the medication is absorbed directly into the parasite's chitinous exoskeleton. It targets voltage-gated sodium channels, forcing them to remain locked in the "open" position. This results in continuous, uncontrollable nerve transmission throughout the parasite's body. The parasite dies from extreme neuromuscular exhaustion and total paralysis.

Common Drugs:

1. Permethrin
2. Malathion
3. Spinosad
4. Benzyl Alcohol
Mnemonic (SKIN): Stinging, Keep out of eyes, Irritation, Numbness.
Nursing Pearl: Always follow exact leave-on time; premature washing may allow survival.
