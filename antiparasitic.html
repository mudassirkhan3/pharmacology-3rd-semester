💊 Antiparasitic MOA Guide

1. Antiprotozoals

Drug enters the parasite and is converted into a toxic substance inside. This chemical creates permanent breaks within the parasite's DNA structure. Without functional DNA, the parasite cannot grow or make proteins. It quickly loses its ability to survive and dies.
Drug Ingestion ↓ Activation to Poison ↓ DNA Destruction ↓ Parasite Death
Clinical Uses:
  • Malaria (prophylaxis/treatment), Giardiasis, Amoebic dysentery, and Trichomoniasis.

2. Antihelminthics

Drug targets the worm's ability to absorb energy or control its muscles. It blocks nutrient intake or disrupts internal nerve cell signals. The worm becomes starved or suffers total paralysis of its body. Unable to grip the intestine, it is naturally flushed out by digestion.
Nerve/Energy Blockade ↓ Starvation or Paralysis ↓ Loss of Intestinal Grip ↓ Expulsion by Bowel Movement
Clinical Uses:
  • Pinworms, Roundworms, Tapeworms, Hookworms, and Strongyloidiasis.

3. Ectoparasiticides

Drug is absorbed into the pest’s hard outer shell (exoskeleton). It forces the parasite’s nerve channels to stay open at all times. This causes non-stop nerve firing and uncontrollable body twitching. The pest suffers paralysis, dies, and is washed away from the skin.
Absorption into Shell ↓ Nerve Channel Lock ↓ Uncontrollable Paralysis ↓ Parasite Death
Clinical Uses:
  • Head/Body Lice, Pubic Lice (Crabs), and Scabies mite infestations.
