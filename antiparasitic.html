💊 Antiparasitic Pharmacology

1. Antiprotozoals

(Metronidazole / Chloroquine)

⚙️ MOA Flow

Ingestion ➜ Reduction to Radicals ➜ DNA Damage ➜ Parasite Death
Mnemonic for ADRs: "M.E.T.A.L."
• Metallic taste | Epigastric distress | Toxic to CNS | Abdominal cramps | Leukopenia
Nursing Takeaways:
  • No Alcohol: Severe vomiting (Disulfiram-like reaction).
  • Patient Education: Urine will turn red/brown—this is normal and harmless.

2. Antihelminthics

(Albendazole / Ivermectin)

⚙️ MOA Flow

Binding to Worm Protein ➜ Disruption of Glucose/Nerves ➜ Starvation/Paralysis ➜ Expulsion
Mnemonic for Uses: "W.O.R.M.S."
• Whipworms | Oxyuriasis (Pinworms) | Roundworms | Migrating larvae | Stapeworms
Nursing Takeaways:
  • Family Hygiene: Everyone in the house must be treated to break the cycle.
  • Hygiene: Hot water wash for all linens. Short nails prevent re-infection.

3. Ectoparasiticides

(Permethrin)

⚙️ MOA Flow

Topical Absorption ➜ Sodium Channel Blockade ➜ Nerve Over-stimulation ➜ Paralysis & Death
Mnemonic for ADRs: "S.K.I.N."
• Stinging | Keep away from eyes | Irritation | Numbness
Nursing Takeaways:
  • Exact Timing: The drug must stay on for the required hours (check the specific order).
  • Fomites: Seal non-washable items in bags for 14 days to starve the parasites.
