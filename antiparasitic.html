Classification: Antimalarial Agents

Pharmacological strategies for the treatment and prophylaxis of Plasmodium species infections, focusing on heme toxicity and metabolic disruption.

1. Heme Ingestion
2. Heme Polymerization
Inhibited by Drug
3. Toxic Heme Accumulation

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Chloroquine / Hydroxychloroquine
MOA: Accumulates in the food vacuole of the parasite, preventing the detoxification of heme into non-toxic hemozoin. The resulting accumulation of toxic free heme leads to parasite death.
Uses: First-line treatment for malaria (sensitive strains), rheumatoid arthritis, and SLE.
ADRs: Retinopathy (maculopathy), pruritus, headache, and GI upset.
Artemisinin Derivatives (Artesunate)
MOA: The endoperoxide bridge is cleaved by heme iron within the parasite, producing free radicals that damage parasite proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids.
Uses: First-line therapy for severe/complicated P. falciparum malaria.
ADRs: Generally well-tolerated; rare dizziness, transient neutropenia, and delayed hemolysis.
Primaquine
MOA: Disrupts mitochondrial electron transport and targets the exoerythrocytic (liver) stages of malaria by producing reactive oxygen species.
Uses: Radical cure for P. vivax and P. ovale (targets hypnozoites in the liver).
ADRs: Severe hemolysis in patients with G6PD deficiency; GI distress.

💡 Mnemonic: "C.A.P." (Malaria)

Chloroquine • Artemisinin • Primaquine.

Classification: Antihelminthics

Agents targeting parasitic worms (nematodes, trematodes, cestodes) by inhibiting essential structural proteins or causing muscular paralysis.

⚡ Animated Flow: Benzimidazole Mechanism
1. Tubulin Binding
2. Microtubule Synthesis
Blocked by Drug
3. Parasite Starvation

📂 Drug Inventory: Detailed MOA, Uses & ADRs

Albendazole / Mebendazole
MOA: Inhibits β-tubulin polymerization, preventing microtubule formation within the parasite, which impairs glucose uptake and causes worm starvation.
Uses: Nematode infections (Ascaris, Hookworm, Pinworm, Strongyloides).
ADRs: Abdominal pain, liver enzyme elevation (long-term), headache.
Ivermectin
MOA: Agonist of glutamate-gated chloride channels in parasite nerve/muscle cells, increasing chloride influx leading to hyperpolarization and flaccid paralysis of the worm.
Uses: Strongyloidiasis, Onchocerciasis (River Blindness), Scabies.
ADRs: Mazzotti reaction (fever, rash, lymphadenopathy) due to killing of microfilariae.
Praziquantel
MOA: Increases parasite membrane permeability to calcium, inducing massive contraction and paralysis of the worm's musculature, exposing it to the host immune system.
Uses: Trematodes (Schistosomiasis) and Cestodes (Tapeworms).
ADRs: Dizziness, headache, abdominal pain, and nausea.

💡 Mnemonic: "A.I.P." (Antihelminthic)

Albendazole • Ivermectin • Praziquantel.
