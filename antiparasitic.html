1. Antiprotozoal Agents

Mechanism of Action Explanation

These agents function as Intracellular Toxins. Once ingested, the drug is activated by the parasite’s own enzymes into reactive free radicals. These radicals aggressively fragment the parasitic DNA, making it impossible for the organism to replicate or maintain its genetic integrity, leading to rapid cell death.

Common Drugs: Metronidazole, Tinidazole, Chloroquine, Hydroxychloroquine, Atovaquone.
Clinical Uses: Malaria (prophylaxis/treatment), Amoebic Dysentery, Giardiasis, Trichomoniasis, Toxoplasmosis.
💡 Mnemonic (ADR): METAL: Metallic taste, Epigastric distress, Toxic to CNS, Abdominal cramps, Leukopenia.

2. Antihelminthic Agents

Mechanism of Action Explanation

These agents act as Metabolic & Neuromuscular Blockers. They bind to the parasite's tubulin (structural protein) or specific nerve receptors. By inhibiting glucose uptake, the worm starves of energy. Simultaneously, the drug induces flaccid paralysis, causing the worm to lose its mechanical grip on the intestinal lining, allowing it to be expelled via bowel movements.

Common Drugs: Albendazole, Mebendazole, Ivermectin, Praziquantel, Pyrantel Pamoate.
Clinical Uses: Pinworms, Roundworms, Tapeworms, Hookworms, Strongyloidiasis.
💡 Mnemonic (Uses): WORMS: Whipworms, Oxyuriasis, Roundworms, Migrating larvae, Stapeworms.

3. Ectoparasiticides

Mechanism of Action Explanation

These agents are Topical Neurotoxins. Once applied, they penetrate the chitinous exoskeleton of the pest. The drug locks the parasite's voltage-gated sodium channels in the "open" position. This leads to continuous nerve firing and uncontrollable twitching. The parasite eventually exhausts its nervous system and dies due to acute paralysis.

Common Drugs: Permethrin, Malathion, Spinosad, Benzyl Alcohol.
Clinical Uses: Head/Body Lice, Pubic Lice (Crabs), Scabies Mites.
💡 Mnemonic (ADR): SKIN: Stinging, Keep out of eyes, Irritation, Numbness.
