Antimycobacterial Drugs: Tuberculosis & Leprosy

Clear classifications, drug names, animated workflows, and exam mnemonics for treating mycobacterial infections.

First-Line Anti-Tuberculosis Drugs (R.I.P.E.)

The standard 4-drug initial combination therapy used to treat active tuberculosis infections.

📂 Clear Drug Names & Uses

Isoniazid (INH)Core frontline bactericidal drug; stops mycolic acid cell wall building.
Rifampin (RIF)Stops bacterial RNA synthesis; turns body fluids a harmless red-orange.
Pyrazinamide (PZA)Works best in acidic environments inside macrophages to kill dormant TB bacilli.
Ethambutol (EMB)Blocks cell wall assembly; prevents bacterial resistance development.
⚡ Flow Animation: Mechanism of Action (Multi-Target Mycobacterial Attack)
🦠
Step 1: Ingestion Drugs penetrate waxy mycobacterial cell walls and enter infection sites.
🧱
Step 2: Wall & RNA Disruption INH & EMB destroy cell wall components; RIF stops RNA transcription.
💤
Step 3: Dormant Kill PZA targets semi-dormant bacteria hiding inside immune cells.
✨
Step 4: Eradication Prevents resistance emergence through powerful four-drug synergy.

💡 Drug Names Mnemonic: "R.I.P.E." IMP FOR EXAM

RRifampin
IIsoniazid
PPyrazinamide
EEthambutol

💡 Side Effects Mnemonic: "N.O.R.P.H."

NNeuropathy (INH - co-administer Vitamin B6 / Pyridoxine) IMP FOR EXAM
OOptic neuritis (Ethambutol - optic nerve damage & red-green color blindness) IMP FOR EXAM
RRed-orange body fluids discoloration (Rifampin) IMP FOR EXAM
PPainful joints / Hyperuricemia & Gout (Pyrazinamide)
HHepatotoxicity (liver toxicity across INH, RIF, and PZA)

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) regularly. Ethambutol requires baseline and periodic visual acuity/color discrimination testing.

Second-Line Anti-Tuberculosis Drugs

Used when patients develop multi-drug resistant TB (MDR-TB) or cannot tolerate first-line medications.

📂 Clear Drug Names & Uses

Fluoroquinolones(Levofloxacin / Moxifloxacin) Strong DNA-gyrase inhibitors used for drug-resistant strains.
Injectable Aminoglycosides(Amikacin / Streptomycin / Capreomycin) Protein synthesis blockers given via injection.
BedaquilineNovel ATP synthase inhibitor used specifically for severe resistant TB.
LinezolidOxazolidinone antibiotic effective against resistant intracellular mycobacteria.
⚡ Flow Animation: Mechanism of Action (Alternative Resistant Pathogen Attack)
🎯
Step 1: New Targets Bypasses standard pathways by targeting bacterial ATP production or protein translation.
🛑
Step 2: Replication Halt Stops resistant bacteria from multiplying inside lung cavities.
🛡️
Step 3: Resistance Shield Added to combination protocols to protect against total treatment failure.
💡
Step 4: Long-term Cure Extends treatment duration (up to 18–24 months) to secure complete clearance.

💡 Uses Mnemonic: "M.D.R.T.B."

MMulti-drug resistant strains
DDrug intolerance reactions
RRecurrent treatment failure
TToxic side effect management
BBacterial load reduction

💡 Adverse Effects Mnemonic: "O.T.T.E.R."

OOtotoxicity / hearing loss (injectables)
TTendon issues (fluoroquinolones)
TToxicity to kidneys / nephrotoxicity
EElectrolyte imbalances
RRetinal / optic nerve damage

🚫 Clinical Warnings

Second-line regimens require prolonged supervision, frequent audiometric testing, and close kidney/liver monitoring.

Anti-Leprosy Drugs (Multidrug Therapy - MDT)

Combination medications used to cure Hansen's disease caused by Mycobacterium leprae.

📂 Clear Drug Names & Uses

DapsoneSulfone antibiotic that blocks bacterial folic acid synthesis; core leprosy drug.
RifampinPowerful bactericidal agent included in the monthly MDT blister pack.
ClofazimineAnti-inflammatory and anti-mycobacterial dye that binds bacterial DNA.
⚡ Flow Animation: Mechanism of Action (Leprosy Multidrug Clearance)
💊
Step 1: MDT Blister Pack Combining Dapsone, Rifampin, and Clofazimine prevents single-drug resistance.
🔬
Step 2: Dual Inhibition Dapsone blocks folate; Rifampin blocks RNA; Clofazimine targets DNA & inflammation.
🌿
Step 3: Skin & Nerve Rescue Clears bacilli from skin lesions and peripheral nerves to stop deformity progression.
✨
Step 4: Clinical Cure Achieves permanent microbiological cure over 6 to 12+ months.

💡 Treatment Regimen Mnemonic: "D.R.C."

DDapsone (daily baseline oral therapy)
RRifampin (monthly supervised dose)
CClofazimine (monthly supervised + daily anti-inflammatory support)

💡 Adverse Effects Mnemonic: "S.K.I.N.S."

SSinister hemolysis in G6PD deficiency (Dapsone) IMP FOR EXAM
KKidney / liver strain
IIron deficiency anemia risk
NNatural reddish-brown skin discoloration (Clofazimine) IMP FOR EXAM
SSevere hypersensitivity skin rashes

🚫 Clinical Warnings IMP FOR EXAM

Screen patients for G6PD deficiency before starting Dapsone to avoid acute hemolytic anemia. Warn patients about temporary reddish-brown skin pigmentation changes caused by Clofazimine.
