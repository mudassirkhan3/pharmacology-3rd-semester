3 Mechanism of Action (MOA)

Interactive Pathways

Endocrine agents disrupt tumor growth pathways through targeted receptor interactions. Select a class below to view its specific mechanism:

Pathway: SERMs (Receptor Competitor)
Step 1: Binding Site Tamoxifen binds competitively to estrogen receptors on breast cancer cells.
Step 2: Blocking Growth Prevents endogenous estrogen from stimulating malignant cell proliferation.
Step 3: Mixed Activity Retains estrogenic agonist effects in bone and uterine tissue.
Pathway: Aromatase Inhibitors (Estrogen Starvers)
Step 1: Peripheral Source In postmenopausal women, adrenal androgens convert to estrogen via aromatase.
Step 2: Enzyme Blockade AIs block the aromatase enzyme completely, stopping peripheral synthesis.
Step 3: Tumor Starvation Deprives ER-positive breast tumors of their primary growth fuel.
Pathway: GnRH Analogs & Anti-Androgens (Hormone Shutdown)
Step 1: Pituitary Surge GnRH agonists initially surge then desensitize pituitary receptors.
Step 2: Gonadal Shutdown Halts LH/FSH release, dropping testosterone/estrogen to castrate levels.
Step 3: Receptor Blockade Anti-androgens block any remaining circulating androgens from binding.

4 Clinical Uses

Indications

Hormone therapies are central pillars in the long-term management of hormone-driven cancers:

01 Estrogen Receptor-Positive (ER+) Breast Cancer (Tamoxifen, AIs, Fulvestrant)
02 Advanced Prostate Cancer (GnRH analogs and Anti-androgens)
03 Hematological Malignancies: Lymphomas and Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (Corticosteroids)
04 Endometrial and Ovarian Cancers

6 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)

Side Effects

By manipulating normal hormone balance, these therapies produce predictable endocrine deficiency symptoms and organ toxicities:

1. Menopausal Symptoms & Sexual Dysfunction — Common

Hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness (Anti-estrogens/AIs), and erectile dysfunction/loss of libido (Anti-androgens).

2. Bone Mineral Density Loss — Important / Serious

Accelerated osteoporosis, bone demineralization, and fracture risk due to estrogen/androgen suppression.

3. Thromboembolic Events — Important / Serious

Increased risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (classic for Tamoxifen).

4. Metabolic & Uterine Effects — Common

Hyperglycemia, weight gain, Cushingoid features (Corticosteroids); endometrial hyperplasia and uterine cancer risk (Tamoxifen).

8 Important Complications & Phenomena

Serious Alerts
⚠️ The "Flare Phenomenon"

Starting GnRH agonists (Leuprolide) for prostate cancer causes a transient testosterone spike during weeks 1–2, temporarily worsening bone pain or urinary obstruction. Managed by co-administering an anti-androgen beforehand.

🛡️ Bone Protection Strategies

Aromatase inhibitors and androgen deprivation therapy reduce bone density. Patients require baseline DEXA scans, calcium, vitamin D, and bisphosphonates or denosumab prophylaxis.

9 Specific Drug Profiles

Prototypes

Tamoxifen

SERM Subclass
Key Info: Selective estrogen receptor modulator used for ER-positive breast cancer in pre- and post-menopausal women.
MOA: Competitive estrogen receptor antagonist in breast tissue; partial agonist in bone and endometrium.
Uses: ER+ breast cancer treatment and prevention in high-risk patients.
ADRs & Complications: Hot flashes, thromboembolic events (DVT/PE), endometrial hyperplasia, and uterine cancer risk.

Leuprolide

GnRH Agonist Subclass
Key Info: Pituitary GnRH receptor agonist administered as a depot injection for hormone-sensitive cancers.
MOA: Desensitizes pituitary GnRH receptors after initial stimulation, shutting down LH/FSH and gonadal sex hormones.
Uses: Advanced prostate cancer and hormone-responsive breast/endometrial cancers.
ADRs & Complications: Tumor flare phenomenon (initial testosterone surge), hot flashes, bone loss, and erectile dysfunction.
